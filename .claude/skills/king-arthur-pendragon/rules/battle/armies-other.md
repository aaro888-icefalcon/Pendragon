### Zazamanc Army Notes

**Faris (entries 02 and 15):** These are the troops of the noble elite, riding on Arab chargers or Barb war coursers. They are the Zazamanc equivalent of knights. Both have Flee Fighting capability.

**Camel Riders (entry 12):** Camels are 1-humped dromedaries. They are so much taller than horses that camel riders get +5 against horsemen, and horsemen have −5 against them. This modifier applies in all melee rounds.

**Fanatics (entry 09):** These warriors attack the horse first — "the second man always attacks horse" means any knight with followers has one fanatic engage the horse rather than the rider. The "second man" in ×1d3 fanatics always targets the horse specifically.

**Muttaharrik Tulaiha (entry 19):** Elite Zazamanc cavalry with exceptional Flee value (20). They are the finest light cavalry force; their Flee Fighting and high Flee value make them very difficult to catch. Use Pursue maneuver only if Army Intensity is very low.

**Zumbooruk (entry 20):** Camel-mounted arbalest troops. The name comes from the wasp-like sound of the bolts in flight. If attacked in melee, they immediately Sprint away (Camel Sprint = 17). They only fight at range.

**Drum Band:** Whenever the Drum Band is present and playing on the field, the Zazamanc army gets −5 to Core Intensity modifier. If the band is silenced (Win against them in melee), the bonus is lost. Full Glory (25) only awarded if the knight laughs loudly every time recounting the incident.

**Gigantic Hero:** So large that when mounted he gets an Attack from Above +5 bonus even against other mounted men. When afoot, no mounted man has a bonus against him.

**Ransoms:** Zazamanc cultures have a sense of nobility similar to Western knighthood. Ransom rules apply normally to units marked £.

### Spanish Army Notes

**Spanish Pony:** Same stats as a War Pony (5d6), but adroit in mountainous rocky Spanish terrain — no Horsemanship penalty in rocky ground (waiving the standard −5 for rocky terrain).

**Improved Armor:** The Iberian peninsula is famed for its metallurgy. Iberian armor grants +1 AP compared to equivalent armor elsewhere. This is already factored into the stat blocks (Chain = 11 rather than 10, etc.).

**Spanish Commoners:** Unlike many other cultures, Spanish commoners generally stand and fight rather than sprinting away.

**Almogavers (entry 13):** Their Hate (Horsemen) 20 is always active. They Determined (D) against horsemen specifically — they will not retreat from cavalry. Attack horse legs first.

**Terror (Horsemen) — entry 04:** When this group sees knights charging, they make a Fear roll. On a failure, they Sprint (they have the S marker). The GM may roll 1d20 for them; on 10 or less, they sprint immediately on seeing a lance charge.

### Hun Army Notes

**All Flee Fighting:** Every single Hun unit has the Flee Fighting capability. They are never Engaged in the traditional sense — they always maintain distance and shoot. To force melee, players must use Follow, Pursue, or Charge maneuvers to corner them.

**Compound Bow (Cultural Specialty):** All Huns use Compound Bow as their primary weapon. Range modifier 8 means they can fire from further away than standard bows.

**Tactics against Huns:** The Flee Fighting opposition makes standard melee maneuvers ineffective. Best approach: Use Stand Fast or Remain Disengaged while others flank (requiring scripted Opportunity). Their Army Intensity drops quickly if they can't get to missile range — put them in close terrain.

**Flee values:** Huns' high Flee values (18–20) make them very hard to catch. Players pursuing with Pursue maneuver will face the Rearguard roll (18–20 on d20) which represents the Huns who turn to fight while the rest flee.
