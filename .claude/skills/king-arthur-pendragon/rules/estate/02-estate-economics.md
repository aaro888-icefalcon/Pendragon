# Estate Economics

Complete income sources, expense categories, budget models, the Winter Phase economic cycle, shortage consequences, and desperation income mechanics.

**Use when:** Running Winter Phase Step 4 (economic check), adjudicating income shortfalls, applying impost/squeeze, or calculating household costs.

---

## Income Sources

### Assized Rent
Fixed annual payments from tenants. The most stable and predictable income. Forms the base for calculating household staff costs (see Expenses below).

### Profits and Perquisites of Court
Income from manor court: fines, fees, tolls on transactions, and other judicial revenues. Variable but reliable. Included in the stated annual estate income figure.

### Production Income
Income from direct exploitation of the demesne: crops, livestock, woodland products, fishing, mills, etc. Also included in the stated annual income figure.

The total of all three = the estate's **Annual Income** (the £ figure used throughout these rules).

---

## Expense Categories

All expenses are calculated as proportions of annual income. The estate's stated income is the **gross**; expenses consume it before any discretionary spending.

| Category | Proportion of Annual Income |
|----------|-----------------------------|
| Army (military upkeep) | 55% |
| Court (administration, hospitality) | 25% |
| Family maintenance | 10% |
| Discretionary | 10% |

### Household Staff Sub-breakdown (within Army/Court figures)

| Staff Type | Proportion of Assized Rent |
|------------|---------------------------|
| Household Staff | 50% |
| Manorial Staff | 25% |
| Raw Materials | 25% |

## Winter Phase Step 4: Economic Procedure

Perform these steps in order each Winter Phase:

1. **Calculate total income** for the year (estate income + any investments, minus any losses from damage).
2. **Calculate total mandatory expenses** (Army + Court + Family = 90% of income).
3. **Subtract expenses from income.** Remainder is discretionary (normally 10%).
4. **Apply any shortages:** if expenses exceed income, determine deficit in Lots (see below).
5. **Apply Damage or Recovery** if applicable (see `04-estate-events.md`).
6. **Apply Discretionary Spending** if desired (see below).

---

## Lots System

**1 Lot = £1 of annual income.**

Lots measure both damage and shortfall. A manor with £10 income has 10 Lots maximum.

---

## Shortage Results

If total income (after all sources) is less than mandatory expenses, the estate is in deficit. Calculate the deficit in Lots (£1 = 1 Lot).

| Deficit (Lots Short) | Consequence |
|---------------------|-------------|
| 1 | Family members go without small comforts; no mechanical effect but noted |
| 2 | Courtiers are unhappy; -1 to all Hospitality-related rolls this year |
| 3 | Army begins to grumble; foot soldiers' loyalty at risk (GM discretion) |
| 4 | Family maintenance inadequate; wife's Loyalty passion check (roll vs. passion; failure = -1 to passion) |
| 5 | Servants underpaid; 1d3 servants may depart (GM chooses which) |
| 6 | Army partially unpaid; knights may refuse dangerous service this year |
| 7 | Family goes hungry; all family members must make a Survival check (standard rules) |
| 8 | Estate in serious distress; liege lord may hear of it; potential demand for explanations |
| 9+ | Crisis: estate cannot function; family survival at risk; GM may call for family event rolls |

**Impoverished threshold:** If annual income falls to £1 or less, the estate is effectively ruined. The knight loses noble status if this persists more than one year without recovery.

## Discretionary Spending

The 10% discretionary income (normally) may be spent on:
- Feasts and hospitality (see Outrageous Consumption)
- Gifts to retainers
- Improvements (partial payment toward construction)
- Any personal expenditure

### Outrageous Consumption

Spending discretionary funds on spectacular display earns Glory.

| Amount Spent | Glory Awarded |
|-------------|---------------|
| £1–£4 | 1 Glory |
| £5–£9 | 2 Glory |
| £10–£19 | 4 Glory |
| £20–£29 | 6 Glory |
| £30–£49 | 8 Glory |
| £50+ | 10 Glory |

This represents one-time display spending, not investments. Cannot be applied to investment improvements.

## Desperation Income

When the estate cannot meet expenses, a knight may pursue extraordinary measures. Each carries risks.

### Squeeze / Impost

Extracting additional income from tenants beyond their normal rents. Requires a Trait check (Cruel vs. Merciful, or Just vs. Arbitrary — GM chooses appropriate trait based on circumstances).

| Squeeze Level | Extra Income | Lots Damaged | Trait Check Required |
|---------------|-------------|--------------|---------------------|
| 10% extra | +10% annual income | 0 | None |
| 25% extra | +25% annual income | 1 | Cruel check |
| 50% extra | +50% annual income | 2 | Cruel check |
| 100% extra | +100% annual income | 4 | Cruel check; also Arbitrary check |

- **Lots Damaged** means the estate's productive capacity is reduced by that many Lots this year (harsh extraction harms future yields).
- **Trait check failure** means the tenants resist: income is not gained, and 1d3 Lots of additional damage occurs from flight or destruction.
- **Fumble on Trait check:** Revolt. Tenants actively resist; military action may be required; liege lord notices.

### Borrowing

- A knight may borrow money from moneylenders, merchants, or other nobles.
- Interest rate: **50% per year** (standard medieval moneylender rate).
- Lender requires **collateral worth double the loan** (land, livestock, moveable goods).
- Failure to repay: lender claims the collateral. If collateral is land, knight loses that portion of estate.
- Borrowing from a fellow knight: negotiate individually (typically 0–25% interest if friendly).

### Raiding, Pillaging, Plundering Own Tenants

A knight who raids his own estate to raise quick cash:

| Action | Cash Gained | Permanent Estate Damage |
|--------|------------|------------------------|
| Raid own estate | 1d6 × £1 | 1 Lot (Temporary) |
| Pillage own estate | 2d6 × £1 | 2 Lots (1T + 1P) |
| Plunder own estate | 3d6 × £1 | 4 Lots (1T + 3P) |

**T = Temporary damage** (recoverable); **P = Permanent damage** (reduces estate income long-term).

Additionally: liege lord will likely hear of it; family loyalty checks may be required; Honor loss is possible at GM discretion.

## Constabulary Herd

A knight's estate maintains working animals. Standard constabulary herd at £10 manor:

| Animal | Quantity | Purpose |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Oxen | 4–8 | Plowing, hauling |
| Cart horses | 1–2 | Transport |
| Dairy cattle | 2–4 | Food production |
| Pigs | 4–12 | Food |
| Sheep | 10–30 | Wool and food |
| Poultry | As available | Eggs and food |

Larger estates scale proportionally. The Constable is responsible for this herd.
