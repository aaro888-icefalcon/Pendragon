# Estate Basics

Defines what an estate is, how it is structured territorially, and the obligations (military service, homage, fealty) attached to holding land.

**Use when:** Determining what a knight holds, who they owe service to, how loyalty passions are generated, or adjudicating questions of feudal obligation.

---

## Territorial Hierarchy

| Unit | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Honour | All lands held by a great lord; composed of multiple manors/estates |
| County | Royal administrative unit; 3–5 Hundreds |
| Hundred | Sub-county division; roughly 100 hides of land; administrative/judicial unit |
| Vill | Smallest administrative unit; one or more manors |
| Manor | Basic economic/social unit; a knight's primary holding |
| Forest | Unfarmed land (woodland, moor, waste); under separate lordship rules |

A knight normally holds one or more **manors**. A collection of manors under one lord is an **estate**. A great lord's collection of estates is an **honour**.

---

## Estate Size Categories

| Category | Annual Income | Notes |
|----------|--------------|-------|
| Small Manor | £10 | Minimum viable knight's holding |
| Medium Estate | £20–£49 | Multiple manors or one improved manor |
| Large Estate | £50+ | Multiple manors with vassal knights |
| Barony | £120+ | Typically includes vassal knights owing service |

The standard example of a large estate: **£120 income** with two £10 vassal knights (each contributing 1 knight + 3 foot soldiers to the lord's army).

---

## Estate and Glory

A knight gains Glory equal to the annual income (in £) of his estate. This Glory is awarded once per year during the Winter Phase.

---

## Responsibilities of an Estate Holder

1. Provide military service to liege lord (servitium debitum — see below)
2. Maintain household and dependents
3. Administer justice on the manor
4. Pay dues and renders to liege lord
5. Maintain the estate against deterioration

Failure to meet these responsibilities may result in loss of the estate.

---

## Military Service (Servitium Debitum)

**Base formula:** 1 knight + 3 foot soldiers per **£10** of annual income.

| Estate Income | Knights Owed | Foot Soldiers Owed |
|--------------|-------------|-------------------|
| £10 | 1 | 3 |
| £20 | 2 | 6 |
| £50 | 5 | 15 |
| £120 | 12 | 36 |

- The knight himself counts as one of the knights owed.
- Vassal knights on the estate provide their own service quotas upward.
- A £120 estate with two £10 vassal knights: the lord owes 12 knights + 36 foot; the two vassals each contribute 1 knight + 3 foot, leaving 10 knights + 30 foot the lord must provide from his own resources.

**Duration:** 40 days per year standard. Additional service may be negotiated or demanded.

---

## Family Survival Rolls

Each Winter Phase, if the estate cannot meet its financial obligations, family survival becomes an issue. See `02-estate-economics.md` (Shortage Results) for the full procedure and consequences.

## Obtaining an Estate

An estate may be obtained by:
1. **Inheritance** — automatic upon parent's death if heir survives
2. **Grant from liege** — lord grants land in exchange for homage and service
3. **Marriage** — through wife's dowry (land component)
4. **Conquest** — capturing land and having the king confirm the grant
5. **Purchase** — rare; land is not normally sold in the feudal system

The GM determines availability. The king or great lord must confirm all grants.

## Loyalty Passions: Homage and Fealty

### Homage vs. Fealty

| Term | What It Is | Mechanic |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Homage** | The formal act of becoming a vassal; an ongoing relationship | Generates a Loyalty (Lord) passion |
| **Fealty** | The oath sworn during homage; promises specific obligations | Part of the homage ceremony |

These are distinct: a knight may owe fealty (oath) to multiple lords for different holdings, but homage creates the stronger personal bond.

### Generating Homage (Loyalty Passion)

Base: **3d6 + 3**

Apply modifiers:

| Condition | Modifier |
|-----------|---------|
| Lord is renowned for generosity | +3 |
| Lord has high Glory (5,000+) | +2 |
| Knight was raised in lord's household | +3 |
| Lord and knight share a Religious trait passion | +2 |
| Lord is cruel or unjust (known) | -3 |
| Lord has low Glory (under 1,000) | -1 |
| Knight was given land reluctantly / under duress | -3 |
| Knightly relationship is purely transactional | -2 |

Minimum result: 6. Maximum before modifiers: 21 (3d6+3 max 18+3).

### Using Loyalty (Lord) in Play

- Roll Loyalty (Lord) to determine whether a vassal knight follows an order that conflicts with his personal code.
- A **critical success** on Loyalty means the knight obeys without question and gains 1 point of the passion.
- A **success** means the knight obeys.
- A **failure** means the knight hesitates; GM may allow a second roll or impose a delay.
- A **fumble** means the knight refuses; relationship is strained; liege may demand explanation.

## Honour and Estate Distinction

- **Honour** (moral sense): A knight's personal reputation for keeping his word, fulfilling obligations, and behaving with integrity. Tracked as a score, typically 0–20+.
- **Honour** (territorial sense): The collection of lands held by a great lord. Context distinguishes usage.

Actions that reduce personal Honor (the score): breaking oaths, releasing retainers without cause, shortchanging wife's maintenance, dismissing a handmaiden without valid reason.

---

## Obtaining an Estate: Sequence

1. Knight is offered or petitions for a holding.
2. Parties negotiate terms (income, military obligations, any special renders).
3. Knight performs **homage**: kneels, places hands between lord's hands, swears fealty.
4. Lord formally invests knight with the estate (symbolic transfer of sod, twig, or glove).
5. Generate **Loyalty (Lord)** passion using 3d6+3 + modifiers (above).
6. Record annual income, military service obligations, and any special dues.
