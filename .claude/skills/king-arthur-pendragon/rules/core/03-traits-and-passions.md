# Traits and Passions

All 13 trait pairs, trait mechanics, passion invocation, famous traits/passions, religious bonuses, chivalry bonus, and dishonorable acts.

**Use when:** a trait or passion roll is called for, checking for inspiration, adjudicating honor loss, or applying religious/chivalric bonuses.

---

## The 13 Trait Pairs

Each pair sums to 20. Each side is tracked separately. Values run 1–19 normally; a value ≥ 16 is **Famous**.

| Trait | Opposite | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Chaste** | Lustful | Resists sexual temptation vs. pursues carnal pleasure |
| **Energetic** | Lazy | Active and vigorous vs. prefers ease and rest |
| **Forgiving** | Vengeful | Pardons wrongs vs. seeks retribution |
| **Generous** | Selfish | Gives freely vs. hoards for self |
| **Honest** | Deceitful | Tells truth vs. lies and manipulates |
| **Modest** | Proud | Humble, self-effacing vs. vain, boastful |
| **Just** | Arbitrary | Follows rules/fairness vs. acts on whim |
| **Merciful** | Cruel | Shows mercy vs. inflicts suffering |
| **Pious** | Worldly | Devoted to God vs. devoted to earthly matters |
| **Prudent** | Reckless | Thinks before acting vs. acts impulsively |
| **Temperate** | Indulgent | Exercises restraint vs. overindulges |
| **Trusting** | Suspicious | Believes others vs. doubts motives |
| **Valorous** | Cowardly | Faces danger courageously vs. avoids it |

## Trait Roll Mechanics

Called by GM when a situation triggers a trait. Roll d20 against the relevant trait value.

### Table 4-1: Trait Roll Results

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Critical success** | Character acts strongly on that trait AND gains an experience check for it |
| **Success** | Character acts on that trait; player chooses the specific details within the trait's spirit |
| **Failure** | Character acts on the opposite trait instead |
| **Fumble** | Opposite trait is checked (for experience); character acts strongly on the opposite trait |

### Directed Traits

- **Player-invoked:** character may voluntarily invoke a directed trait for +2d6 bonus to rolls
- **GM-imposed:** GM may apply up to +5 bonus from a directed trait when circumstances are extreme

### Famous Traits (Value ≥ 16)

- NPC reactions are influenced by the character's famous trait
- Each Winter Phase: gain Glory equal to the trait value (e.g., Valorous 17 = 17 Glory/year)
- Famous trait behavior cannot easily be avoided — the trait compels action

---

## Traits Over 19

When a trait reaches 20+:
- Opposite trait = 0 (cannot roll it; automatic failure)
- Bonus to die roll = value − 20 (add to d20 before comparing)
- Any result of 20+ = critical success
- No fumble possible

## Passions

Passions represent deep emotional commitments. Values range 1–20+.

### Standard Starting Passions

| Passion | Starting Value |
|---|---|
| Loyalty (lord) | 15 |
| Love (family) | 15 |
| Hospitality | 15 |
| Honor | 15 |
| Hate (Saxons) | 3d6 |
| Loyalty (group) | 3d6 (situational) |
| Love (spouse) | 3d6 (upon marriage) |
| Loyalty (Pendragon) | 2d6+6 (for Uther's direct vassals) |

### Famous Passions (Value ≥ 16)

Same as famous traits: each Winter Phase, gain Glory equal to the passion value.

## Passion Invocation (Inspiration)

A player may invoke a passion before a roll to gain a bonus. GM may also call for a passion roll.

### Table 4-2: Inspiration Results

| Passion Roll Result | Effect | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical success** | +20 to all trait/skill rolls OR double current value (whichever is higher) | One day maximum |
| **Success** | +10 to all relevant rolls | One day maximum |
| **Failure** | Disheartened: −5 to all rolls | Rest of scene |
| **Fumble** | Melancholic (mood disorder) or Maddened (removed from play until healed) | GM discretion |

**Maddened:** character is out of play until another character successfully Chirurgeries them (or GM-determined recovery). Lancelot's famous madness is a literary precedent.

---

## Fear Passions

- **Never** grant Glory when invoked
- **Never** grant inspiration bonuses
- Can only cause Disheartened/Melancholic/Maddened results on failure/fumble

## Honor

Honor is a core passion. Reducing Honor below certain thresholds has consequences:

- **Honor reduced to 4:** lord must outlaw or degrade the knight
- **Honor reduced to 0:** character removed from play

### Table 4-3: Dishonorable Acts

| Act | Honor Lost |
|---|---|
| Attacking an unarmed knight | −1 |
| Cowardice | −1 |
| Desertion | −1 |
| Plundering a holy place | −1 |
| Killing an unarmed holy person | −2 |
| Killing, kidnapping, or raping a noblewoman | −2 |
| Lending money at profit (usury) | −2 |
| Performing physical labor | −2 |
| Breaking an oath | −3 |
| Flagrant cowardice | −3 |
| Treachery against family | −5 |
| Treason against lord | −5 |
| Killing a kinsman | −6 |
| Learning magic spells | −8 |

## Religious Bonuses

### Requirements

A knight qualifies for religious bonuses when the relevant virtues all reach **16 or higher**.

### Roman Christian Virtues (all five at 16+)

**Virtues:** Chaste 16+, Forgiving 16+, Merciful 16+, Modest 16+, Temperate 16+

**Bonus:** +6 Total Hit Points

### British Christian Virtues (all five at 16+)

**Virtues:** Energetic 16+, Forgiving 16+, Generous 16+, Modest 16+, Temperate 16+

**Bonus:** +3 Total Hit Points, +2 Damage dice

### Pagan Virtues (all five at 16+)

**Virtues:** Generous 16+, Energetic 16+, Proud 16+, Worldly 16+, Reckless 16+

**Bonus:** +2 Healing Rate

### All Religious Knights

Any knight meeting their religion's virtue requirements gains:
- **+100 Glory per year** (Winter Phase)

## Chivalric Bonus (Future — Arthur's Reign)

Six chivalric traits: Generous, Energetic, Modest, Just, Merciful, Valorous.

**Requirement:** sum of all six traits ≥ 80, plus oath sworn.

**Bonus:**
- Armor of Honor: 3 points of armor reduction (adds to worn armor)
- +100 Glory per year (Winter Phase)

**Oath text:** "To protect the widow, the orphan, the poor; not to slay a vanquished and defenseless adversary; not to take part in a false judgment or treason; to never give evil counsel to a lady; to help, if possible, a fellow being in distress."

---

## Evil Virtues (NPC Only)

Anti-Christian virtues: Vengeful, Selfish, Deceitful, Cruel, Suspicious.

If total of these five traits reaches 80+, the NPC is quantifiably evil and may be subject to diabolic effects. Player characters meeting this threshold are removed from play.
