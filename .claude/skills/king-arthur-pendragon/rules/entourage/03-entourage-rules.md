# Entourage Rules: Procedures and Winter Phase

All procedural rules for acquiring, managing, and resolving entourage members, including the complete entourage-updated Winter Phase sequence.

**Use when:** Running the Winter Phase with followers present; acquiring a squire or wife; resolving departure events; adjudicating follower loyalty, honor consequences, and glory.

---

## Acquiring a Squire

### Standard Acquisition

A knight may acquire a squire at any time if one is available (GM adjudicates availability based on setting and connections).

1. Negotiate with the squire's family (or prospective squire, if older).
2. The squire enters service; his starting age is **14**.
3. Generate his stats: Squire Skill = 15 + background modifier; Knightly Skills = (Age − 11) + modifier.
4. Set Loyalty (Lord) to **15**.
5. Pay **£1/year** upkeep going forward.

### Temporary Squire Acquisition

If no appropriate candidate is available or the knight needs an immediate squire:
- Engage a temporary squire with fixed stats (see `02-entourage-members.md`).
- Loyalty (Lord) = 3d6 (variable; may be low).
- No background modifier; Squire Skill fixed at 15; Knightly Skills = 1d6+2.

### Multiple Squires

Additional squires beyond the first are **not automatic** under Standard of Living. Each additional squire costs £1/year. The knight must justify the additional squires (multiple estates, GM permission).

---

## Squire Career Progression

Each year during the Winter Phase:

1. **Age the squire** +1 year (Step 3 of Winter Phase).
2. **Improve skills** per follower improvement rules (Step 7):
   - Skills ≤ 15: +1 automatic.
   - Skills 16–20: roll 1d6; on 6, +1.
   - Skills 21+: roll 1d20; on 20, +1.
3. **Check for departure** if squire is over 21 (see below).

### Squire Departure (Over Age 21)

Each year of service past age 21:
- Roll 1d20.
- If roll ≤ years of service past 21, the squire departs.
- Example: 3 years past 21 → departs on 01, 02, or 03.

**Early Dismissal:** Dismissing a squire before knighthood (without valid cause) costs **3 Honor**.

**Valid cause for dismissal:** Theft, cowardice witnessed by others, betrayal of the lord, or other demonstrable offense. In these cases, no Honor is lost.

## Acquiring a Wife

1. Player declares intent to the GM at start of Winter Phase Step 6a (Marriage).
2. GM rules whether the marriage is possible given current story circumstances.
3. Player rolls on the Random Wife Table (see `02-entourage-members.md`).
4. Generate wife's starting stats.
5. Determine dowry (cash and/or land) and dower (portion of estate guaranteed to her).
6. Award Glory for the marriage from the match quality table.
7. Record Love (Family) passion (starting value set by GM; typically 15 for a new match).
8. Record the wife's upkeep as part of the Family (10%) expense budget.

### Previous Marriage (Wife Older than 21)

If the wife-to-be is over 21, roll on the Previous Marriage table (see `02-entourage-members.md`). Apply Glory gained and note any living children or ex-family connections.

## Wife Maintenance and Honor

The knight is obligated to maintain his wife at an appropriate standard.

| Situation | Consequence |
|-----------|------------|
| Full maintenance paid | No issues |
| Shortchanged year 1 | Roll Selfish check; noted if passes |
| Shortchanged year 2+ | Roll Selfish check AND Cruel check each year |
| Any shortchanging | -1 Honor per year |
| Wife reduced to £1 or less income | Insult to her family; Honor loss + potential family reaction |

## Acquiring a Handmaiden (For a Lady)

1. The lady selects a girl of noble birth, typically age 14.
2. The girl enters service; starting skills per `02-entourage-members.md`.
3. Loyalty (Lady) set to **15**.
4. Cost: **£1/year** per handmaiden.
5. Only the longest-serving handmaiden has a guaranteed position.
6. Additional handmaidens may be dismissed if economics require (see dismissal rules).

## Follower Loyalty Checks

When a follower's loyalty is tested (dangerous orders, hardship, divided allegiance):

| Result | Outcome |
|--------|---------|
| Critical Success | Follower obeys without question; passion increases by 1 |
| Success | Follower obeys |
| Failure | Follower hesitates; may refuse the specific action; no permanent penalty |
| Fumble | Follower refuses; relationship strained; may depart |

**Triggering a loyalty check:** Ordering a follower to act against their nature, cross a personal code, risk life without glory, or perform something the knight himself would not do.

## Glory from Retinue: Detailed Calculation

1. Sum all upkeep payments made to followers during the year (servants, soldiers, professionals).
2. Each £1 paid = 1 Glory (up to a maximum of 100 Glory per year from this source).
3. Do NOT include: the knight's own expenses; wife's maintenance (counted under Family); squire's £1 (counts toward the total).
4. Record in Step 8 (Compute Glory) of Winter Phase.

**Practical cap:** At £100+ in retinue spending, the knight has reached the maximum 100 Glory from this source. Additional spending beyond £100 gains no extra Glory from this rule (though it may generate other benefits).

## Complete Winter Phase (Entourage-Updated)

The full Winter Phase sequence with all entourage modifications incorporated:

### Step 1: Perform Solo

Unchanged from core rules.

### Step 2: Experience

Apply experience rolls as per core rules. Include any skills used by the knight during the year.

### Step 3: Aging

- Increase the knight's age by 1.
- **Increase the age of:**
  - Wife (by 1)
  - Squire(s) (by 1 each)
  - Handmaiden(s) (by 1 each)
  - Any other followers who have an age stat

### Step 4: Check Economic Circumstances

Follow the estate economic procedure (see `02-estate-economics.md`):
1. Calculate income.
2. Calculate expenses (Army 55%, Court 25%, Family 10%, Discretionary 10%).
3. Determine surplus or deficit in Lots.
4. Apply damage and recovery if applicable.
5. Resolve Shortage consequences if in deficit.

### Step 5: Stable Rolls

Unchanged from core rules (horse health, etc.).

### Step 6: Family Rolls

#### 6a. Marriage

- Player declares intent.
- GM rules on possibility.
- Roll on appropriate table (Wife or Husband depending on PC gender).
- Record dowry, dower, starting passions, and Glory.

#### 6b. Children

Unchanged from core rules (pregnancy, birth, survival rolls).

#### 6c. Family Events

Unchanged from core rules.

#### 6d. Servant Departures

**Squire (if over 21):**
- Roll 1d20.
- If roll ≤ years of service past age 21, the squire departs.
- Determine if he seeks knighthood elsewhere or simply leaves service.

**Handmaiden (if over 21):**
- Roll 1d20.
- 01–19: She remains.
- 20: She departs. GM determines fate (marriage, nunnery, another household, death, disappearance).

### Step 7: Training and Practice

Apply follower improvement to all followers:

| Skill Range | Rule |
|------------|------|
| ≤ 15 | +1 automatically |
| 16–20 | Roll 1d6; on 6, +1 |
| 21+ | Roll 1d20; on 20, +1 |

Apply to each follower individually. Only improve skills actually used during the year, or skills the knight arranged training for.

### Step 8: Compute Glory

Sum all Glory sources:
- Combat and adventure Glory (from play)
- Estate Glory (£1 per £ of annual income)
- Retinue Glory (£1 per £ spent on followers, max 100)
- Marriage Glory (if married this year)
- Construction/improvement Glory (if built enhancements)
- Outrageous Consumption Glory (if spent on display)

### Step 9: Add Glory Bonus

Add any Glory bonus from the Glory table based on total accumulated Glory (per core rules).

## Mesnie Hiring and Upkeep

### Hiring Procedure

1. Knight declares what type of soldier is needed.
2. GM rules on availability (peacetime vs. wartime; location; budget).
3. Knight agrees to upkeep rate.
4. Soldier joins mesnie at stats listed in `02-entourage-members.md`.

### Paying the Mesnie

- Upkeep is paid annually from the Army expense budget (55% of income).
- If the army budget is insufficient, soldiers may: depart (loyal ones last longer), demand pay in kind (equipment, food), or become unreliable.
- Soldiers who go unpaid for one full year without valid reason will typically depart unless bound by personal loyalty (GM's call).

### Mesnie Glory Awards

| Soldier Type | Annual Glory Awarded to Knight |
|-------------|-------------------------------|
| Knight | 45 |
| Cavalryman | 26 |
| Hobilar | 12 |
| Guardsman | 17 |
| Foot Soldier (any type) | 12 |
| Poor Foot Soldier | 10 |
| Armored Foot Soldier | 16 |
| Crossbowman | 15 |

These Glory amounts are for **maintaining** the soldier in the mesnie. The actual Glory is awarded through retinue spending (£1 per £ of upkeep), not separately — these values help approximate the retinue Glory from military expenditure.

## Honor and Entourage

Honor is a separate score from Glory. Actions related to entourage management that affect Honor:

| Action | Honor Effect |
|--------|-------------|
| Knight a squire at appropriate time | +1 Honor |
| Dismiss squire early without cause | -3 Honor |
| Maintain wife appropriately | No change |
| Shortchange wife | -1 Honor/year |
| Dismiss handmaiden without cause | -1 Honor (to the lady) |
| Dismiss loyal officer of 3+ years without cause | -1 Honor |
| Fulfill all servitium debitum obligations | No change (expected) |
| Fail to provide military service owed | -1 to -3 Honor (depending on severity) |

---

## Follower Capture and Ransom

If a squire or wife is captured in war or raid:

- **Squire:** Must be ransomed. Ransom = typically 1–3 times annual upkeep (£1–£3). Knight loses 1 Honor if he fails to make a reasonable effort to ransom his squire.
- **Wife:** Must be ransomed or rescued. Failure to attempt rescue or ransom is a serious Honor violation (GM sets penalty; typically -3 to -5 Honor minimum).

Ransoms paid count as discretionary expenditure for that year.

## Nomenclature of Noblewomen (Reference)

These terms are used in the rules for the Lady's entourage:

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Handmaiden | Generic term for any noble woman working at court for a higher-ranking noble; same as Woman-in-waiting |
| Maiden | Young, unmarried noble woman (under 21) |
| Damsel | Older unmarried noble woman (21+); also "old maid" |
| Lady | Title for married daughters of knights and wives of knights |
| Maiden-in-waiting | A maiden whose assignment is to work in a noble household; implies loyalty bond |
| Damsel-in-waiting | Same role, but the woman is over 21 |
| Lady-in-waiting | Married woman serving in this role |
