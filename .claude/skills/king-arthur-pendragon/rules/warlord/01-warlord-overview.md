# Warlord Overview: Baronial Play

Covers the layer of play above knight-level — what barons and bannerets are, how baronial mechanics sit atop knight-level Pendragon, and the noble hierarchy of Logres.

**Use when:** Establishing a character's baronial status, interpreting hierarchy, determining who outranks whom, or introducing a player to warlord-level play.

---

## Campaign Scope

Book of the Warlord covers three campaign periods:
- **Uther Period** (485–495)
- **Anarchy Period** (495–509)
- **Boy King Period** (509–519, ending at Battle of Badon Hill)

---

## Player Options for Baronial Play

### Everyone's Equal
All Player-knights are barons of roughly equal rank. Most useful for single-session or short campaigns.

### Everyone's a Lord
All Player-knights are barons; one is the senior baron who coordinates the others. One player may be the senior baron's player.

### Lord and Retainers
One Player-knight is the baron; the others are household knights sworn to him. The baron player manages the estate; other players adventure as retainers.

### Nobleman at Court
All Player-knights are courtiers of a great baron or king. They do not hold estates; they serve at court and are dispatched on missions.

|------|-------------|-------|
| High King | 10,000 | Uther and Arthur only |
| Emperor | 7,500 | Roman holdovers; essentially extinct |

| Duke (Dux) | 4,000 | Highest Logres rank in Uther Period; disappears then resurfaces |
| Count (Earl) | 3,000 | Second rank of baron in Early Phase |
| Great Abbot / Archbishop | 3,000 | Equal to count for game purposes |
| Baron (Major) | 2,000 | The primary "warlord" rank |
| Knight Banneret | 1,500 | Special: estate holder by royal grant for valor; NOT synonym for estate holder |
| Vassal Knight | 1,000 | Holds a manor from a baron; the typical player starting rank |
| Bachelor Knight | 750 | Landless knight; household knight |
| Miles / Eques | 500 | Mounted warrior swearing fealty (not homage); no squire |
| Esquire | 250 | Young nobleman in training |
| Esquire-at-Arms | 200 | Armed esquire serving as fighter |

**Rules change (from KAP 5.1):** Knight Banneret is no longer a synonym for estate holder. It is a specific title granted by the king for valor in battle.

## Territorial Administration Hierarchy

From largest to smallest unit:

| Unit | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Kingdom | The realm of a king; composed of counties |
| County | Administered by a sheriff; has a county court (twice yearly) |
| Hundred | Sub-division for tax/judicial purposes; each has a hundred court |
| Honour | Baron's holding; caput major + outlier parcels |
| Estate | Small honour; may be a caput major of a larger honour |
| Manor | Basic land unit; smallest independent holding |
| Market Town | Grants Assized Rent bonus; see bonuses below |
| City | Grants larger Assized Rent bonus; see bonuses below |
| Port | Grants Assized Rent bonus for nearby sea access |

### Logres Has:
- ~540 hundreds
- Each hundred usually contains 10–20 settlements and 2–3 towns

## Key Distinctions: Homage vs. Fealty

| Term | Binding | Given by |
|------|---------|----------|
| Homage | Permanent loyalty oath | Knights to their lord |
| Fealty | Temporary loyalty vow | Miles; can be re-sworn to new lord |

A vassal with Homage cannot switch lords without dishonor unless the lord has breached his obligations. Diffidatio (formal defiance) is the only legal way to end homage, and only if the lord is in breach.

---

## The Baron's Role

A baron is a **tenant-in-chief**: he holds land directly from the king (in capite). Obligations run both upward (to the king) and downward (to his own vassals).

**Upward obligations:**
- Provide servitium debitum (knights and foot soldiers)
- Attend the King's Council when summoned
- Pay reliefs, aids, and other royal perqs
- Obtain royal permission for major decisions (marriage, castle-building, major feasts)

**Downward obligations:**
- Provide protection to vassals
- Maintain justice within his lands
- Maintain the army and household
- Ensure land is held and defended

## Barons of Logres at a Glance

### Great Barons (8 total, Uther Period)
These hold the largest honours in Logres:
1. Eldol, Duke of Glevum (CR ~£400+)
2. Gorlois, Duke of Cornwall (also Duke of Tintagel)
3. Edaris, Duke of the Marche
4. Lucius, Duke of the Saxon Shore
5. Corneus, Duke of Lindsey
6. Ulfius, Duke of the Vale (most powerful in Logres per the Anarchy)
7. Roderick, Count of Salisbury (CR £1,574; 15 eschilles)
8. Sulien, Count of Bedegraine

### Rich Barons (3 in Uther Period)
Holdings between Great and Minor:
- Cadwy, King of Summerland (CR ~£200–400)
- Bassianus, Baron of Noviomagus
- One additional (variable)

### Minor Barons (10 in Uther Period)
Holdings of roughly £100–200 Customary Revenue.

### Barons of the Robe (12 in Uther Period)
Clerical barons: abbots, bishops, archbishops. Hold by free alms tenure; provide prayers rather than knights. Equal to counts or barons by rank.

## Annual Glory from Land

Annual Glory = (1 point per £ of Customary Revenue) + Glory from Improvements (maximum 100 total per year).

## Relationship to Knight-Level Play

- Knight-level play uses the Book of the Manor/Estate economics (£6–10 manor, variable harvests).
- Warlord-level uses the **New Economics Model** (fixed income, see `02-baronies-and-honors.md`).
- **Key rules change:** In KAP 5.1, manors are valued at £6 ("money you never see"). In the Warlord system, a standard manor = £10 Assized Rent (fixed). A knight still derives £6 for his and his family's Standard of Living — the reconciliation is that the additional £4 covers army and court expenses now made explicit.
- Additional squires beyond the first now cost £1/year each and are NOT automatically included in Standard of Living upgrades.
