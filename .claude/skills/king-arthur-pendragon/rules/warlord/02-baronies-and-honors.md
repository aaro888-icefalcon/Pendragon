# Baronies and Honours

Covers the structure of baronial land tenure — honours, demesne vs. enfeoffed land, all forms of tenure, and the complete 10-step barony generation process with all tables.

**Use when:** Generating a new barony, determining what a baron holds, calculating Customary Revenue, or adjudicating inheritance and land structure questions.

---

## Core Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Parcel | Any distinct landholding with associated rights |
| Holding | All parcels under a lord's control, including enfeoffed lands |
| Caput major | Chief estate; where the lord lives; the manor improved over generations |
| Demesne | Land directly controlled by the lord (not enfeoffed to vassals) |
| Enfeoffed | Portions granted to the lord's own vassals |
| Estate | Small honour with a caput major; largest holding that cannot be broken up by heirs |
| Honour | Large holding = caput major estate + outlier parcels |
| Outlier | Parcel separate from caput major but part of the same holding |
| Manor | Basic land unit; smallest independent holding (typically £10 Assized Rent) |
| Fee Farm (Ferm) | Holding leased for annual payment; holder keeps surplus |
| Free Manor | Manor outside county's normal shrieval process |
| Allodial | Held free of any lord or obligation — only kings in Britain |
| Money Fief | Fief where vassal receives cash instead of land |

---

## Income Definitions

| Income Type | Equals |
|-------------|--------|
| Assized Rent | Income of the land, paid as Render (foodstuffs) |
| Other Income | Hundred/County Court profits + money fiefs + liberties |
| Customary Revenue | Assized Rents + Other Income |
| Free Income | Fee Farm profits + Improvements income + fee simple/allodial income – obligations – maintenance costs |
| Annual Income | Customary Revenue + Free Income |
| Production | Equal to Assized Rents (conversion of raw materials; counted separately) |
| Total Income | Annual Income + Production + Officers' Pay Bonus |
| Discretionary Fund | Demesne Free Income + 10% of Demesne Customary Revenue |

**Note:** Free Income may be negative due to obligations and maintenance costs.

**Note:** The value of a holding = its Customary Revenue. A "£300 honour" has £300 Customary Revenue.

## The 13 Forms of Tenure

A baron holds his land by one of these forms:

| Form | Knight Service? | Notes |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| By barony (per baronium) | Yes | Standard; baron is member of King's Council |
| By knight-service | Yes | Below baronial level |
| By scutage | Shield fee instead of service | Payment replaces knights |
| By sergeantry | Non-knight military or household service | Messengers, hunters, foresters |
| By free alms (frankalmoinage) | No — prayers instead | Church/abbey tenure |
| By free burgage | No | Town burgesses |
| By copyhold | No | Customary tenure of peasants |
| By fee farm | No | Annual rent payment |
| By fee simple | No | Rare free tenure |
| Time Immemorial | No | So ancient no one remembers the grant |
| By custos | Temporary | Custodian/warden during minority or vacancy |
| On lease | Temporary | Short-term arrangement |
| During pleasure | At lord's will | No security of tenure |
| For life | No further obligation | Ends on holder's death |
| In perpetuity | Yes, hereditary | Standard hereditary tenure |

### Step 2: Determine Honour Size

Roll 1d6:

| 1d6 | Honour Category | Customary Revenue |
|-----|-----------------|-------------------|
| 1 | Minor Knight's Estate | £50–100 |
| 2 | Knight's Honour | £100–150 |
| 3 | Small Baron | £150–200 |
| 4 | Baron | £200–300 |
| 5 | Rich Baron | £300–500 |
| 6 | Great Baron | £500+ |

Exact value within range: roll 1d6 × £10, add to range minimum.

### Step 3: Determine Caput Major

The caput major is the chief estate. Its Assized Rent = roughly 40–60% of total Demesne Customary Revenue.

**Caput Major Base Value:** Roll 1d6 × £10 for the base Assized Rent of the caput major itself (minimum £20 for a baron, £10 for a knight).

**Caput Major Components (determine each):**
- **Settlements:** Roll on Table 4.2 (see below)
- **Stronghold:** Roll on Table 4.3 (see below)
- **Furnishings:** Roll on Table 4.4 (see below)
- **Improvements:** Roll on Table 4.5 (see below)

### Step 4: Determine Outliers

Number of outliers = Honour Size / £10, minus the caput major value / £10. Each outlier is a separate parcel.

**Outlier Location (Table 4.6):**

| 1d6 | Location |
|-----|----------|
| 1–3 | Same hundred as caput major |
| 4–5 | Same county, different hundred |
| 6 | Different county |

**Outlier Type (Table 4.7):**

|-----|------|-------|

| 3 | Estate | £20–40 Assized Rent; has its own steward |
| 4 | Market Town | Manor + market; bonus income |
| 5 | Fee Farm | Annual payment; no servitium debitum |

### Step 5: Determine Demesne vs. Enfeoffed Split

**Rule:** The liege lord (king) considers ALL of the baron's land when calculating servitium debitum. The baron himself separates demesne (direct control) from enfeoffed (given to his own knights).

**Standard split:** 60–70% demesne, 30–40% enfeoffed.
- Exactly: the baron keeps enough demesne to support his household + household knights; the rest goes to vassal knights.
- Vassal knights receive approximately £20 per knight (a standard manor), providing 1 knight + 3 Foot Soldiers back to the baron.

### Step 7: Determine Furnishings

**Table 4.4: Caput Major Furnishings**

| 1d6 | Furnishings | Value £ |
|-----|-------------|---------|
| 1 | Bare (straw floors, trestle tables) | £5 |

### Step 8: Determine Improvements

Improvements are permanent additions to the caput major that generate ongoing income or benefits. Roll 1d6 for number of improvements (0 to 5, subtract 1 from roll, minimum 0).

**Table 4.5: Improvements**

| 1d6 | Improvement | Annual Income / Benefit |
|-----|-------------|------------------------|
| 1 | Mill | +£2 Assized Rent |
| 2 | Fish Pond | +£1 Assized Rent |
| 3 | Dovecote | +£1 Assized Rent |
| 4 | Improved Fields | +£2 Assized Rent |
| 5 | Orchard/Garden | +£1 Assized Rent |
| 6 | Special | Roll on Table 4.9 |

**Table 4.9: Special Improvements**

| 1d6 | Improvement | Benefit |
|-----|-------------|---------|
| 1 | Forge/Smithy | +£3; can produce arms and armor |
| 2 | Tannery | +£2; leather goods |
| 3 | Watermill (larger) | +£3 |
| 4 | Warren | +£1 + hunting rights |
| 5 | Chapel/Church | Glory bonus; +£1 from tithes |
| 6 | Tower/Fortification | +DV; see castle rules |

**Improvement Glory:** Each improvement = +1 Annual Glory (up to the 100-point maximum with land glory combined).

**Assized Rent after improvement:** Income from improvements built by the current holder = Free Income (not Assized Rent). Once the holder dies, the escheator reassesses, and the improvement income is folded into Assized Rent for the heir.

### Step 9: Determine Vassals

Count how many vassal knights the baron has. Standard: 1 vassal knight per £20 of enfeoffed Customary Revenue. Each vassal knight:
- Holds approximately £20 (one manor)
- Provides 1 knight + 2 field Foot Soldiers + 1 garrison Foot Soldier to the baron's army

**Vassal knights are rare:** Ratio of vassal knights rarely exceeds 20% of total knights. Most knights are household knights.

### Step 10: Final Calculations

Compute:
- **Total Honour Customary Revenue** = All Assized Rents + All Other Income
- **Demesne Customary Revenue** = Honour CR − Vassals' CR
- **Servitium Debitum** = 1 knight + 2 Foot + 1 garrison Foot per £10 Honour Customary Revenue
- **Demesne Army** = 1 knight + 2 Foot + 1 garrison Foot per £10 Demesne Customary Revenue
- **Annual Glory** = 1 per £ Customary Revenue + Improvements (max 100/year)
- **Discretionary Fund** = Demesne Free Income + 10% Demesne Customary Revenue

## Lots System (Shortage Tracking)

A "Lot" = 1/10 of a parcel's total income (Customary Revenue + Free Income).

Used to track shortfalls during bad years, sieges, or disaster:
- Each missing Lot represents 10% reduction in that parcel's income for the year.
- The baron may cover Lots from his Discretionary Fund or Treasure.
- If Lots are unpaid, army size and Standard of Living are reduced proportionally.

---

## Fee Farms

A Fee Farm (Ferm) is a parcel leased from the king for an annual payment.
- The holder pays the annual fee and keeps all surplus income.
- No servitium debitum is owed on Fee Farm income.
- Fee Farm profit = Fee Farm Customary Revenue − Annual Fee = Free Income.
- Failure to pay the fee: the king can reclaim the parcel.
- Gersuma: the fee paid to the king's sheriff for the right to determine who leases a Fee Farm.

---

## Growing Wealth / Accumulating Improvements

A baron may add improvements to his caput major using Discretionary Fund:
- Cost varies by improvement type (see Table 4.5 above).
- Construction time: 1 year minimum; larger works 2–5 years.
- Income from new improvements = Free Income until the holder dies (then becomes Assized Rent).
- Glory: +1 per improvement, applied annually (subject to 100/year cap).
