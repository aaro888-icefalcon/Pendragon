# Baronial Court

Covers the officers, household, court roles, and the complete Graft system for running a baron's court.

**Use when:** Staffing a baronial household, adjudicating officer positions and pay, running a solo court scenario, or resolving Graft attempts (any of the 5 Graft types).

---

## Court Composition

Court size scales with Demesne Customary Revenue. The court consists of:
- Lady Wife and family
- Officers (High and Standard)
- Court members (administrators, clergy, entertainers, ladies-in-waiting)
- Household knights and foot soldiers (covered under army budget)
- Servants (household staff and manorial staff)

**Court Expense:** 25% of Demesne Customary Revenue (includes Officers' Pay Bonus and Foot Soldier Officers' Pay Bonus).

**Household Staff Expense:** 50% of Demesne Assized Rents (personal servants, kitchen staff, grooms, etc.; ratio ~1 per £1 of Demesne Assized Rents).

**Manorial Staff Expense:** 25% of Demesne Assized Rents (bailiffs, blacksmiths, stablers, laborers at each manor; ratio ~1 per £2 of Demesne Assized Rents).

## Obtaining Office

A player-knight may seek an officer position through:

### Procedure
1. **Identify the position:** Is there a vacancy, or must the current holder be displaced?
2. **Make your case:** A character needs either a high relevant skill OR patronage from the baron (or another powerful figure).
3. **Roll Intrigue** to navigate court politics (vs. competing candidates' Intrigue if contested).
4. **Baron's Discretion:** The Gamemaster (as the baron) decides based on the character's skills, loyalty, and political standing.
5. **Confirmation:** A formal investiture; the character swears fealty to the office.

### Full-Time Officers
Officers who hold a full-time position cannot adventure freely. They may designate a deputy for day-to-day management (a deputy-officer, usually an esquire or commoner, with lower-level skills).

### Deputy-Officers
Esquires and even commoners can serve as deputies:
- Deputy-officer handles routine functions
- The titled officer is still responsible for the position's performance
- Deputies earn a small stipend (typically £0.5–£1/yr)
- The titled officer must oversee and correct the deputy's work periodically

## Graft System

Graft covers all forms of corruption available to officers and barons. There are 5 types, each with different risk/reward profiles.

### Graft Types

| Type | Description | Risk Level |
|------|-------------|------------|
| Perqs | Legitimate perquisites; taking standard "tips" and gifts | Very Low |
| Bribery | Accepting payment to use your position favorably | Low |
| Exploitation | Squeezing extra from those under your authority | Medium |
| Petty Larceny | Small-scale theft from accounts or stores | Medium-High |
| Grand Larceny | Large-scale embezzlement or outright theft | High |

**Bribery Income (Table 3.6):**
- Small bribe (routine favor): £0.5–£2
- Moderate bribe (significant favor): £2–£10
- Major bribe (life-altering favor): £10–£50
- Bribery income = Free Income; not reported

**Exploitation Income (Table 3.7):**
- Minor exploitation (extra fees from commoners): 5% of controlled income
- Moderate exploitation: 10% of controlled income
- Heavy exploitation: 20% of controlled income (risks peasant unrest)

**Petty Larceny (Table 3.8):**
- Taking small amounts from accounts, stores, or deliveries
- Typical: £1–£5 per month
- Discovery chance: 10% per month (modified by skill)

**Grand Larceny (Table 3.9):**
- Major embezzlement or outright theft of significant sums
- Typical: £20–£100 per incident
- Discovery chance: high; immediate investigation if discovered

### Graft: 10-Step Resolution Process

1. **Decide on Graft type** (Perqs, Bribery, Exploitation, Petty Larceny, Grand Larceny).
2. **Determine target amount** (how much the character wants to take).
3. **Check position:** Does the character hold a position that gives access to the funds/power being exploited?
4. **Determine base difficulty:** Higher amounts = higher difficulty.
5. **Apply skill roll:** Relevant skill is Stewardship (for financial graft) or Intrigue (for bribery/political graft).
6. **Roll for success:**
   - Critical: Full amount; no suspicion raised.
   - Success: Full amount; minor suspicion (−5 to next Loyalty check).
   - Failure: Half amount; suspicion raised.
   - Fumble: No amount; investigation triggered.
7. **Check for discovery:** Even on success, there is a periodic discovery check (frequency depends on Graft type).
8. **Discovery consequences:** If discovered, roll on Discovery Consequences table.
9. **Discovery Consequences (Table 3.11):**

| Roll | Consequence |
|------|-------------|
| 1–3 | Warning only; graft must stop |
| 4–6 | Fine equal to 2× amount taken |
| 7–9 | Loss of office; fine |
| 10–12 | Loss of office; public humiliation; Honor loss |
| 13–15 | Loss of office; imprisonment (1d6 months) |
| 16–18 | Loss of office; outlawry |
| 19–20 | Execution |

10. **Honor and Loyalty effects:** Each Graft incident (regardless of discovery) costs the character a Directed Trait check vs. Honest. Being caught reduces Loyalty (Lord) of household by −5.

## Solo Scenarios

The book includes two solo scenarios for baronial court play:

### Solo 1: Graft
The player-knight holds an officer position and navigates 10 game events related to their graft activities. Each event has a skill check and a consequence based on success/failure. The scenario covers one year and can result in significant income gain or catastrophic exposure.

### Solo 2: Court Intrigue
The player-knight seeks a favor from the baron, competes with rivals at court, and must navigate the political landscape. Skill checks: Intrigue, Courtesy, Diplomacy (if available), and relevant traits.

## Retainers vs. Employees

| Category | Examples | Oath? | Dismissal |
|----------|---------|-------|-----------|
| Retainers | Household knights, Foot Soldiers, squires, ladies-in-waiting | Yes | Cannot dismiss without Honor penalty; affects Loyalty of others |
| Employees | Servants, entertainers, professionals, esquires | No | Dismissible at will, though long service creates informal obligation |

**Retainer bond:** The lord has given his word to train, protect, and care for retainers. Breaking this word causes Honor loss and destabilizes the household's Loyalty.

## Standard of Living

Standard of Living = Family Expense + Pay Bonus + £5 (army/basic provision allocation).

**Family Expense** = 10% of Customary Revenue.

For a £300 demesne baron: Family = £30 → Standard of Living = £30 + Pay Bonus + £5.

Compare to KAP 5.1 Grades of Maintenance:
- A £100 demesne baron: ~£15 family → Superlative maintenance
- All but the very poorest barons qualify for Superlative maintenance automatically

**Bachelor baron (no wife/children):** Approximately 20% less spending on family; may be treated as equivalent lavish personal expenditure (rule of thumb: ignore the savings).

**Money Fief Standard of Living:** If the liege lord provides food and lodging, Standard of Living is normal. If the knight pays his own room and board, halve the spent £ when determining Grade of Maintenance.
