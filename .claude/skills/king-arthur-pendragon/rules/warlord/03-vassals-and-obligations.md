# Vassals and Obligations

Covers all feudal duties running in both directions — upward to the king, downward to vassals — including servitium debitum, scutage, aids, reliefs, wardship, marriage rules, and all exact monetary values.

**Use when:** A baron must fulfill or demand feudal service, adjudicating inheritance, calculating army size obligations, or when a lord or vassal wishes to invoke or deny a feudal right.

---

## Feudal Obligations: Upward (Baron to King)

### Servitium Debitum ("Debt of Service")

The military obligation owed to the liege lord based on Customary Revenue of the WHOLE honour (including enfeoffed lands).

**Formula:**
- 1 knight + 2 field Foot Soldiers + 1 garrison Foot Soldier per £10 of Honour Customary Revenue
- Any remainder (less than £10) goes to garrison Foot Soldiers at £1 each

**Example:** £320 honour → 32 knights + 64 field Foot + 32 garrison Foot (servitium debitum). The king may set a lower number by grace; the difference is converted to Discretionary Fund (see below).

**Reduced Servitium Debitum:**
- If the king reduces the requirement, income normally going to the "missing" knights/Foot is diverted to Discretionary Fund.
- Rate: £4 per knight reduced, £0.5 per Foot Soldier reduced.
- If 10 Foot Soldiers are eliminated, an additional £0.5 is saved (Officers' Pay Bonus).

### Royal Perquisites (Perqs)

These are special rights the king exercises when land changes hands or during minorities.

#### Escheat
Reversion of land to the king when a baron dies without an heir or commits certain felonies. The king may re-grant the land, keep it, or add it to the royal demesne. An escheator (royal officer) occupies and inventories the land during vacancy.

#### Primer Seisin
The king collects **one full year's profits** if the heir is in possession of the land when it changes hands, or **half a year's profits** if the lands are held by a warden/custos during the transition.

#### Relief
An inheritance tax paid by the heir before taking possession of the holding.
- Standard rate: **1 year's income** of the holding
- Paid at time of inheritance; the heir cannot exercise lordship until relief is paid
- The king may reduce or waive relief as a mark of favor

#### Wardship
When an heir is a minor (under age 21), the king may exercise wardship:
- The king (or his appointee) administers the holding during the minority
- All income from the holding during wardship goes to the king (or wardship holder)
- The ward's personal maintenance is the responsibility of the wardship holder
- At majority (21), the heir pays relief and takes possession
- The king may grant wardship to another baron as a favor or reward

#### Marriage
The king has the right to approve (or deny) the marriages of his tenants-in-chief and their heirs, especially female heirs.
- A baron must obtain royal permission before marrying a widowed baroness or heiress
- Failure to obtain permission = potential fine or loss of favor
- The king may arrange a marriage for a ward; the ward may object but must pay a fine to refuse

## Feudal Obligations: Downward (Baron to Vassals)

### Protection
A baron must protect his vassals from external threats. Failure to protect = vassals may legitimately seek a new lord (with legal process).

### Justice
A baron must provide justice within his lands via his seigniorial court. Commoners and knights both have access to this court for disputes within the holding.

### Maintenance
The baron must maintain the holding in a state that allows vassals to fulfill their own obligations.

### Forms of Address
- Count: "My Lord Count" or "My Lord"
- Baron: "My Lord Baron" or "My Lord"
- Knight: "Sir [Name]"
- Banneret: "Sir [Name]" (the title does not change form of address)

## Feudal Obligations: Vassal to Baron

### Homage
A permanent oath of loyalty. The vassal knight kneels, places hands in the baron's hands, and swears homage. This bond cannot be broken without dishonor unless the baron breaches his obligations.

### Fealty
A vow of (temporary) loyalty. Less binding than homage. Miles (cavalry) swear fealty rather than homage. Can be re-sworn to a new lord if the previous lord is dead or has released the vassal.

### Servitium Debitum (Vassal's)
Each vassal knight owes military service according to his holding:
- Standard: 1 knight + 2 field Foot + 1 garrison Foot per £10 of his Customary Revenue
- For a £20 manor: 2 knights + 4 field Foot + 2 garrison Foot
- The vassal must present himself and his men when the baron calls the army

### Aids (Feudal Aids)
Three customary aids that a baron may demand from his vassals without requiring consent:
1. **Ransom the baron:** When the baron is captured and must be ransomed
2. **Knight his son:** A one-time aid when the baron knights his eldest son
3. **Dower his daughter:** A one-time aid when the baron arranges his eldest daughter's first marriage

Amount of each aid: typically 1/10 of the vassal's annual income for that year.

### Scutage (Shield Fee)
A payment in lieu of knight service. Instead of providing knights, the vassal pays a cash fee.
- Known during Uther Period but not widely implemented until King Arthur's reign
- Rate set by the king; typically £1–2 per knight owed
- A baron may collect scutage from his own vassals if the king levies scutage on him

### Attendance at Court
Vassals must attend the baron's court when summoned. Failure = amercement (fine). Persistent failure = legal proceedings.

### Military Service
Vassals serve in the baron's army for the campaign season (typically 40 days per year at standard feudal practice, though Pendragon campaigns may be longer).

## Loyalty Rules

All vassals have a **Loyalty (Lord)** passion. Base value varies by circumstance.

**Starting Loyalty:** 15 for household knights; rolled for vassal knights (typically 3d6+3).

**Loyalty Effects:**
- If a vassal's Loyalty falls below 5, he may defect, seek a new lord, or actively work against the baron.
- A baron may raise Loyalty through gifts, attention, honors, and keeping his obligations.
- A baron lowers Loyalty by: failing to protect, failing to pay, demanding unreasonable service, showing public disrespect.

**Loss of Loyalty triggers:** The baron breaking fealty, demanding unauthorized aid, failing to provide justice, public humiliation of the vassal.

## Baronial Rights (Rights Over Vassals and Commoners)

### Rights Over Knights (Vassals)
- Receive homage and fealty
- Require military service
- Require attendance at court
- Approve (or deny) marriages of female vassals and wards
- Exercise wardship over minor heirs of vassals
- Receive relief from inheriting vassals

### Rights Over Commoners
- **Thief-Hanging (Infangthief):** Summary execution of a thief caught on the lord's land
- **Outfangthief:** Pursuing and executing a thief caught elsewhere (rare; requires explicit charter grant)
- **Seigniorial Court:** Jurisdiction over petty crimes, boundary disputes, marriage fees, burial fees
- **Gallows:** Right to maintain a gallows (if granted by the king)
- **Toll:** Right to collect for road/bridge/ford crossing (if granted)

### Seigniorial Court Income
The seigniorial court generates income through:
- Customary payments (fixed fees for stated events)
- Court fees (commoners paying to have cases heard)
- Fines (fixed charges for breaking laws)
- Amercements (arbitrary penalties for minor violations)

### Hundred Court Profits
If the baron holds the hundred court, he receives **3% of the holding's Customary Revenue** annually as Other Income.

## Inheritance Rules

### Standard Inheritance
1. The eldest son inherits the whole honour (primogeniture)
2. If no sons: daughters inherit equally (co-heiresses) or the eldest daughter inherits
3. If no children: the heir is determined by proximity of blood (brothers, then nephews, etc.)
4. The king must approve and receive relief before the heir takes possession

### Minor Heirs
- The king exercises wardship until age 21
- All income from the holding goes to the wardship holder during the minority
- The heir is knighted at 21 (if the family can afford it)

### Widow's Inheritance
- A widow is entitled to dower: 1/3 of her husband's lands for life
- She cannot remarry without the king's permission (if her late husband was a baron)
- If she has minor children, she may petition for wardship of her own children and the holding

### Attainder
If a baron is convicted of treason or a capital felony:
- He forfeits all lands and titles
- His heirs are "tainted" and cannot inherit his titles or lands
- The king may pardon specific heirs separately

### Escheat
If a baron dies with no valid heir, or is attainted without pardon, land escheats to the king.

## Feuds

A feud is a state of open hostility between two baronial families, short of full-scale war.

**Initiating a Feud:** A deliberate injury (killing, property destruction, public humiliation) without subsequent satisfaction.

**King Uther's Stance on Feuds:** He permits limited feuds but may intervene if they damage the realm's stability.

**Conducting a Feud:**
- Raiding (small parties attacking property, not open battle)
- Litigation (using the courts to harass)
- Blocking trade or travel
- Seeking the king's ear to damage the enemy's standing

**Ending a Feud:**
- Payment of compensation (wergild equivalent)
- Marriage alliance
- Royal decree (king orders it ended)
- Destruction of one party

**Feud vs. War:** A feud becomes war when armies are raised. Unauthorized war between barons is illegal and may provoke royal intervention.

## Calculating Army Size

**Full formula from Customary Revenue:**

| Per £10 of Honour CR | Knights | Field Foot | Garrison Foot |
|----------------------|---------|-----------|---------------|
| From whole honour | 1 | 2 | 1 |

**Honour total army (servitium debitum):**
- Demesne Army + Vassals' Army = Total Servitium Debitum

**Vassals' contribution:** Each vassal provides knights and Foot Soldiers according to their own holding per the same formula.

**Army expense:** 55% of Demesne Customary Revenue covers the Demesne Army maintenance.

**Eschille:** A squadron of 5–15 mounted/armored warriors; typical = 10 knights.

**Conroi:** The baron's complete army; composed of one or more eschilles plus Foot Soldiers.

**Example — Count Roderick (£1,574 CR):**
- Servitium debitum: 157 knights + 314 field Foot + 157 garrison Foot
- 15 eschilles

## Mercenaries

A baron may hire mercenaries beyond his servitium debitum:
- Foot Soldiers: £1 each per campaign season
- Mounted sergeants: £2–3 each
- Knights: £5–10 each (very expensive)
- Cost comes from Discretionary Fund
- Mercenaries have no Loyalty (Lord) — they serve for pay; unpaid mercenaries defect

During the Anarchy: mercenaries are common; many displaced knights sell their swords.
