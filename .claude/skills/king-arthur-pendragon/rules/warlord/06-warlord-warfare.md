# Warlord Warfare

Covers raising armies, army composition, mercenaries, campaigning, the Assize of Arms, and battle sizes for the Early Phase.

**Use when:** Determining how many troops a baron can field, calling up vassals, hiring mercenaries, adjudicating campaign supply, or using the Book of Battle with Early Phase army sizes.

---

## Army Composition

### Formula: Army from Customary Revenue

Every £10 of Honour Customary Revenue generates (at full servitium debitum):
- 1 knight
- 2 field Foot Soldiers
- 1 garrison Foot Soldier

**Demesne Army vs. Total Army:**
- **Total army (servitium debitum)** = knights and Foot from ALL honour land, including enfeoffed
- **Demesne army** = knights and Foot from demesne land only (the baron's household knights + his own Foot)
- **Vassals' army** = knights and Foot provided by vassal knights from their own manors
- Total army = Demesne army + Vassals' army

**Example (£300 Demesne, £80 Vassals, £380 total):**
- Servitium debitum: 38 knights, 76 field Foot, 38 garrison Foot
- Demesne army: 30 knights, 60 field Foot, 30 garrison Foot
- Vassals' army: 8 knights, 16 field Foot, 8 garrison Foot

### Army Expense

Army expense = 55% of Demesne Customary Revenue.

This covers:
- Household knights (with horses and equipment)
- Foot Soldiers (field and garrison)
- Squires (1 per knight; additional squires cost £1/year each from Discretionary Fund)
- Lord's own equipment as part of his Standard of Living (£4 of the army budget covers the lord's needs)

### A £10 Demesne Manor Army (New Model)
- Army expense (£5.5): Lord Knight with horses £3, Squire with horse £1, three Foot Soldiers £1.5

## Army Units

### Eschille
A squadron of 5–15 mounted and armored warriors. The typical eschille in Pendragon contains **10 knights**.

### Conroi
The baron's full army; composed of one or more eschilles plus all Foot Soldiers. A baron commands his conroi as a unified force.

**Example conrois:**
- Sir Staterius, Baron of Thornbush: 24 knights + 52 Foot Soldiers
- Count Roderick of Salisbury: 15 eschilles (150 knights + ~300 Foot Soldiers)
- Duke Ulfius: 124 knights + proportional Foot Soldiers (CR £1,312.9)

## Raising the Army

### Summoning Vassals

1. The baron issues a formal call to arms (written or messenger-borne).
2. Each vassal has 40 days to muster at the appointed location.
3. Failure to muster without excuse = loss of Loyalty; repeated failure = legal action.

**Minimum notice:** 7 days for local defense; 40 days for a campaign in another county.

### Household Knights

Household knights are the baron's most reliable forces — they live in his household and are always at hand. No muster delay.

### Foot Soldiers

Foot Soldiers are levied from the baron's demesne population. They are not professional soldiers; they are trained levies. Garrison Foot Soldiers are permanently stationed; field Foot Soldiers are called up for each campaign.

### Calling the Array (Full Levy)

In emergencies, a baron may call up every able-bodied man from his estates. This provides additional Foot Soldiers beyond the servitium debitum but:
- These troops are poorly equipped (peasant weapons, no armor)
- They are used for garrison, fortification, and labor, not front-line battle
- They cannot be kept in the field for more than a few weeks without disrupting agriculture

## Mercenaries

Mercenaries are available throughout the Early Phase, especially during the Anarchy when many knights are displaced.

### Costs

| Type | Pay per Campaign Season | Notes |
|------|------------------------|-------|
| Foot Soldiers | £1 each | Basic infantry |
| Mounted Sergeants | £2–£3 each | Better trained; light cavalry |
| Knights | £5–£10 each | Variable; highly sought |
| Specialist (engineers, siege crews) | £3–£5 each | Required for siege equipment operation |

All pay from Discretionary Fund. If pay is missed, mercenaries defect (no Loyalty; purely contractual).

### Mercenary Loyalty

Mercenaries have **Loyalty (Contract) = 5** by default. They fight competently but will not sacrifice themselves. If the baron is losing badly and payment is doubtful, roll Loyalty vs. 10 or they desert.

### Finding Mercenaries

During the Anarchy Period: Common. Roll Intrigue or use Diplomacy to locate and negotiate with mercenary companies.

During Uther Period: Less common but available near cities and ports.

## Campaign Rules

### Supply

A campaigning army consumes resources at the rate of **1 Lot per month per 10 knights** (and proportional Foot). Lots are taken from the baron's estates (Render) or seized from enemy territory (raiding).

Raiding enemy territory for supply: roll Stewardship to organize; reduces the raided area's Customary Revenue by 1d6×10% for the following year.

### March Rate

- Mounted army: ~20 miles/day on good roads; ~10 miles/day on poor terrain
- Foot army: ~12 miles/day on good roads; ~6 miles/day on poor terrain

### Battle Sizes for the Early Phase (Table I.1)

| Unit/Intensity | Scale | Knights | Total Combatants |
|---------------|-------|---------|-----------------|
| Fight | n/a | Fewer than 50 | Up to 500 |
| Clash | 0 | 50–150 | 500–1,500 |
| Engagement | 0 | 150–300 | 1,500–3,000 |
| Small | 0 | 300–800 | 3,000–8,000 |
| Medium | 5 | 800–1,200 | 8,000–12,000 |
| Large | 10 | 1,200–2,000 | 12,000–20,000 |
| Huge | 10 | 2,000+ | 20,000+ |

**Notes for Book of Battle use:**
- Battle sizes are calibrated for later periods; armies are smaller in the Early Phase
- Use the above table to determine appropriate Battle Size for Early Phase encounters
- Armies above "Small" are exceptional in the Uther and Anarchy Periods

## Assize of Arms 515 (Boy King Period)

In 515, King Arthur issues the Assize of Arms, standardizing military equipment requirements.

### Requirements by Income

| Annual Income | Required Equipment |
|---------------|-------------------|
| £10 (manor) | Sword, shield, chainmail (or equivalent) |
| £20–£40 | As above + horse (rouncy minimum) |
| £40+ (knight) | Full knightly kit: charger, chainmail, lance, sword, shield |
| Baron | As knight + must maintain household knights per servitium debitum |

### Assize of Arms: Key Provisions

1. All free men must maintain arms appropriate to their income.
2. Arms cannot be sold, pawned, or given away without replacement.
3. Lords are responsible for ensuring their men are properly equipped.
4. Annual musters (reviews of arms) to verify compliance.
5. Failure to comply = fine; repeated failure = loss of land/status.

## Army Budget Summary

From a £300 Demesne Customary Revenue baron:

| Expense | Amount |
|---------|--------|
| Army (55% of Demesne CR) | £165 |
| — covers: 30 household knights, 60 field Foot, 30 garrison Foot | |
| Court (25% of Demesne CR) | £75 |
| Family (10% of Demesne CR) | £30 |
| Discretionary Fund (10% of Demesne CR + Free Income) | £30 + Free |
| Household Staff (50% of Demesne Assized Rents) | varies |
| Manorial Staff (25% of Demesne Assized Rents) | varies |
| Raw Materials (25% of Demesne Assized Rents) | varies |

A baron choosing to reduce his army (fewer than servitium debitum) gains extra Discretionary Fund:
- Each knight eliminated: +£4 to Discretionary Fund
- Each Foot Soldier eliminated: +£0.5 to Discretionary Fund
- Risk: the king may revoke the grant if the baron consistently cannot fulfill servitium debitum

A baron choosing a larger army (beyond servitium debitum) pays from Discretionary Fund:
- Extra knights: £4 each
- Extra Foot: £0.5 each

## Warfare Periods

### Uther Period (485–495)
- Uther commands all armies against Saxons
- Feuds between barons are permitted but regulated
- Unauthorized war between barons risks royal intervention
- Saxon threat primarily from the east and southeast

### Anarchy Period (495–509)
- No High King; each baron defends himself
- Many castles change hands (see Appendix A tables)
- Mercenaries are common and opportunistic
- King Idres of Cornwall expands significantly
- King Nanteleod of Escavalon is the primary British warlord late in the period
- County courts often non-functional
- Bandits and monster threats increase

### Boy King Period (509–519)
- King Arthur consolidates power through military campaigns
- Novel Disseisin, Mort D'ancestor, and Darrein Presentment assizes restore order (see `07-baronial-politics.md`)
- Assize of Arms (515) standardizes military obligations
- Battle of Badon Hill ends the period (~519–520)
- Kings who submit to Arthur: Idres, Cadwy, Nanteleod
- Kings who resist: fought and defeated
