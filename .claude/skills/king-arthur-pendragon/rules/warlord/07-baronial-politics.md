# Baronial Politics

Covers all political mechanics — loyalty rules, rebellion, the royal perqs system, the Anarchy's political landscape, the Boy King's legal reforms, and the Friends and Foes system.

**Use when:** Determining NPC relationships, adjudicating a baron's standing with the king, resolving loyalty crises, running political intrigue, or applying Anarchy-period or Boy King-period political rules.

---

## Loyalty Mechanics

Every relationship between a vassal and his lord is tracked via the **Loyalty (Lord)** passion.

### Starting Loyalty Values

| Relationship | Starting Loyalty |
|-------------|-----------------|
| Household knight (sworn retainer) | 15 |
| Vassal knight (enfeoffed) | Roll 3d6+3 (average 13.5) |
| Officer | Roll 3d6+3 |
| Mercenary | 5 (contractual only) |
| Miles/Eques (fealty, not homage) | Roll 3d6 (average 10.5) |

### Loyalty Modifiers (Annual Events)

Each winter, review significant events. Loyalty shifts:

| Event | Change |
|-------|--------|
| Baron protected the vassal in battle | +1d6 |
| Baron gave a significant gift | +1d3 |
| Baron honored the vassal publicly | +1 |
| Baron was victorious in war | +1 |
| Baron failed to protect the vassal | −1d6 |
| Baron demanded unauthorized aid | −1d3 |
| Baron publicly humiliated the vassal | −1d6 |
| Baron failed to pay wages or provide maintenance | −1d3 per incident |
| Baron was captured or badly defeated | −1d3 |
| Graft discovered (any officer) | −5 to entire household |

### Loyalty Consequences

| Loyalty Level | Effect |
|--------------|--------|
| 20+ | Passionate loyalty; vassal will sacrifice himself |
| 15–19 | Loyal; fulfills all obligations readily |
| 10–14 | Adequate; fulfills obligations but no more |
| 5–9 | Wavering; may delay, make excuses, or underperform |
| 1–4 | Unreliable; may defect to an enemy or simply leave |
| 0 | Defection, betrayal, or active opposition |

## Baronial Rights (Political Dimension)

### Right of Revolt

A baron has the **right of revolt** (legal right to resist the king) if:
- The king has violated his own obligations to the baron
- The king has seized land without due process
- The king has failed to provide justice when requested

Invoking the right of revolt requires:
1. Formal declaration (diffidatio) — "I withdraw my homage"
2. This ends the legal relationship; the baron is no longer a vassal
3. The king may treat this as rebellion, but it is legally distinguishable from treason

**Diffidatio:** A formal statement of defiance by a baron to end his vassalage. It is the ONLY legal way to end the homage relationship. It is legal if the king is in breach of his obligations.

### Regal Service

Certain rights and ceremonies belong exclusively to the king:
- **Crown-Wearing:** The king wears his crown on high feast days; barons must attend
- **Baptism:** The king may be godfather to a baron's child; this creates additional loyalty obligations
- **Trial by Combat:** A baron may request trial by combat in the King's Bench for serious legal matters
- **Champion's Right:** The king's champion fights duels on his behalf

## Royal Perqs and Political Leverage

The king can use royal perqs as tools of political pressure:

### Escheat Threats
The king can threaten escheat against a baron he dislikes by:
- Finding technical grounds for felony accusations
- Refusing to approve an heir's claim
- Using Primer Seisin aggressively (demanding a full year's income at every succession)

### Wardship as Reward or Control
- Granting wardship of a rich minor heir to a loyal ally = significant income for the ally
- Giving wardship of a rival's minor heir to that rival's enemy = political weapon

### Relief Manipulation
- The king may reduce or waive relief as a favor: builds loyalty
- The king may demand above-standard relief: extracts wealth and punishes
- Relief is nominally one year's income, but the king sets the actual amount

### Marriage Control
- The king arranges marriages of widows and heiresses
- A well-arranged marriage between two barons = political alliance
- A forced marriage = resentment and potential trouble

## King's Grace / Favor System

A baron's standing with the king is tracked informally by the Gamemaster. Key factors:

| Factor | Effect on Standing |
|--------|-------------------|
| Fulfilling servitium debitum reliably | +favor |
| Attending court when summoned | +favor |
| Sending gifts and presents to the king | +favor |
| Military success on the king's behalf | +favor |
| Hosting the king on his progress | +favor |
| Failure to muster troops | −favor |
| Feuding with another baron without permission | −favor |
| Building a castle without royal license | −favor |
| Unauthorized marriage | −favor |
| Treason, diffidatio without cause | −favor, potential attainder |

**High Royal Favor:** The king grants additional liberties, reduces relief demands, approves favorable marriages, grants wardships.

**Low Royal Favor:** The king scrutinizes the baron's obligations, demands full relief, denies liberty grants, may find grounds for escheat.

## Political Intrigue Mechanics

### Friends and Foes (Appendix E)

To randomly determine the relationship between any two knights or lords, roll 3d6 and apply situational modifiers.

**Situational Modifiers:**

| Situation | Modifier |
|-----------|---------|
| Enemy of an enemy | +1 |
| Enemy of a friend | −3 |
| Family by blood | +5 |
| Family by marriage | +3 |
| Friend of an enemy | −3 |
| Friend of a friend | +3 |
| Same liege lord | +3 |
| Opposing liege lord | −3 |
| Each Trusting/Suspicious at 16+: | +2/−2 |
| Each Generous/Greedy at 16+: | +1/−1 |
| Each Forgiving/Vengeful at 16+: | +1/−1 |

**Result Table:**

| 3d6 Total | Relationship |
|-----------|-------------|
| 3–4 | Hatred: Hate (Name) passion at 12+1d6; likely feud |
| 5–8 | Unfriendly: Directed Trait Suspicious (Name) +10 |
| 9–12 | Neutral: No game effect |
| 13–16 | Friendly: Directed Trait Trusting (Name) +10 |
| 17–18 | Loyalty: Loyalty (Name) passion at 12+1d6; reliable ally |

### Intrigue vs. Courtesy vs. Diplomacy

| Skill | Use |
|-------|-----|
| Courtesy | Navigating one's own court; social graces |
| Intrigue | Court politics; scheming; gaining information |
| Diplomacy | Formal discourse between lords and kings; treaty negotiation; arranging marriages |

**Diplomacy starting value:** 2 (not a knight starting skill; typically held by clerics and educated administrators).

**Diplomacy scope:** Settling disputes between barons; dealing with king's officers; dealing with cities, bishops, abbots; setting terms of mutual raiding; arranging formal battles; negotiating marriages between powerful families; competing to rent Fee Farms.

**Courtesy as substitute for Diplomacy:** Anyone with Courtesy may attempt Diplomacy at ¼ Courtesy skill. Modifiers may apply.

## Anarchy Period Political Rules

### Overview (495–509)

With no High King:
- Each baron defends himself and his honour
- County courts often non-functional
- No central authority to arbitrate feuds
- Royal perqs (Escheat, Relief, Wardship) are not enforced
- New castles may be built without license
- Mercenaries are common

### Key Anarchy Political Dynamics

**Logres counties during the Anarchy:**
- Caerwent, Caercolun: Occupied by Angles (Cwichelm, King of the Angles)
- Caercolun, Hartland, Linden: East Saxon (Æscwine) and Angle pressure
- Hantonne, Dorsette: West Saxon (Cerdic) and South Saxon (Ælle) expansion
- Regnentium: South Saxon (Ælle) territory
- Cantium: Kentish Saxon (Æsc)
- Ascalon, Jagent, Tintagel: King Idres of Cornwall
- Summerland: King Cadwy (allied with King Nanteleod c. 507)
- Glevum, Wuerensis: Contested; Sir Saraviz of Glevum (self-proclaimed), King Leodegrance
- Silchester, Rydychan, Berroc: Duke Ulfius (most powerful British baron)
- Salisbury: Countess Ellen; Player-knights of Salisbury

**Baronial Replacement Table (Table 5.1):**
Used when a holding's lord is unknown or vacant in the Anarchy. Roll to determine who has seized control:

| 1d20 | Replacement Holder |

## Boy King Period Legal Reforms

King Arthur restores order through three major legal instruments in the Boy King Period:

### Assizes of the White City (c. 509–510)

A package of laws proclaimed at Winchester (White City) at the beginning of Arthur's reign.

**Key provisions:**
- Re-establishes the King's authority over all land in Logres
- Barons holding land taken during the Anarchy must submit to Arthur or face dispossession
- County courts are re-established under royal sheriffs
- All fee farms and escheats are reasserted as royal rights

### Novel Disseisin ("Recent Dispossession")

A plaintiff makes an accusation in court that he has been deprived of lands by the defendant.
- The court **immediately restores** the plaintiff to the holding upon the accusation
- The court then institutes escheatment proceedings to find the proper heir
- Burden of proof is on the defendant to prove lawful possession

**Effect in play:** A baron who seized land during the Anarchy may lose it very quickly if the original holder (or heir) survives and makes a Novel Disseisin claim.

### Mort D'Ancestor ("Death of an Ancestor")

A plaintiff claims in court that the defendant unlawfully seized a holding upon the death of an ancestor of the plaintiff.
- Used to reclaim land taken during the minority of an heir
- The court determines the proper heir and orders return of the holding

**Effect in play:** Heirs coming of age in the Boy King Period can use Mort D'Ancestor to reclaim lands seized during the Anarchy.

### Darrein Presentment ("Last Presentation")

A plaintiff accuses a defendant of unlawfully seizing the right of advowson (the right to appoint the head of a church or abbey).
- Applies to church appointments that were seized during the Anarchy
- Restores the advowson right to the proper holder

## Rebellion Mechanics

### Grounds for Rebellion

A baron may rebel against the king under these circumstances:
1. The king has violated feudal obligations (failed to protect, failed to provide justice)
2. The king has seized land without cause
3. The king has taxed beyond customary limits without consent

### Rebellion Process

1. **Grievance:** The baron articulates his grievance (public or private)
2. **Diffidatio:** Formal withdrawal of homage (required for legal rebellion)
3. **Alliance-building:** The baron seeks allies among other disaffected barons
4. **Military action:** The rebellion becomes open war

### Consequences of Failed Rebellion

- Attainder: loss of all lands and titles
- Heirs are tainted; cannot inherit
- The king distributes the attainted lands to loyal barons

### Consequences of Successful Rebellion

- Extremely rare; usually ends in negotiation rather than overthrow
- The king may be forced to concede a charter of rights or grant relief
- In King Arthur's case, the rebellious barons (Kings of the North and similar) are defeated militarily

## Economics of Political Power

### Converting Render to Treasure

If a baron wishes to save his Discretionary Fund for future use (rather than spending it as Render, which spoils):
- £1 Fund = £0.5 Treasure (exchange rate; conversion has a cost)
- Treasure can be stored indefinitely and used for ransoms, bribes, large purchases

### Borrowing Money

A baron may borrow from the Royal Usurer:
- Available in major cities (Londinium, Winchester, etc.)
- Interest rates charged by the Royal Usurer:

| Loan Term | Monthly Interest |
|-----------|----------------|
| Short (1–6 months) | 5% per month |
| Medium (6–12 months) | 3% per month |
| Long (1–3 years) | 2% per month |

- Failure to repay: the usurer appeals to the king; the baron's estates may be seized
- Borrowed income = Discretionary Fund for the year received; must be repaid

### Geld (Tax)

The king levies a land tax (geld) at **17% of Customary Revenue** in Uther's time. This is a fixed annual obligation included within the baron's budget (i.e., it is already accounted for in the expense calculations — the income figures in this book are post-geld).

During the Anarchy: Geld is not collected (no central authority).

During the Boy King Period: Geld is reasserted but may be set at a different rate by Arthur.

## Churches and Political Power

### British Church
- Nearly all privately owned by lords (advowsons)
- Lords collect money from institutions and maintain them
- The British Church answers to the king and secular lords

### Roman Church
- Owns its own establishments
- Answers to the king and the Pope (Rome)
- Collects its own income; independent of secular control
- Political tension between Roman and British Church is ongoing

### Church as Political Actor
- Abbots and bishops are barons (hold by free alms) and sit on the King's Council
- The Great Abbot/Archbishop = equal to count in political rank
- Church barons can be useful allies (provide prayers, legitimacy, literacy) but are exempt from military service
- Advowson = significant political gift (or source of contention)
