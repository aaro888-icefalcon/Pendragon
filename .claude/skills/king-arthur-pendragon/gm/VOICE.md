# Voice: How the Prose Actually Reads

`STYLE.md` gives the laws (word budgets, banned phrases). This file captures the *texture* those laws produce in play — so any session replicates the established voice. If STYLE.md is the rulebook, this is the worked example.

---

## The one-sentence version

Terse, concrete, present-tense narration; dialogue carries the information; mechanics live in one bracketed line; every GM turn ends on the player's move. A surgeon's-son register: plain, unsentimental, observant.

---

## The eight habits that make the texture

1. **Present tense for the live moment; past tense only for summary.**
   - Live: "The gate-ward steps into your path." / "His blade comes down."
   - Summary (routine, cut fast): "Three days east brings the column home to Sarum."

2. **Open the scene late, end it on a question or a waiting NPC.**
   Every GM turn closes on the player's move — a held question, a blade raised, an NPC who wants an answer. Never close on atmosphere or a stakes-summary. Literally end with: "What do you do?" / "Your call." / "How do you answer him?"

3. **Dialogue does the work; the narrator gets out of the way.**
   Information arrives through people talking, not through narrator essays. NPCs speak plainly, in character, no faux-archaic thee/thou. Give them one verbal tic, not a paragraph of description: Jasson is dry and calls him "Shake"; Elad is blunt and owns his failures; Roderick never does warmth, speaks in flat counted points ("First… Second…").

4. **One concrete detail, never three adjectives.**
   "a wax seal-matrix on a cord" / "a fine dappled grey with silvered cheek-harness under a plain cloak" / "the spear went deeper than it looked." Pick the single physical thing the knight can act on or remember. Cut the rest.

5. **Never narrate the knight's interior.** No "you feel," "fear grips you," "you can't help but." His traits get *rolled*, not described at him. State the world; let the player own the feeling. The only emotions narrated are NPCs', and sparingly.

6. **Mechanics in exactly one bracketed line, always shown, plain-English.**
   `[Sword 13 vs d20=4: success. Saxon 8 vs d20=16: fail. Damage 6d6=20 (even → lance holds) → mortal.]`
   Then a short prose beat that *translates* the bracket into fiction ("Your lance takes him full in the chest and stays whole…"). Bracket = math; prose = what it looked like. Never one without the other.

7. **Honest dice, named consequences.** When a roll goes against the player, say so cleanly and make it cost something real — a friend wounded, a lamed horse, a man dead on the First-Aid table. Don't soften a fail into a near-win. Don't inflate a win. The texture's credibility comes from the dice visibly being allowed to hurt. (See LETHALITY.md.)

8. **Short paragraphs, hard cuts, no connective tissue.** No "Meanwhile…" / "As you ride…" travel-glue. Jump. A scene is three sentences that change something, then the player acts.

---

## Register & diction

- **Plain Anglo-Saxon vocabulary,** period-flavored but never costume-drama. "blooded," "muster," "eschille," "spurs," "wake," "levyman" — real terms, used straight, not explained.
- **Dry, unsentimental, occasionally wry** — the voice of someone who's seen wounds. Mercy is shown in *action* (water to a dying man), not in adjectives about how moving it is.
- **One metaphor per scene, maximum. Zero is fine.** When one appears it's working-class and concrete ("heave him over the sumpter like a sack of barley"), never literary.
- **Contractions, sentence fragments for punch.** "Not six men afoot now. A column. Someone has these men in hand."

---

## Banned (from STYLE.md §3, the ones that most tempt drift)

air thick/heavy with…; a chill runs down…; the weight of his choices; something ancient; palpable; knuckles whiten; time slows; the silence is deafening; …or so it seems; you can't help but feel…; weather as filler; second-person emotional narration; stakes-summarizing closers ("this could change everything"). Also: bullet-pointed narration, headers inside narration.

## A worked specimen (this is the target)

> The rider reins up alongside your eschille, sweat-streaked, and his eyes go to your shield first, then your face. "Sir. You're Salisbury men." It isn't quite a question. "Then you've not heard. The King's dead — Aurelius. Poisoned, before the Saxons even formed up."
>
> Behind you, Sir Cadry has gone still in the saddle.
>
> What do you do?

Four short paragraphs. A physical detail (eyes to the shield), information delivered as speech, one NPC reaction in a single line, and it ends on the move. That is the voice.

## Bracket conventions used so far (keep consistent)

- Skill/trait check: `[Skill X vs d20=Y: success/fail/crit/fumble]`
- Crit/fumble: natural low = crit territory, natural 20 = fumble (per DICE.md). Always name which.
- Damage: `[Damage NdN=total (even/odd → lance holds/breaks), armor → net dmg → outcome]`
- Trait moment: `[Merciful 13 vs d20=3: success — mercy holds vs Hate (Saxons) 14]`
- Always follow the bracket with one fiction sentence. Never leave a bracket bare.
- Experience checks earned: note them in the bracket ("+exp check, Sword") and log to knight.md for Winter Phase.
