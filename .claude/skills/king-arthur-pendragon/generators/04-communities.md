# Communities

**Scope:** Villages, market towns, cities, tribal settlements, monastic communities, and any other gathering of people in Arthurian Britain.

**Use when:** Players arrive at an unplanned settlement, you need to characterize a place during preparation, or you want to distinguish two similar-seeming settlements from each other.

**Procedure:** (1) Decide whether it is a Rural Village, a Significant Town/City, or a Tribal/Marginal gathering. (2) Roll d12 for Rationale for Existence, Who Runs It, a Significant Local, and a Current Pressing Problem. (3) Roll d100 twice for Community Tags. (4) Synthesise results into a coherent picture. Use the "Involvements" table when you need a hook to the community.

*Source: WWN Communities chapter, reskinned for Arthurian Britain. Sci-fi entries replaced with genre-appropriate equivalents.*

## Rural Villages

Small farming hamlets, isolated manors with their dependent hovels, fishing villages, and charcoal-burner settlements.

### Rationale for the Village's Existence (d12)

| d12 | Reason |
|-----|--------|
| 1 | Once a garrison outpost of a Roman or British lord |
| 2 | A mine, quarry, or peat-cutting, perhaps now exhausted |
| 3 | A place where refugees of a calamity settled |
| 4 | Holy ground or a shrine to a local saint or pagan power |
| 5 | A particular crop, animal, or resource grows very well here |
| 6 | A safe waypost on a Roman road or river crossing |
| 7 | Refuge for a despised minority or outcast group |
| 8 | A former bandit camp that went legitimate |
| 9 | A self-ruled home for a particular kinship group |
| 10 | A safe base for salvagers who work nearby ruins |
| 11 | Decayed remnant of an ancient Roman town or fort |
| 12 | Grew up around a lord's manor or fortified hall |

## Significant Towns and Cities

Market towns, cathedral cities, Roman ruins reoccupied, fortified burhs, and great halls of powerful lords.

| d12 | Reason |
|-----|--------|

| 2 | A trade nexus that has greatly prospered |
| 3 | An industrial or productive centre — iron, cloth, grain |
| 4 | Heavy resource extraction nearby — timber, lead, tin |
| 5 | It controls a vital defensive point — ford, pass, harbour |
| 6 | It is built around a working Roman aqueduct, road network, or structure |
| 7 | A stronghold of a significant local culture or ethnic minority |
| 8 | A sacred city to an important faith — seat of a bishop or monastery |
| 9 | A shared market for the many villages of a fertile region |
| 10 | A place of great natural beauty or unusual healthfulness |
| 11 | A shelter from dangerous surrounding lands |
| 12 | The inhabitants are somehow bound here by custom, oath, or fear |

| 5 | A royal viceroy or appointed governor | A rich merchant | Food supplies are not arriving as they should |

| 5 | Their seers or shamans said it was ordained |
| 6 | The leadership wants to find something here |
| 7 | Their herds or quarry have led them here |
| 8 | They have been trapped here by circumstances |
| 9 | They are paralysed by internal dissent |
| 10 | They have been paid or induced to be here |

### Community Tag Entries

**Ancient Roman Infrastructure**
The community still has access to functioning Roman works — an aqueduct, a hypocaust system, paved roads, a bridge that is not falling down. This infrastructure may be managed by hereditary keepers who only partly understand it.
- **E:** Abusive lord overusing the infrastructure; outside agent seeking to cause havoc; reckless enchanter trying to steal its power
- **F:** The harried chief of the keepers; a scholar fascinated by the Roman works; a merchant entirely reliant on its use
- **C:** The infrastructure's costly maintenance is coming due; it is starting to fail; it was built for a purpose the locals no longer understand
- **T:** Irreplaceable infrastructure component; Roman-engineered tools worth vast sums; a mysterious device that controls it
- **P:** A dangerously energetic section of the works; a hidden control centre; a sanctified room within the infrastructure that locals use as a shrine

**Bad Neighbours**
The community has a conflict with a neighbouring community. Low-level skirmishes, sabotage, and ill-will characterise the relationship.
- **E:** A foreign lord profiting by the quarrel; a zealot who demands violent action; the real culprit who started it
- **F:** A despairing peacemaker of a shared faith; a local with family from the rival; a helpless ruler
- **C:** One side appears at fault but is actually less blameworthy; both rulers use the quarrel to distract their people; it was a minor dispute that is spiralling
- **T:** Proof of the culprit's guilt; a cache of weapons meant to start real bloodshed; treasure that would erase the cause of the dispute
- **P:** Dangerous no-man's-land between the communities; a burnt farm; a religious festival turned semi-riot

**Blood Feud**
Two or more groups within the community hate each other. Their neighbours and the local law have kept things from overt violence, but interference and mutual harm are constant.
- **E:** A wholly unsympathetic group leader; a schemer exploiting the feud; a ruler going to brutal excess to suppress it
- **F:** A reluctant participant in the feud; a local digging for the real truth; a merchant who would profit from peace
- **C:** The groups were formerly close allies; one group is favoured by local rulers; one side is getting completely out of hand
- **T:** Treasure the groups are fighting over; exculpating evidence; a lost symbol of former peace
- **P:** A bloody back alleyway; a sabotaged business; a tavern dominated by one faction

**Brilliant Innovation**
A local has come up with a wonderful new idea — a new agricultural method, a better way to work iron, a faerie-taught craft process.
- **E:** A grasping guildmaster; an overbearing local ruler; a local leader whose power is threatened by the change
- **F:** A visionary supporter of the innovator; an outside merchant seeking to profit; a local whose tenants would benefit
- **C:** The innovation requires materials only adventurers can find; the innovation is riskier than it seems; the innovator is actually a fraud
- **T:** A critical component for the innovation; wealth from a successful test run; vital plans and design documents
- **P:** An ambitious test zone; a guild-hall full of resentful locals; a tavern where people fight over who will benefit

**Broken Spirits**
The locals are in a state of despair and dull apathy. They have lost the things that gave them pride and hope.
- **E:** A cruel tyrant who broke them; a slave-taker preying on the hopeless; a merchant of despair and its costly escapes
- **F:** A determined young local leader; a proud old man who remembers better days; a furious rebel
- **C:** An outside power wants to keep them broken; their fall was due to their own sins; they could be very dangerous if they find their spirit again

**Corrupt Laws**
What law exists here is for sale or does not apply to favoured groups. Strangers may be fleeced, evildoers absolved for payment, powerful gentry free to act as they please.
- **E:** An immensely venal magistrate; a lord who sees no problem with the "natural" order; a crime boss openly exploiting the corruption
- **F:** A crusading law enforcer; a royal investigating official; a victim of a cruel injustice
- **C:** The favoured class is vital to the community's security; the locals would rather pay predictable bribes than face unpredictable justice; the real law is enforced by a secret group of natives

**Criminal Bosses**
One or more crime lords have powerful influence here. Local lawmen know about them but lack the strength to confront them.
- **E:** An overtly criminal chief; a well-controlled head of the watch; a crime boss planning to use the PCs as catspaws
- **F:** A victim of an untouchable boss; a determined bounty hunter; an ambitious criminal seeking to make room above
- **C:** The crime boss's support is what keeps the community prosperous; the local government is openly staffed by cartel members; locals will deal with PC troublemakers out of fear

**Cursed Circumstances**
The community has been cursed with some blight — a sorcerer's revenge, an outraged saint's wrath, a old pagan power disturbed. Outsiders wonder why the locals have not left.
- **E:** A charlatan offering false hope; a local demagogue blaming a convenient culprit; a native profiting from the curse
- **F:** A scholar seeking details of the blight; a stubborn curse-survivor; an aspiring curse-lifter with a secret advantage
- **C:** The curse has a profitable side effect; it was meant as a blessing; it can be directed by a willing conspirator
- **T:** A personal anti-curse ward; hidden wealth of a curse victim; a means to lift the curse
- **P:** An enterprise blighted by the curse; a festival held to pray for mercy; a ruin of a curse victim's home

**Decaying Roman Working**
A great Roman structure — aqueduct, mill, road-system, signal-tower — has been a critical part of the community but is now beginning to fail. The locals have no idea how to fix it.
- **E:** Saboteur from an enemy community; scavenger stealing critical components; an over-confident enchanter attempting a disastrous repair
- **F:** One of the structure's hereditary keepers; a native dependent on its effects; a desperate researcher
- **C:** Part of the community would profit by its failure; it risks catastrophic collapse; the rulers punish any talk of it failing
- **T:** A critical repair text for it; valuable Roman components; an intact Roman device crystallised from the structure's power
- **P:** A control nexus for the structure; a business entirely dependent on it; the site of a recent accident

**Duelling Lords**
Two different major powers are fighting over control of the community — a baron and a bishop, a mayor and a rich merchant, two rival lords.
- **E:** A third party profiting by the strife; a traitor working for one rival; an outsider who wants both rivals to fail
- **F:** A harried local peacemaker; an impotent official appointed by a disinterested higher authority; an appealing partisan of one rival
- **C:** The rival with the most legitimate claim is the least suitable ruler; a vital civic task is left undone; both rivals have terrible plans

**Enemy Within**
The locals are convinced there is some terrible threat working from within their society — witchcraft, faerie infiltrators, Saxon spies, shapeshifting creatures.
- **E:** A local inquisitor targeting personal enemies; the leader of the sinister evil; a traitor in service to it
- **F:** An unjustly accused victim; the local ruler trying to restrain the mob; a skilled and careful hunter of the evil
- **C:** The evil is real but actually running the inquisition; the hunters are creating the evil whether intentionally or not; the evil really is exactly as bad as the hunters claim

**Faerie Cooperation**
The community has a deal with some manner of faerie or otherworld power. It may be a genuinely peaceful exchange, but more likely involves acts and sacrifices other humans would refuse.
- **E:** A sinister overseer of the bargain's price; a local magnate growing rich off the dark deal; a faerie entity seeking to expand the terms
- **F:** A victim of the bargain's price; an outside investigator seeking the truth; a hereditary hunter of that faerie type come to purge the evil
- **C:** The bargain is actually reasonably fair and decent; the locals don't want it but neighbouring lords profit by it and force them into it; the faerie powers are actually better than the human lord they replaced

**Faded Remnant**
This community once was much larger. Only a tithe of citizens remains on the site, and much former architecture crumbles abandoned.
- **E:** A looter seeking to plunder the remains; a zealot with a plan to return to glory; an outside strongman who wants to coerce the locals
- **F:** A learned scholar of the noble past; a hard-scrabble present survivor; a newcomer who sees genuine hope in the place
- **C:** They do not fully understand what they have inherited; they were crushed because of their own past evils; they are not the actual heirs but squatters in an empty place

**Fallen Prosperity**
The community used to be much richer. Something happened recently to crush its source of income. Factions fight over what remains.
- **E:** An outside profiteer squeezing the newly-poor; a local monopolising the remaining income; a demagogue blaming everything on a convenient enemy
- **F:** A plucky local trying to make a new go of things; a harried distributor of limited charity; a ruined-rich native trying to maintain dignity
- **C:** Their loss is a rival's gain; someone is offering them a new industry at a terrible price; the leadership refuses to accept the new reality

**Foreign Enclave**
Most or a substantial minority of the locals are descended from foreigners — Roman settlers, Irish immigrants, Saxon foederati, Byzantine traders — and maintain their distinct identity.
- **E:** A ruthless independence fighter; an outside ruler with no regard for the locals; a local grandee preaching contempt for foreign ways
- **F:** A peacemaking local leader; a local in love with a foreigner; a pragmatic foreign magistrate
- **C:** Separatists are being supported by co-ethnics elsewhere; the polity's leaders don't want them here; they are hated by their own co-ethnics for some reason

**Guild Oligarchy**
Real control of this community lies with the senior members of the local guilds. Their decisions have the practical weight of law.
- **E:** A profoundly corrupt guild boss; an ambitious newcomer with brutal methods; a ruthless head of a guild that is really a criminal front
- **F:** A hard-bitten elder among the craftsmen; an outsider trying to make room; a reformer seeking to oust the corrupt guild heads
- **C:** The guilds have intermarried or become entangled with the ostensible rulers; the guilds offer protection from a real or imagined threat; they hate each other only slightly less than the competition

**Heavy Fortification**
The community is remarkably well-fortified — tall walls, concentric defences, a strategic position, a large body of garrison troops.
- **E:** An outside enemy seeking to pierce the defences; a rebel trying to declare independence; a heavy-handed ruler demanding protection money
- **F:** An industrious maintenance chief; the lord's appointed military commander; a local warning of impending danger
- **C:** The threat is gone but those who profit by the defences maintain them; the defences are impractical for a community this size; the cost is becoming unbearable

**Hidden Ruler**
While the community has a public leader, real authority is hidden from outsiders — a crime lord, a faerie power, a secret society, or a masterful manipulator.
- **E:** A secret cult-backed leader; a nefarious agent of an enemy power; a minor functionary who is actually the hidden master
- **F:** A frustrated outside authority; a local seeking better government; a victim of the hidden leader's will
- **C:** Most people know that the real authority is concealed; the hidden ruler is a mortal enemy of the legitimate authority; the hidden ruler's effective authority is over a large affiliated group

**Incompetent Leaders**
The community is led by one or more genuine incompetents — uncontrolled passions, hopelessly impractical ideals, pig-headed obstinacy, or profound laziness.
- **E:** An heir who is totally unsuited to their new role; a disinterested ruler forced on them by an overlord; a charismatic ninny with ridiculous plans
- **F:** A deposed former leader; a desperate elder; a victim of one of their bungled plans
- **C:** An outside rival is backing the fool; the idiot has tremendous institutional legitimacy; they provide a critical skill unrelated to ruling

**Inherited Roman Architecture**
Many of the community's structures date back to Roman times and have unusual architectural traits — bath-houses, paved streets, hypocausts, signal-towers. The locals find them too useful or too durable to destroy.
- **E:** A thing from below; an outside pillager bent on sacking the structures; a reckless explorer opening things best left sealed
- **F:** An heir to the ancient arts of maintenance; the chief of the local structure guard; the keeper of a particularly useful building
- **C:** The locals mine treasures from buried depths; the structures were built with a purpose the locals have lost; some require a dark sacrifice to keep functioning
- **T:** A key to unlock a sealed structure; a Roman artifact; a map to a hidden structure
- **P:** A mundane business in a remarkable Roman building; an ancient structure retrofitted into a habitation; a buried Roman street beneath the community

**Lawless Class**
Some class of citizen is above the law — tacitly or explicitly. They may be immune to punishment for crimes against commoners, or they may simply be too dangerous to confront.
- **E:** A professionally immune provocateur; cruelly indulgent gentry; a grasping mercantile oligarch
- **F:** A hapless magistrate; an avenger outside the law; a victim of an unpunishable crime
- **C:** The lawless themselves enforce a rough law among commoners; the immune are most dangerous to each other; special servants of the immune are also immune

**Martial Tradition**
The natives have a long tradition of military skill — organised levies, fighting monks, berserker hill-folk, skilled horsemen.
- **E:** A ruthlessly amoral military leader; an obsessive training-master; an outsider using the locals as brute muscle
- **F:** A determined local defender; a petitioner in need of their prowess; the keeper of the local martial tradition
- **C:** They use weapons only they can employ; they have bled themselves white in gaining glory; they are eager to conquer their neighbours

**Monstrous Tribute**
The locals have cut a deal with an unspeakable entity — a faerie lord, a pagan power, a terrible beast — trading tribute for forbearance or assistance.
- **E:** The entity's sinister local overseer; a local magnate growing rich off the arrangement; the entity seeking to expand the terms
- **F:** A victim of the tribute; an outside investigator; a hunter of that entity's type
- **C:** Most people know but say nothing; the entity genuinely protects them from something worse; some locals are beginning to question the price

**Pilgrimage Site**
The community is a destination for pilgrims — a saint's shrine, a holy well, a sacred stone, a miraculous image.
- **E:** A charlatan selling false relics; a rival shrine undermining this one; a lord trying to profit by controlling pilgrim traffic
- **F:** A genuine miracle-seeker; a shrine-keeper trying to maintain it honestly; a scholar documenting the holy history
- **C:** The miracles are becoming less frequent; a powerful local lord wants the shrine under his control; the shrine was originally a pagan holy place

**Raider Scourge**
The community is regularly raided by Saxons, Irish pirates, Pictish reavers, or brigand bands.
- **E:** The bandit or raider chief; a lord who profits by the chaos; a traitor within who signals the raiders
- **F:** A determined local defender; a merchant desperate to reopen safe trade; a survivor of a brutal raid
- **C:** The raiders are actually protecting the community against something worse; the raids are partly the community's own fault; some locals have arrangements with the raiders

**Rebel Stronghold**
This community harbours or supports rebels against their lord, the king, the church, or some established power.
- **E:** The lord's punitive expedition; a spy within the rebels; a faction of rebels with terrible plans
- **F:** An idealistic rebel leader; a local trying to maintain peace between rebels and loyalists; a sympathetic outsider
- **C:** The rebels' cause is genuinely just; the rebels are being manipulated by a third party; the rebels' victory would not benefit the common people

**Seat of Rule**
This is the administrative or political centre for the surrounding region — a lord's castle, a bishop's city, a king's hall.
- **E:** An ambitious rival for the centre's power; a hostile faction within; a foreign power with designs on the seat
- **F:** A local who needs the centre's proper functioning; an official trying to do honest work; a client community badly served by the current ruler
- **C:** The seat's authority is weaker than it looks; a recent scandal has badly damaged the ruler's legitimacy; the seat is more important symbolically than actually

**Secret Treachery**
Someone or some faction within this community is betraying it to its enemies — feeding information to Saxon raiding parties, poisoning the lord's wells, sabotaging the harvest.
- **E:** The traitor's controller; the traitor themselves; a faction that has decided collaboration is survival
- **F:** A suspicious local who has noticed something; an outsider who received a worrying message; an honest official who suspects but has no proof
- **C:** The treachery is being committed reluctantly under duress; the community's leaders already know but are afraid to act; the "traitors" are actually trying to prevent something worse

**Xenophobic Locals**
The locals deeply distrust or outright hate outsiders and foreigners. Strangers are viewed with suspicion, followed, overcharged, or actively threatened.
- **E:** A ruthless local agitator who stirs up hatred; a lord who profits by the isolation; a demagogue who blames foreigners for every misfortune
- **F:** A local with genuine curiosity about the world; a merchant who needs outside trade to survive; a local in love with a foreigner
- **C:** The xenophobia is rooted in a genuine past betrayal; outsiders are being kept away because the community has a terrible secret; a faction within genuinely wants contact with the outside world
