# Complications and Twists

**Scope:** Tables for what goes wrong, what goes right, historical events that shaped the present, the diseases of rule, NPC betrayal patterns, sudden shocks, and the slow pressures bearing down on the kingdom of Arthurian Britain.

**Use when:** You need to complicate an existing situation, generate background pressures bearing down on a court or faction, understand why this region is the way it is, or add a sudden twist to an adventure already in motion.

**Procedure:** Pick the relevant section for your need. For "what's wrong with the kingdom right now," roll on Current National Problems. For a historical event that shaped a region, roll on Historical Crises and Historical Events. For individual NPCs betraying or surprising the players, use the NPC Twist tables. For faction-level shocks, use the Government Diseases and Recent Events tables.

*Source: WWN Nation Construction, History Construction, Government Construction, and Characterization chapters, reskinned for Arthurian Britain. "Working" → Roman engineering or faerie enchantment; "Blighted" → cursed folk or Saxon raiders; "arratu" → blighted moor; "mage" → sorcerer or wise-man; "Deep" → mine or barrow-complex.*

## NPC Betrayal Patterns

When a Friend, patron, or ally turns against the players, roll for how and why.

### Form of the Betrayal (d10)

| d10 | How They Betray |
|-----|----------------|
| 1 | They were never loyal — they were an enemy agent from the beginning |
| 2 | They sold the players out to save their own life or property |
| 3 | They were threatened or blackmailed into it |
| 4 | They made a worse deal than the one they had with the players |
| 5 | They genuinely changed their mind and now believe the players are in the wrong |
| 6 | They panicked and gave up information under pressure without meaning to betray |
| 7 | They were deceived into betraying without realising that was what they were doing |
| 8 | Their loyalty was to something higher — their lord, their god, their family — not to the players |
| 9 | They were seduced or enchanted into disloyalty |
| 10 | A past grudge they never disclosed motivated everything all along |

### When It Is Discovered (d6)

| d6 | Discovery |
|----|-----------|
| 1 | Immediately — the players watch it happen |
| 2 | Shortly after — they find evidence within the same session |
| 3 | After the fact — they have to piece it together from consequences |
| 4 | By a third party who tells them |
| 5 | Long after — the damage is already done |
| 6 | Never conclusively — they suspect but cannot prove |

### What the Betrayer Wanted (d8)

| d8 | Motive |
|----|--------|
| 1 | Their own safety — they feared the consequences of loyalty more than disloyalty |
| 2 | Wealth — they were simply paid enough to turn |
| 3 | Protection of a loved one — someone they care about was threatened |
| 4 | Advancement — loyalty to the players was a dead end |
| 5 | Revenge — some old wrong, real or imagined, that the players did not know about |
| 6 | Ideological — they genuinely believe the players' enemies are right |
| 7 | Desperation — they had no good options left and this seemed least bad |
| 8 | Cowardice — they simply could not hold firm under pressure |
