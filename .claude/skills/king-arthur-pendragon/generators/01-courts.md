# Courts

**Scope:** Arthurian noble courts, church hierarchies, knightly households, criminal networks, and dynastic clans — any tight-knit group of NPCs with conflicting goals and shared stakes.

**Use when:** The players enter an unfamiliar noble hall, a bishop's palace, a monastery, a rich merchant's compound, or any place run by a web of interconnected personalities. Also use when generating the court of a named lord between sessions.

**Procedure:** (1) Decide the court type (Aristocratic/Religious/Business/Criminal/Familial). (2) Roll d12 for Main Theme, d12 three times for Major Figures, d12 twice for Minor Figures. (3) Roll d12 once each on Internal Conflict and External Conflict. (4) Roll d100 twice on the Court Tags table for flavor. Synthesize the results into a coherent whole.

*Source: WWN Courts chapter, reskinned for Arthurian Britain.*

## Court Types

| Type | Arthurian context |
|------|------------------|
| **Aristocratic** | A king's hall, an earl's castle, a baron's keep, a knight's manor |
| **Religious** | A bishop's court, an abbot's chapter, a hermit brotherhood, a pagan shrine-council |
| **Business** | A rich merchant's trading house, a craftsmen's guild, a wool-merchant combine |
| **Criminal** | A bandit chieftain's warband, a smuggler ring, a court faction that operates in secret |
| **Familial** | A noble clan with many branches, a knightly family dynasty, a peasant extended family of influence |

|-----|-------|

| 2 | Decadence — the lord feasts while the kingdom suffers |

| 11 | A member has become entangled with outlaws or pagans | Some vital act will infuriate a powerful neighbor |
| 12 | A faction in the court is secretly scheming against the lord | A rival has laid a legal claim against a court estate |

### Tag Entries (selected; use as needed)

**Affliction's Mark**
A persistent illness or curse gnaws at the court. The lord may be stricken, a beloved member, or a spreading blight that resists all prayer and physic.
- **E:** Manipulative physician offering false hope; a faction that profits if the afflicted die; spreader of the plague
- **F:** The stricken member; a struggling healer; a worried betrothed
- **C:** The sickness is a consequence of the court's own sins; a cure exists but demands a dire moral price
- **T:** A palliative that slows the disease; proof of its cause; the promised price of a perhaps-real cure
- **P:** Hushed and shuttered sickroom; gathering place with forced gaiety; untouched quarters of the recently dead

**Ancestral Duty**
The court was charged by its founder with some great task — protecting a sacred site, sheltering a holy bloodline, guarding a Roman road — and much of its authority rests on continuing to carry it out.
- **E:** Schemer who profits if the duty is disrupted; rebel who resents the duty and its costs; tyrannical lord who forces others to bear the burden
- **F:** A grimly-determined keeper of the old obligation; an outsider desperately reliant on the duty being performed; a member who suffers most from the work
- **C:** No one fully understands the duty's original meaning; the duty now costs more than the court can bear; a recent calamity has left them too weakened for the work
- **T:** Vital tool for carrying out the obligation; ancient grant or charter authorising the work; key to lifting the burden
- **P:** Ancient work-chamber; hall emblazoned with symbols of the duty; training hall for the next generation

**Awesome Legitimacy**
The court cannot be imagined away. No one can picture this family not ruling this land or this bishop not holding this see. Their destruction would be unthinkable.
- **E:** An implacable tyrant consecrated by custom; a manipulator who hides his will behind the court's authority; an outsider who would ruin the court despite the chaos
- **F:** A court member keenly aware of their responsibility; a local haplessly oppressed by the court; an outsider who deals with the court very cautiously
- **C:** The court really is as indispensable as it seems; the source of its untouchability is weakening; if it falls it takes much of local order with it
- **T:** Ancient regalia of authority; dusty deed to a rich estate; wealth of a failed rebel
- **P:** Long-forgotten spare audience chamber; ancient archive; gathering place in continuous use for generations

**Awkward Birth**
A pregnancy is roiling the court. A member is with child by the wrong man, or a new heir excludes a displeased prior claimant, or the child shows signs of faerie blood or worse.
- **E:** Elder who would see the problem "resolved"; a murderously suspicious husband; a demoted heir
- **F:** The naive young lover; a desperate hidden father; the worried mother
- **C:** Secret accusations of illegitimacy are circulating; the disgraced husband's family is enraged; the pregnancy is being used by its mother as a political lever
- **T:** Proof of the real father; blackmail material on the court's leadership; wealth that was hidden by the former presumptive heir
- **P:** Nursery far worse-appointed than the child's station demands; a room used as a gilded prison for the expectant mother; the stronghold of the disappointed former heir

**Blandished Leadership**
The lord or lady has been led astray by a favourite. They dote on their companion, granting extravagant favours and imperilling the court.
- **E:** A rival who sent the favourite as a gift or agent; the ruthlessly manipulative favourite; the murderously angry neglected spouse
- **F:** The sadly displaced former confidant; a frustrated and capable steward; a disapproving confessor
- **C:** The favourite has a supernatural edge in their appeal; the spouse likes the lord distracted for their own plans; the court is fighting for influence with the favourite rather than with the lord

**Cadet Branches**
The court's authority is splintered among multiple junior branches of the family, each claiming its share. They struggle over control of the main court.
- **E:** A disgraced scion with a plan for revenge; the lord who hates a particular branch; the scheming head of a junior branch
- **F:** A bastard seeking legitimate recognition; a visionary from a cadet branch; the rightful leader denied their place
- **C:** One "cadet" branch actually has the stronger claim but is too weak to press it; the main court plays the branches against each other; outside rivals want the court to remain divided

**Dark Secret**
The court conceals something that would destroy its position if revealed. Old treacheries, hidden crimes, secret bastardies, or dark bargains.
- **E:** The villain they are allied with; a ruthless keeper of secrets; an amoral and villainous senior member
- **F:** A secretly worried junior member; an outside investigator; a bitter survivor of the court's crime
- **C:** The act was accepted or normal at the time; revealing it would implicate other powerful people; the leadership has been quietly trying to make amends
- **T:** Proof of the heinous deed; treasure acquired through the sin; a valuable relic taken from a victim
- **P:** A secret archive full of dark evidence; a noble monument built on a corrupt foundation; a private sanctum for only the innermost circle

**Devil's Bargain**
The court made a bargain — with a pagan lord, a faerie queen, a dark enchanter, or a scheming bishop — that gave them a crucial edge. Now the price is coming due.
- **E:** The vile entity they pacted with; the lord planning to foist the price onto an innocent; the heartless creditor
- **F:** The undeserving victim of the price; a rival who suspects something is wrong; an enemy of the power they pacted with
- **C:** Only a small faction of the court knows about the bargain; the current lord didn't know about it until threatened; the power they pacted with is a mortal enemy of their kingdom

**Disputed Inheritance**
The court's rule is due to pass on, but multiple claimants exist. Each has some colorable legitimacy, and factions and secret supporters proliferate.
- **E:** The cruel and vicious heir; the incompetent heir who has the best legal claim; an outside enemy backing the strife
- **F:** The most talented heir with the weakest position; the hapless heir who needs protection; the loyal retainer trying to avert disaster
- **C:** Legitimacy rests on possession of a now-lost holy relic; if the struggle continues much longer the estate will be ruined; a selfish regent is exploiting the interregnum

**Excess Heirs**
The lord remains vigorous but has too many legitimate heirs. There is not enough wealth or position to give the losers a dignified station.
- **E:** The cruel but talented eldest; a scion in league with dark powers; the secretly murderous younger heir
- **F:** The capable but poorly-supported candidate; the hapless lord unable to enforce a choice; the vengeful retainer of a murdered heir
- **C:** The lord desires a Darwinian culling of heirs; the court desperately needs the heirs to cooperate against a common threat; several heirs were formerly unknown

**Fatal Extravagance**
The court is exhausting itself on tournaments, feasts, and displays of magnificence it cannot afford. It may be maintaining face before rivals, or being manipulated by merchants.
- **E:** The grandiose lord; the smiling merchant of expensive pleasures; the selfishly indulgent senior member
- **F:** The court member vainly trying to economize; the worried steward; the client left destitute by lack of the lord's attention

**Forbidden Romance**
Someone in the court is deeply in love with someone they should not be — a rival's kin, a social inferior, an incestuous bond, another member's spouse.
- **E:** The unreciprocating object who will not take no for an answer; the cruelly manipulative beloved; the court elder bent on ending it
- **F:** The earnest go-between friend; the appealing beloved; the aspiring peacemaker
- **C:** The reasons for opposing the match are extremely sound; the member is being exploited; a rival wants the match to go through in order to disgrace the member

**Foreign Ties**
The court has strong ties with a foreign power — a continental count, a Saxon king, a Byzantine merchant — that may or may not be hostile to Britain.
- **E:** A foreign spymaster making demands; a rival who despises the foreign power; a court member who is entirely in the foreign power's service
- **F:** An appealing foreign petitioner; a harried court member trying to square their obligations; an investigating official suspicious of the ties
- **C:** The foreign power used to be friendly to Britain but has recently become a rival; the court is secretly dependent on the foreign power's protection; the support consists of the foreign power not doing something they could do

**Hidden Blight**
One or more members of the court have faerie blood, a hereditary curse, or some condition they are concealing. Their particular affliction is not physically obvious but has severe hidden manifestations.
- **E:** The afflicted leader being shielded by others; a ruthless investigator seeking answers; a court member bent on "resolving" the matter permanently
- **F:** The afflicted struggling with their curse; a court member trying to protect the secret; a parent trying to help their afflicted child
- **C:** The blight is light enough to be lifted somehow; the blight is actually useful to the afflicted in some way; they disguise it as an extreme human inclination

**Impure Blood**
Lineage matters enormously to this court, and their bloodline would be considered tainted by others if the full truth were known — faerie ancestry, a base-born grandfather, or worse.
- **E:** A court assassin cleaning up loose ends; a rival prying into a dangerous rumour; a court member making a terrible bargain to erase the stain
- **F:** A court member who does not deserve the obloquy; an innocent threatened by the revelation; a hunter seeking what they allegedly bargained with

**Inadequate Tools**
The court's authority has long rested on a particular set of tools — violence, money, legal rights, clerical backing — but those tools no longer work. The court has no better ideas than to use them harder.
- **E:** A manipulator goading the lord to excess; a frustrated lord with no new ideas; a member scheming to restore their old power
- **F:** An anguished victim of their overreach; a member trying to find new ground; a member fearful of the consequences

**Inept Ruler**
The court's lord is incompetent or debilitated, but cannot be removed. The alternative is unacceptable, or their bungling is very much to the benefit of a powerful faction.
- **E:** An insane ruler; a sincere but wholly unsuitable leader; a sinister manipulator influencing the puppet lord
- **F:** A competent but unsupported alternative; a victim of the lord's bungling; a faction being crushed by the ineptitude
- **C:** The lord seems an idiot but is secretly pursuing a hidden goal; rival courts are backing this leader; the lord's removal would cause ruinous chaos

**Iron Obligation**
There are laws, oaths, or customs the court simply cannot break — sworn to a king, consecrated to God, bound by a faerie compact — and these obligations are preventing them from solving a growing problem.
- **E:** A suicidally rigid lord; a troublemaker who exploits the obligation; a traitor somehow immune to it
- **F:** An innovator trying to work around the obligation or have it dissolved; a troubled defender of it who sees the problem; an ally who cannot be helped because of it
- **C:** The obligation exists for a good reason and breaking it would be worse; it can be changed if a certain great deed is performed; the obligation's enforcer is corrupt or willing to bend judgment

**Ministerial Capture**
The upper servants — steward, chamberlain, chancellor — have taken effective control of the court. The lord is distracted, or the servants have irresistible blackmail, or they have the lord's complete unearned trust.
- **E:** A colorless senior official; a scheming steward; a spymaster turned kingmaker
- **F:** A minor court member who realises the problem; a lesser official upset by the corruption; the proper lord made helpless by the situation

**New Generation**
The court's prior leadership was recently decimated by age, illness, or battle, leaving young and inexperienced members in charge. They are bold but not yet wise.
- **E:** A reckless spare heir turned lord; an upstart using the position purely for personal advantage; a rival pouncing on their untested leadership
- **F:** A harried older counsellor who is being ignored; a baffled new lord who does not understand how governance works; an idealistic new office-holder with grand plans

**Poisonous Cliques**
The court is riven by two viciously hostile factions, both determined to ruin or destroy the other. All normal business is paralysed.
- **E:** An outside rival who provoked the clique formation; a traitor seeking mutual destruction; a brutal clique leader who will do anything
- **F:** An unaligned member trying to survive; an outside ally distressed by the infighting; a weak lord unable to rein in the hostilities

**Priestly Influence**
The court's lord is in the thrall of a particular cleric or holy figure. Their wishes carry undue weight, and this brings the hostility of rival religious factions.
- **E:** A Rasputin-like confessor; a zealously pious lord; a spider-minded court chaplain with strings on everyone
- **F:** A member who favours a different faith or priest; a cast-off former house chaplain; a client abandoned so that resources could go to the church

**Proxy Speaker**
The court's lord communicates only through a particular proxy — a chief minister, a spouse, an heir, a confessor — and all other lines of control have been cut off.
- **E:** A grasping favourite; an impatient heir-acting-as-regent; a scheming minister
- **F:** A worried child of the lord; a cast-off former counsellor; a persecuted enemy of the proxy

**Rampant Corruption**
The court is so corrupt that even mundane exercises of authority require bribing the right people. Members have little interest in the overall good.
- **E:** A hopelessly venal lord; a greedy kingmaker behind the scenes; a colorless chief official with sticky hands
- **F:** A member with genuine loyalty to principle; a member cast out for not playing along; a victim of a bribed crime

**Recent Brutality**
Something happened recently that went beyond all usual bounds of Arthurian politics. A massacre of a whole family, a humiliating public execution, a massacre of prisoners. Everyone is on edge.
- **E:** A lord with an out-of-control taste for blood; a court member with no one daring to check them; a returning exile coming for vengeance
- **F:** An heir to a recent victim; a helpless enforcer of the usual norms; a survivor of a massacre

**Regency Council**
A council of powerful regents runs the court because the lord is too young, sick, or incapable. Some regents genuinely serve the lord's interests; others exploit the regency.
- **E:** An abusive senior regent; a selfish parent-regent; a self-absorbed great noble guardian
- **F:** A loyal servant of the true lord; a well-meaning council member; a deposed former regent who was too honest

**Ruling Regalia**
The court's authority rests on ancient, powerful relics — a holy sword, a consecrated crown, a saint's bones — and any loss of them will throw the court into chaos.
- **E:** A master thief in the employ of a rival; a strong-arm court member plotting their chance; an outsider who just wants the relics' power
- **F:** The traditional guardian of the relics; the lord too weak to reliably safeguard them; a would-be thief trying to take them from an unworthy lord
- **C:** The relics were secretly lost some time ago and the current ones are forgeries; the power of the relics is objectively necessary for the lord to function; a bearer will die if the relics are removed too long

**Threatened Violence**
The shadow of impending death hangs over the court. Capricious executions, active assassins, or a particularly bloody custom puts everyone on edge.
- **E:** A paranoid lord seeing knives in every shadow; a master hired blade with a grudge; a renegade returned for vengeance
- **F:** A survivor of a recently-murdered member; a worried bodyguard seeking help; a frightened member convinced they are next

**Waning Wealth**
The court is heading toward poverty. Its source of income is under attack by a rival, a greedy overlord, or simple misfortune.
- **E:** A lord making unreasonable demands for member sacrifices; a merciless creditor; a monstrous enemy ruining their holdings
- **F:** A harried steward looking for money; a client left destitute by lack of support; a former manager of a now-ruined estate
