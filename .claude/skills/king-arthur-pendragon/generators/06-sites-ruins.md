# Sites and Ruins

**Scope:** Barrows, Roman ruins, faerie places, hermitages, ancient strongholds, abandoned villas, lost shrines, and other sites of adventure in Arthurian Britain.

**Use when:** Deciding what is inside a location the players are about to explore, generating a new ruin on a map, or adding character to a wilderness hex.

**Procedure:** (1) Decide what kind of site it is using the General Places tables. (2) Roll 1d100 twice for Ruin Tags or Wilderness Tags to give the place two overlapping characteristics. (3) Read each tag's E/F/C/T/P entries and blend the two tags together to generate enemies, friendly faces, complications, treasures, and specific places. You do not need all five elements — use whatever is most useful for the session.

*Source: WWN Ruin Tags, Wilderness Tags, and Establishing Ruins chapter, reskinned for Arthurian Britain. "Outsider" → faerie or demon; "Blighted" → cursed folk, beast-kin, pagan war-band; "Working" → Roman engineering, faerie enchantment, or holy relic; "arratu" → blighted moors, faerie forest, cursed ground; "automaton" → bound spirit, golem, guardian shade; "Anak raiders" → Saxon raiders; "Deep" → mine, barrow-complex, underground Roman workings.*

## Types of Site

### Arthurian Britain Ruin Types (d12)

| d12 | Ruin Type |
|-----|-----------|
| 1 | A city once inhabited by not-quite-humans — giants, fair folk, or pre-Celtic peoples |
| 2 | A Roman structure whose purpose is no longer clear — bath-house, amphitheatre, granary |
| 3 | A spot that was once a sacred site of the old religion |
| 4 | A vast monument complex — stone circle, cursus, long barrow — dedicated to a vanished people |
| 5 | Fortress raised by a dead warlord, failed king, or militant faith |
| 6 | A sorcerer's tower or hermit's compound once or presently inhabited by a powerful figure |
| 7 | Roll twice on 1d6 above; the first result was built on top of or in the ruins of the second |
| 8 | Roll again, but now inhabited by bandits, exiles, Saxon refugees, or other interlopers |
| 9 | A Roman road-fort whose garrison has been replaced by something worse |
| 10 | A barrow complex — multiple burial chambers, several generations of the honoured dead |
| 11 | A working or partly-working Roman relic — mill, aqueduct, dock — now badly decayed |
| 12 | A former estate or villa, gutted and half-collapsed, but recognisable |

## Ruin Tag Entries

Each tag lists Enemies (E), Friends (F), Complications (C), Things (T), and Places (P).

### Ancient Archives
The site once housed records — carved stones, written parchments, wax tablets — that would be very interesting to certain modern powers. The archives may be sealed behind a guardian or a puzzle based on now-forgotten procedures.

**E** Maddened archivist, Powerful figure who wants the information kept hidden, Secret-seeker who brooks no rivals

**F** Helpful guardian attendant (perhaps a bound spirit), Hired investigator, Seeker of related lore

**C** The information is conveyed in idioms or scripts that are no longer clear; The information is dangerous to know; The information is buried in vast amounts of irrelevant data

**T** Index to the desired information, Key to decoding the text, Cultural work that elucidates the script or medium

**P** Chamber full of clay tablets or carved stone panels, Scriptorium with crumbling parchment, Room decorated with symbols related to the archive's subject

---

### Aspiring Conqueror
Some power within the ruin has ambitions of conquest and plans to use the site as a base for dominating the surrounding lands. Such a warlord might be wholly reliant on the ruin's defenders, or could be recruiting outcasts and renegades.

**E** Inhuman warlord, Fanatical cult leader, Embittered outlaw-noble

**F** Hapless local village headman, Minion who reconsidered their allegiance, Former leader pushed aside by the new one

**C** Some of the locals honestly think the conqueror would be a better ruler; The conqueror has a legitimate dynastic claim; The conqueror is backed by enemies of the local king

**T** Cache of military supplies, Plunder taken from first victims, An important hostage in their keeping

**P** Barracks whipped into order, Training ground in use, A nearby village they have crushed

---

### Bound Servants
The ruin is still staffed by magical servants — bound spirits, guardian shades, animating enchantments, or ancient constructs following their last orders. They may be causing problems if their tasks can no longer be completed. Some may have re-interpreted their role into something they can do, whether or not they should.

**E** Cruel and immortal enchanter, Bound-servant leader gone mad, Faerie lord seeking to suborn the servants to dark purpose

**F** Helpful bound servant with a shred of genuine will, Fascinated outside investigator, Local who fears the servants

**C** Destroyed servants are worth a great deal as relics; The servants are needed to maintain the ruin's physical stability; The servants have been altered or merged with some outside power

**T** Command token for the servants, Cache of valuables they were guarding, Item they desperately need to fulfil their function

**P** Servant maintenance chamber, A row of inert figures waiting to be awakened, A place of endless servant toil

---

### Birthing Cyst
Something awful is growing in the ruin and will eventually erupt into catastrophic peril. The ruin may have been designed to enable this, or it could have been perverted by some outside power. Outsiders may not recognise the nature of the peril, or even mistake its growth for something beneficial.

**E** A beast to be born for a long-dead faith, Obsessed ancient keeper, Faerie lord determined to provoke the catastrophe for their own purposes

**F** Worried local observer, Last surviving keeper of the ruin, Survivor of an early eruption

**C** The thing's growth produces a profitable byproduct; People are completely mistaken about what is growing down there; People think it already hatched and was dealt with

**T** Item needed to harm or kill the thing, Key to halt or abort the process, Precious offerings made to the unborn disaster by fearful locals

**P** Uncanny chamber where it is growing, Fortified rooms to guard it, Shrine depicting its eventual glory

---

### Bitter Remnants
The ruin is not entirely abandoned — a remnant of its former inhabitants still occupies it. These survivors are almost certainly hostile toward outsiders and intruders who have tried to loot the place for uncounted generations.

**E** Xenophobic remnant chief, Outside lord determined to exterminate them, A powerful ruin rival seeking their destruction

**F** Open-minded remnant member, Eager scholar seeking their secrets, Escapee from the remnants' cruelties

**C** The remnant society is genuinely and completely horrible; The remnants no longer think like humans do; Certain outcasts have trade ties with the remnants

**T** Precious ancient relic they preserved, Loot taken from dead intruders, Secret history of the ruin

**P** Ancient but meticulously-kept chamber, Ancestral shrine unique to them, Maintained monument to past glory

---

### Civil War
At least two organised factions within the ruin are at war with each other. All have reasons that make leaving unappealing, and some are eager to enlist outside help against rivals. Given the low population of most ruins, the war is one of raids, ambushes, and opportunistic murders.

**E** Faction leader with dreams of conquest, Crazed warlord, Treacherous plotter who betrays hired allies

**F** Faction leader with benevolent aims, Innocent local caught in the crossfire, Would-be peacemaker

**C** They both want the same thing but in different ways; Both sides will unite swiftly against outside intruders; Neither side wants anything outsiders will like

**T** The relic or property they are fighting over, Weapon to destroy their rivals, Wealth to bribe new helpers

**P** Site of a vicious recent ambush, Defaced symbol or monument of a rival faction, Dangerous no-man's-land within the ruin

---

### Cyclical Doom
There is a phenomenon in the ruin that makes it tremendously dangerous at certain intervals — a seasonal curse, an awakening of preserved guardians, a cyclically-active trap from the builders' time. The natives may or may not know the cycle, and if it is a very long one they may not have been around to see it happen.

**E** Faerie lord determined to trigger the cycle, Native leader who wants to weaponise it, Reckless outside researcher with no care for consequences

**F** Native aware of the impending disaster, Researcher trying to stop the cycle, Survivor of the last cycle

**C** The cycle leaves behind a valuable byproduct; The cycle only threatens certain occupants; Outsiders have completely misinterpreted the cycle's meaning

**T** Key to trigger or halt the cycle, Device to protect users from the cycle, Object for controlling and directing the cycle

**P** Place scarred by a past cycle's effects, Control or observation chamber, Monument obscurely referencing the cycle

---

### Decrepit Structure
The ruin is falling apart and actively dangerous to its inhabitants. Navigating between areas may require careful progress, rope-work, or avoiding obviously dangerous routes. Some areas in the ruin may provoke a general collapse if significantly damaged.

**E** Outside plunderer with no care for consequences, Enemy actively trying to destroy the place, Berserk native trying to make ill-advised repairs

**F** Refugee forced to live there, Native dweller seeking help to fix things, Architectural scholar of old Roman or pre-Roman building

**C** Valuable materials can be looted by those indifferent to increasing instability; The collapse would reveal or unleash a terrible thing; Many places can only be visited once

**T** Resources that can repair the damage, Precious loot that will destabilise the place if taken, Treasure revealed by a structural collapse

**P** Creaking wooden gallery or stone bridge, Room with holes in the floor or fallen sections, Tower leaning at a drunken angle

---

### Desperate Hunger
Natives of the ruin cannot get enough to eat, and their situation makes it impractical to leave. Beasts may be extremely aggressive due to hunger; intelligent natives may fight more for food than for gold or glory. The most desperate may have fallen back on cannibalism or raiding nearby settlements.

**E** Obese cannibal chieftain, Native leader who will do anything to feed their people, Sorcerer who gives dark nourishment to their servitors

**F** Starved urchin-native, Anxious group leader wanting to cut a deal, Innovator trying to open a new food source

**C** The overpopulation is being resolved by murder; The food-gathering areas were recently blocked off by something; Foodstuffs become toxic rapidly within the ruin

**T** Cache of preserved food, Key to open new hunting areas, Great treasure that looks trifling compared to the worth of food

**P** Pit of gnawed bones, Cages holding unfortunate prey, Viciously desperate feeding area

---

### Dire Tombs
The ruin is characterised by a great many tombs or burial sites and a matching profusion of the restless dead. The revenants may be mindless husks animated by dark magic, or intentionally created to act as guards, or the anguished dead still living according to some lost death-god's teachings.

**E** Revenant lord jealous of their solitude, Ravening undead beast, Necromancer eager for raw materials

**F** Descendant of the dead trying to keep them safe, Fascinated historical researcher, Undead-hunter trying to contain them

**C** The undead are not all of the same mind or allegiance; The locals revere and venerate them as ancestors; The undead are merely symptoms of something worse entombed there

**T** Burial goods left behind, Plunder taken from unfortunate adventurers, A once-commonplace good now extremely valuable

**P** Halls of silent coffin-niches or stone urns, Chapel to a funerary god, Splendid tomb to a dead hero or king

---

### Distant Gate
The ruin is connected to some interesting distant location through a magical gate, a Roman underground road, a faerie ring, or a hidden passage. The natives may exploit this quality, or the gate may be sealed until a specific procedure or object activates it.

**E** Guardian of the gate, Hostile entity from the other side, Intruder determined to unlock the gate

**F** Hapless traveller who stumbled through from the other side, Native gate-guide, Explorer seeking a route to the gate's destination

**C** The gate was sealed for a very good reason; The gate is one-way; Activating the gate risks destroying the ruin

**T** Key to activate the gate, Codes or passes to control its destination, Treasure from the far side of it

**P** Mysterious transit-chamber with symbolism related to the destination, Room with objects or remains from the destination, Dangerously energetic gate room

---

### Dungeon Heart
The ruin's physical integrity is bound with a particular creature or object within the site. If it is destroyed or removed the place will collapse. This may derive from an ancient magical curse, a sympathetic unity of magical power, or ancient self-destruct mechanisms built by paranoid builders.

**E** Faerie lord determined to kill or steal the heart, A heart-creature that would be satisfying to kill, Native holding the heart hostage to force obedience

**F** Guardian of the heart, Native eager to warn outsiders of the truth, Explorer with dire suspicions

**C** The destruction is metaphorical or social in nature; Only a specific part of the ruin will be destroyed; No one involved realises it will cause a slow-motion destruction

**T** Device that will undo the link, The precious object that is the heart, Relic to control the linked things

**P** Chamber where the heart's connection is visible, Place that shifts in sympathy to the heart, Damaged room reflecting damage to the heart

---

### Experimental Lair
A sorcerer or ancient artificer once used this place for experiments that would never be tolerated in civilisation. They may have been sponsored by some amoral power, or independent practitioners seeking the special resources or isolation of this place.

**E** Still-surviving researcher, Hideous creation of the sorcerer, Outsider bent on seizing all its dark lore

**F** Sympathetic creation of the sorcerer seeking freedom, Witch-finder bent on destroying the secrets, Local plagued by the lair's emanations

**C** The lab is still in use; The lab's patrons do not realise what is really going on there; The lab's creations have gone out of control

**T** Valuable research byproduct, Treasure once owned by a research victim, Potent magical lore related to the lab's focus

**P** Testing chamber for the lab's research, Occult and sinister laboratory, Pens or cages for holding research subjects

---

### Failed Intrusion
The ruin recently experienced a serious incursion — adventurers, Saxon raiders, government forces, or angry villagers — that was repulsed, perhaps with significant loss of native life. Important native leaders may have been killed, treasures looted, or hostages taken.

**E** Desperate intruder leader still hiding in the ruin, Bloodthirsty native leader craving vengeance, Dead leader's heir full of terrible ideas

**F** Sympathetic intruder or native survivor, Escaped slave or hostage, Vengeful relative of the dead

**C** The intruders are too desperate to have entirely given up; The two sides basically ruined each other; New outsiders have entered to take advantage of the chaos

**T** Useful relic lost by the intruding forces, Stolen treasure of the natives, Tribal treasure now inaccessible due to the chief's death

**P** Site of a hideous battle, Larder where dead intruders are being kept, Local beast lair full of dragged corpses

---

### Fallen Sanctuary
The ruin was a place of security within recent memory, until some event or invasion turned it into its present state. Some surrounding locals may remember it from its heyday and know interesting facts about it. Others nurse dreams of restoring it.

**E** Dark warlord who overthrew the place, Traitor who arranged its downfall, Terrible creature unleashed in the site's dying throes

**F** Idealistic would-be reconstructor, Native trying to make peace with the occupiers, Aged keeper of the site's old secrets

**C** The site is fallen to the locals but is a sanctuary to a completely different group; The site is still dangerous and hostile to the interlopers; The locals desperately need to retake the site soon for some pressing reason

**T** Treasures hidden by the former owners, The dark instrument used to cast the site down, Token of rightful rule seized by the invaders

**P** Hidden chamber unknown to the invaders, Defaced and ruined room dedicated to the site's original role, Chamber re-purposed for the invaders' use

---

### False Front
The site seems to be a completely different type of place than it truly is. Either it was built this way originally or more recent owners have concealed the truth about it. Others may not realise that what they think they know is just a facade over something deeper.

**E** Secret master of the hidden ruin, Cruel schemer who established the false front, Monstrous foe still buried below the facade

**F** Clueless settler who means to exploit the false site, Explorer with curious references to the truth, Victim of something from below

**C** The false front is a lure to bait prey; The false front is to deflect interest; The false front is meant to be profitable to the true lord of the place

**T** Key to reveal the entrance to the real site, Incongruous treasure from below, Valuable goods used to maintain the facade

**P** Chamber that does not fit with the false front, Secret passage to the depths, Room full of costumes or props for the masquerade

---

### Feral Magic
Some potent working or ancient enchantment has gone berserk in the ruin, tainting the occupants and making the place dangerous. Whatever the magic once did, it now does it too much, in the wrong way, or at a grim cost. The natives cannot escape, have nowhere better to go, or are dependent on the twisted magic.

**E** Magically-warped beast, Native chieftain full of tainted power, Reckless sorcerer making use of the magic

**F** Researcher trying to understand or fix things, Sympathetic magic-plagued native, Outside victim of the site's magic

**C** Valuable loot awaits those willing to break the magic further; The magic can be fixed if something dangerous is done; The decay is spreading outside the ruin

**T** Item to ward off the magic, Valuable sorcerous byproduct, Ancient instructions on how to fix things

**P** Thrumming power-centre of the magic, Lair of magic-twisted beasts, Native shrine to the power

---

### Flooded Halls
The ruin is largely flooded. Exploring it is difficult and dangerous. The natives are either creatures adapted to water or desperate enough to live where few others can pursue them.

**E** Fish-thing with dark hungers, Cult priest of a water-spirit, Chief of an inhuman water-folk

**F** Luckless local boatman, Hermit who has tried to live there, Courageous treasure-diver

**C** The flooding is cyclical; Water flow can be controlled somehow from inside the site; The water is fetid with disease

**T** Pearls or other water-fashioned valuables, Extremely precious relic that would be ruined by soaking, Device to grant water-breathing

**P** Splendid chamber now crusted by slime, Half-filled room with an air pocket, Space full of totally opaque silty water

---

### Freshly Looted
The site has been recently plundered. Many creatures are dead or driven off, and rewards for exploration are limited. But as the site is newly-emptied, numerous outside groups or warbands are moving in, cleaning out the remnants and setting up their own bases.

**E** Conquering Saxon warchief, Ruthless bandit-adventurer who likes the place, Vengeance-mad chief of a rabble of survivors

**F** Sympathetic native driven out of the site, Frustrated adventurer denied a chance to loot, Local official charged with investigating the place

**C** The looters missed the most important treasure; A native faction was working with the looters; A group of natives successfully hid from the looters

**T** Treasure hidden by dead natives, Relic lost by a slain looter, Valuable thing too big and heavy to carry out readily

**P** Empty treasure vault, Ruler's lair plundered and defaced, Hall choked with the recent dead

---

### Friendly Foes
A group dwells here that would normally be very hostile to humans or outsiders, yet is perfectly willing to deal with adventurers. They may be an anomalous group of their kind, or have desperate need for outside help, or have religious reasons to be cooperative. The GM should give them the chance to be visibly friendly.

**E** Bloodthirsty adventurer who does not believe them, Group leader with treacherous plans, Monstrous foe that threatens the group and outsiders both

**F** Friendly being from the group, Local human with secret ties to them, Earnest outside missionary

**C** They are friendly because of a very large misunderstanding; They really are completely sincere in their good intent; They have a problem that only long-term allies can solve

**T** Group-made product normally never available to humans, Treasure gathered from foes, Relic they are using as a bargaining tool

**P** Meeting space furnished for human guests, Separate dwelling area for outsiders, Chamber for obscure group rituals

---

### Hidden Within
The ruin is actually underneath a seemingly ordinary structure or inhabited settlement. Secret passages lead down into it, and only a short distance from ordinary human life a ruin seethes with sinister secrets.

**E** Thing that creeps up from below at night, Long-buried evil, Reckless adventurer stirring up things beneath

**F** Passage-wise urchin or local child, Architect with too much curiosity, Hapless local well-digger or cellar-man

**C** The structure's inhabitants have a secret deal with the ruin-dwellers; The ruin-dwellers masquerade as the structure's inhabitants; A time-delayed catastrophe will happen once the ruin is opened

**T** Key to open the hidden passage below, Treasure hidden in the ruin in ages past, Ancient relic the ruin was made to contain

**P** Passage hidden behind crumbling construction, Mundane room above a monstrous evil, Secret passages with peepholes into the structure above

### Invincible Delusion
The natives of this ruin are totally convinced of something that is simply not the case — their ancient lord never fell, their god has commanded them to live in a particular troublesome way, they are the only true Britons left in the world. They have developed rationalisations to explain obvious contradictions, often involving hostile responses to bearers of iniquitous falsehood.

**E** Hypnotically charismatic leader, Crazed ancient revenant-ruler, Leader who knows the truth but profits from the error

**F** Native with suspicions, Outsider trying to convince them, Outsider whom they have mistakenly incorporated into their error

**C** The delusion makes an otherwise unendurable life bearable; The delusion is true in some critical ways; The delusion is somehow infectious

**T** Proof of the delusion's falsehood, Precious goods piled up for a lost cause, Relic of a golden age

**P** Ruin chamber made a pathetic mimicry of some greater place, Ritual chamber to celebrate the truth, Archive containing troublesome records

---

### Lethal Treasure
The ruin has or produces a very valuable good, but that good is extremely dangerous to acquire or possess. It may be cursed, or the acquisition process may be reliably lethal. This danger may result from the creatures who create it, the guardians that watch over it, or locals who kill those they deem as profaners.

**E** Monstrous beast that produces the good, Ancient bound guardian, Cruel tyrant who forces victims to acquire the good

**F** Desperate young adventurer, Native good-maker trying to avoid disaster, Victim of the good's hazards

**C** Extended exposure to the good is harmful; The good is useful but morally dubious in its production or nature; The good needs special processing to make it safe

**T** Large cache of the unrefined good, Object to nullify or cure the good's danger, Loot lost in an unsuccessful attempt to acquire the good

**P** Hellish processing or production area, Tightly-guarded vault of the good, Area made toxic by the good's effects

---

### Limited Access
The ruin can only be accessed at certain times or with certain special tokens. Natives are cut off from the outside world for long periods. The obstacle may be magical, or the only access route may be subject to natural cycles — seasonal flooding, a ford only passable in summer, a faerie threshold only open at certain times.

**E** Ruin tyrant who views all outsiders as enemies, Ruthless adventurer trying to block rivals, Mad power locked away in the ruin

**F** Eager young scholar, Adventurer with the key to the site, Native yearning to escape

**C** It has been so long since the last opening that everyone has forgotten about it; The access now opens in an inconvenient place; The access can be forced by a terrible sacrifice

**T** Key to open the access, Controls that permanently open the site, Loot from the last expedition to enter the site

**P** Passage that opens on a time-lock, Vast magical seal, Coastal portal revealed only during a particular low tide

---

### Lingering Curse
The ruin is cursed, and everything in it is tainted or troubled by the malediction. There is a strong reason for the natives to remain, or the curse itself induces them to stay. The curse may result from some ancient crime, a working gone wrong, a lost war against a magical foe, or a blessing in one age that is less appreciated now.

**E** Vile sorcerer feeding on the curse's energies, Embittered chieftain who hates all the uncursed, Hapless victim made a monster by the curse

**F** Native sorcerer trying to lift the curse, Outside researcher investigating it, Sympathetic native struggling with the curse's effects

**C** The curse grants a significant advantage as well as a blight; Those cursed need something here to survive; The curse is a mark of native belonging and is viewed as proof of loyalty

**T** Object that can lift the curse, Precious offering to the gods for mercy, Treasure left by a victim of the curse

**P** Shrine to some protective god, Curse-warped place of twisted mundanity, Chokingly-blighted place where the curse was first invoked

---

### Living Dungeon
The ruin is alive, either literally or metaphorically. It may be animated by an ancient spirit that looks through countless cracks and sensors, or infused with the genius of an arcane mind, or made up of tiny organism-colonies like some vast coral. Physical damage usually brings some hostile response, muted by age and disrepair, and the structure itself may be conscious enough to strike bargains.

**E** Demented ruin spirit, Sorcerer trying to bend the site to their will, Native chieftain who worships the ruin's genius as a god

**F** Friendly ruin sub-spirit, Native ruin-keeper, Explorer avid to learn of the place

**C** It is a colony creature with different minds in charge of different sections; The ruin is trying to grow at all costs; The ruin went berserk after its ruination and is now acting irrationally

**T** Poison that can kill the ruin, Precious good created by the ruin's life processes, Indigestible treasures left by prior intruders

**P** Wetly organic passageway, Chamber with a great maw for feeding, Room with tethered organic servitor-creatures

---

### Lost Colony
The ruin was once a colony site for a now-vanished power — Roman, pre-Roman, or faerie. Some pioneers may still survive within, in much-changed form, or their belongings may include objects or relics that are very valuable today. The modern heirs to this lost domain might still consider the ruin to be their lawful property.

**E** Degenerate colony leader, Outside conqueror seeking to seize the site, The hideous thing that destroyed the colony

**F** Confused surviving pioneer-heir, New colonist trying to revive the place, Historian looking for relics

**C** The colonists claimed to belong to the lost kingdom but were really of a more sinister group; Local powers quarrel over site access; Something extremely precious was lost here

**T** Once-commonplace item that is now precious, Ancient wealth of the colonists, Valuable goods they made before they perished

**P** Area decorated in their ancient style, Long-abandoned massacre site, Temple to a forgotten god

---

### Magical Wonder
An active relic or enchantment in the site produces some wondrous effect — permanent summer, healing waters, prophetic visions, endless food. Unfortunately it is so decayed and ill-tuned that all these wonders come with related serious negative effects, which the natives must struggle to deal with.

**E** Ancient half-mad keeper of the relic, Sorcerous monster born of the decay, Outsider determined to exploit the relic at any cost

**F** Adventurer who seeks the relic's benefits, Researcher who wants its secrets, Sorcerer convinced they can fix the relic

**C** The benefits conferred do not exist outside the ruin; The ruin will collapse disastrously if it is seriously damaged; Nearby communities benefit from it and try to keep out intruders

**T** Device to control the relic's benefits, Ruined parts that are still valuable, Research notes that would allow it to be repaired

**P** Chamber of dangerous magical fluxes, Sorcerous emission point where the blessing flows forth, Abandoned laboratory where it was made

---

### Monster Forge
The ruin somehow creates a particular type of beast by its simple existence — an old spawning-pit, a faerie-gate that belches forth creatures, an ancient agricultural complex with feral livestock, a working that produces monstrous life. These beasts probably infest the surrounding area.
