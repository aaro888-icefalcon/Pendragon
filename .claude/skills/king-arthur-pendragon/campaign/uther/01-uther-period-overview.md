# Uther Period Overview (480-495 AD)

Covers the reign of King Uther Pendragon of Logres from his coronation in 480 to the Infamous Feast in 495, the era that precedes the Great Pendragon Campaign's Anarchy Period.

**Use when:** Setting scene for new players, establishing era tone, explaining what makes this period mechanically distinct, or answering "what's different from classic Pendragon."

---

## The Era in Brief

Uther Pendragon is King of Logres — not High King of Britain. His primary drives: destroy the Saxons, obtain the High Kingship through the Supreme Collegium, and (fatally) possess Ygraine, Duchess of Cornwall. The period ends with the Infamous Feast of 495 in which Uther and most of Logres' nobility are poisoned. The Anarchy follows.

**Tone:** Military, brutal, morally gray. The idealism of Arthur's court does not exist yet. Might-makes-right balanced against ancient law and custom. Knights are a recent innovation; cavalry are the common pool from which knights are drawn.

---

## Political Situation

### Kingdom Structure
- **Logres** = Uther's direct kingdom (most of lowland England south of Yorkshire, excluding Cornwall's near-independence and Saxon-held territories)
- **Britain** = the whole island; Uther is NOT High King, merely King of Logres
- **High King** = elected by the Supreme Collegium (requires 3/4 majority of ~32 votes); Uther pursues this throughout the period
- Federated kingdoms beyond Logres: Cambria, Cumbria, Caledonia — all have their own kings; Uther must negotiate, threaten, or display Excalibur

### Uther's Power Base
- Controls Logres (his kingdom) directly through sheriffs, county courts, and royal demesne
- Excalibur (acquired 486) is a massive political tool — its display secures oaths and impresses foreign lords
- Has 14 votes from Logres vassals in the Supreme Collegium; needs 24 of 32 total for High Kingship
- By 490 has 22 votes; the vote is never formally held (Uther abandons it to chase Ygraine/Gorlois)

### Factions
| Faction | Leader(s) | Stance toward Uther |
|---------|-----------|---------------------|
| Logres Great Barons | Ulfius, Edaris, Gorlois, Eldol | Generally loyal; Gorlois breaks 490-491 |
| Saxons | Octa (Nohaut), Eosa (Deira), Aesc (Kent), Ælle (Suth Seaxe) | Hostile; major military threat throughout |
| Malahaut | Centurion King | Neutral to hostile; allies with Saxons by 493 |
| Cornwall | Gorlois (d. 491) | Loyal until 489-490; causes Uther's downfall |
| Northern Kings | Lot (Lothian), Nentres (Garloth), Uriens (Gorre) | Bought with marriages of Uther's stepdaughters (491) |
| Supreme Collegium | 32 Electors (kings + bishops) | Uther courts them actively 490; never holds vote |

## Key Differences from the Great Pendragon Campaign

### Knighthood Is New
- Knights are an innovation, not the default. Player characters begin as cavalry (mounted warriors), not yet formally knights.
- Knighthood = formal homage oath to a lord. All other loyalties are fealties (temporary).
- First several sessions are brutally lethal; recommended 2-3 starting characters per player. Survivors qualify for knighthood.
- No court of Arthur's ideals, no chivalric code as standard, no Round Table.

### Heraldry Is Early
- Heraldry exists but is new and unstandardized. Warlords' heraldry is given in the source but not universal.

### Savage Forest Does Not Exist Yet
- The magical forest appears in 495 (when Excalibur is not returned to the Faerie). Before 495, enchantment is confined to hidden places and forgotten sites.

### No Summerland as Separate Kingdom
- In the GPC, Summerland is independent. Here, King Cadwy has agreed to never attack Uther first in exchange for being left alone. He sends a small annual gift. Uther is agnostic in spiritual matters and accepts the deal.

### Gloucester (Glevum) Politics
- The famous feud between Clarence and Gloucester has not begun yet. Duke Eldol's rival heirs are maneuvering but have not yet divided his lands.

## Social Structure

### Feudal Hierarchy
King → Duke → Count/Earl → Baron → Knight → Squire → Cavalry

**Six Great Barons (Dukes and Counts):**
- Duke of Glevum (Eldol), Duke of the Marche (Edaris), Duke of the Vale/Silchester (Ulfius), Duke of the Saxon Shore (Lucius, minor until ~488), Duke of Lindsey (Corneus), Duke of Cornwall (Gorlois, d. 491)
- Count of Salisbury (Roderick, d. 495), Count/King of Summerland (Cadwy)

### Three Social Tiers
1. **Nobles** — knights and above; subject to feudal law, tried by peers
2. **Commoners** — free persons; frankpledge groups of 5-15 kin, subject to county/hundred courts
3. **Serfs/Villeins** — peasants; bound to manors; legal person of their lord answers for them

### Cities (Civitas)
Six old Roman cities with special charters: Londinium, Silchester, Corinium, Glevum, Durnovaria, Venta. They answer directly to the king. Senate governs each; owes the king upkeep days during the King's Progress.

## Economy

### King's Income Sources
| Source | Mechanism |
|--------|-----------|
| Assized Rent | Fixed rents from royal demesne and favored lords |
| Customary Revenue | Traditional dues from free holders (boon work, etc.) |
| Geld (Saxgeld) | Universal tax maintained by Uther from the Saxon wars |
| Amercements/Fines | Court penalties |
| Escheat | Crown seizes dead holder's property pending heir verification |
| Primer Seizin | One year's profits taken by crown from any inherited estate |
| Wardship | Crown manages (and profits from) underage heirs' estates |
| Royal Forests | Timber, venison, mining rights, assart fees |
| Natural Resources | Stone quarries (66-100% royal share), salt pans, iron, lead/silver (Bedegraine/Summerland — 100% after conquest) |
| Purveyance | Right to take any goods at appraised value |
| Pleas of the Crown | Special royal justice fees (high justice, wreck, treasure trove, deodand) |
| Market Aids | Taxes on cities and market towns beyond geld |
| King's Coffer | Direct personal payments with no accounting |

### Royal Perquisites (Pleas of the Crown)
- Treasure Trove (buried treasure found = 100% to king)
- Wreck (wrecked ships' cargo)
- Seizure of felon's goods
- Deodand (object causing death → sold for alms fund)
- High Justice (life-and-death sentences)
- Infangentheof (right to hang red-handed thief immediately)

## Legal Systems

### Three Overlapping Systems
1. **Ancient Law and Custom** — "The way things have always been." Oral tradition, unwritten. Courts enforce it even when seemingly unfair.
2. **Feudal Law** — Formal obligations of lords to king, standardized by Aurelius Ambrosius. Feudal incidents: Escheat, Primer Seizin, Wardship.
3. **Canon Law** — Roman Church's separate legal system, answered to the Pope, not the king.

### Courts
- **Hundred Court** — most local; sheriff's tourn visits twice yearly; handles common disputes, frankpledge, local crimes
- **County Court** — overseen by sheriff; monthly; felonies, royal writs, amercements
- **Court of Common Pleas** — Winchester; subject vs. subject, no royal interest
- **Court of the King's Bench** — travels with king (coram rege); crimes against king's law, treason, High Justice

### Seigniorial Rights (held by some barons)
- Sake and soke (local court authority), toll (road/market charges), team (vouching), thief-hanging (infangentheof/outfangentheof)

## Military

### Uther's Army
- **Household Knights** (~100 strong) — sworn to Uther personally by homage
- **Baronial Armies** — called up by feudal levy; each duke commands ~26-100 knights plus infantry
- **King's Guard** — elite household unit; Captain is Bellias (to 490), then Brastias (491+)
- **County Levies** — posse comitatus; sheriff musters these; mostly infantry
- **Royal Garrison** — permanent troops at key royal castles

### Book of Battle Summary
- Unit = eschille (5-20 mounted men) or conroi (20-50)
- Each battle is multiple rounds; each round = choose Charge, Hold, Withdraw, or Rally
- Commander's Battle skill matters; player knights roll individually but modifier from commander's success/failure
- Victory types: Decisive (rout), Major (hold field), Minor (stalemate), Defeat, Decisive Defeat

## Character Generation Notes (Uther Period)

- Starting characters are cavalry, not yet knights
- Expect high mortality in first years (480-483); prepare 2-3 characters
- First characters who survive to serve Uther or a Great Baron gain knighthood
- No Chivalry bonus from Arthur's court
- Glory awards follow standard Pendragon rules
- The player-knight home base is Salisbury (Count Roderick's domain) by default — close to royal court, active in all major events

## The Infamous Feast — End of the Period

**Year:** 495, after the Battle of St Albans (Uther's last victory against the Saxons)  
**Event:** At the victory feast, Uther and most of Logres' nobility are poisoned. The Saxon physician is executed, but guilt is disputed.  
**Aftermath:** "There are no lords in Logres." No heir (Arthur hidden by Merlin). No functioning Supreme Collegium. Saxon pressure continues. The Anarchy begins.  
**Suspects:** Saxon physician (official verdict), Morgan (motive: father Gorlois' death), Margawse, Centurion King, King Lot, King Syagrius, Merlin (greatest good argument), the Butler.
