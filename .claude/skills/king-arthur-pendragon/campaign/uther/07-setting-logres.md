# Setting: Logres — Geography, Counties, Courts, and Key Locations

Geography of Logres and surrounding lands, county-by-county breakdown, who holds what, key locations for the Uther Period, and the place-name translation guide.

**Use when:** Determining travel time and direction, answering "who controls this area," locating a castle or city, understanding the political geography of Britain in 480-495.

---

## Logres: Overview

**Logres** is both the name of the kingdom Uther rules and the geographic region of lowland Britain south of Malahaut's territory (roughly modern England, excluding the Saxon-held coasts and Cornwall's near-autonomy). The term is used interchangeably for the political kingdom and the region.

**Capital cities (rotating):** No permanent capital; the court moves with the king. Primary royal residences: the White Tower (Londinium), Castle of the Rock (Salisbury/Sarum), Silchester Castle.

**Population (480):** ~2 million in Logres; 541 hundreds.

**Territory lost during the period:**
- Caercolun area progressively compromised from 485
- Linden Pool (Lincoln) sacked and occupied by Saxons 494-495

## Counties of Logres

### Southern Counties

**Salisbury County** (modern Wiltshire and parts of Hampshire)  
- Count: Roderick (d. 495)
- Castle: Castle of the Rock (Sarum/Old Sarum)
- Key sites: Ambrius Abbey (Amesbury), Stonehenge (Giant's Dance), Cloverhill Lodge (Clarendon), Woodhouse Castle, DuPlain Castle, Vagon Castle, Llud's Hall (treasury and county castle)
- Key terrain: Salisbury Plain with bog iron deposits
- The player-knights' home territory; Count Roderick is their liege lord
- Sheriff: shares with Gentian county

**Summerland County** (modern Somerset, parts of Wiltshire and Gloucestershire)  
- Count/King: Cadwy (special agreement with Uther — see 01-overview)
- Key sites: Glastonbury Abbey (Avalon), Bath (sacred waters), Lake of Diana, Forest of Glamour, Wildcat Camp (Cadbury), Rapidstream Bridge (Bristol), Great Bridge Castle
- Resources: 30 lead mines at Charterhouse/Mendip Hills; largest silver mine in Britain; stone quarry at Boxtree; Axbridge salt pans
- Axe Abbey, Wells Abbey, Barrowvale

**Dorsette County** (modern Hampshire and Dorset)  
- Sheriff: Sheriff of Dorsette
- Key sites: Durnovaria (Dorchester, Roman civitas), Kingswood Lodge (royal hunting), Pass Castle (Corfe Castle), Bow and Arrow Castle (Portland), Bitternbill stone quarry (Purbeck), Stone Abbey of the Giant (Cerne Abbas), Black Abbot's Abbey (Abbotsbury), Charterhouse lead mines, Meadowstream (Wimborne), Spearshaft (Shaftesbury)
- Duke of the Marche holds Bow and Arrow Castle (3 days owed)

**Jagent County** (modern Dorset and Somerset)  
- Sheriff: Sheriff of Jagent
- Key sites: Jagent Castle (Ilchester), Brightstream Abbey (Sherbourne — home of Abbot John the Chancellor), King's Stag Lodge (hunting), Shining Stream Castle, Pinnacle Castle (Montacute), Longport (Langport)

**Ascalon County** (modern Devon, Somerset, and Dorset)  
- Sheriff: Not specified
- Key sites: Exe River (Exeter, Roman town), Red Mount Castle (Exeter again?), Heights Castle (Dunster), Outpost of the Sacred Grove
- Part of the greater southwest; links to Cornwall

**Tintagel County** (modern Cornwall and Devon)  
- Sheriff: Duke of Cornwall serves as his own Sheriff (Tintagel is a special case)
- Key sites: Tintagel Castle, Castle Terrabil (Bossiney), Fowey Moor, Loud Stream Castle (Lydford), Oaktown Castle (Okehampton), Lookout Headland (Totnes), Hurlers (stone circle)
- Resources: Tin mines (largest in Britain, second-largest at Lookout Hill)
- **Political status:** Near-independent under Gorlois (d. 491); absorbed into royal control 491-495

**Hantonne County** (modern Hampshire and Sussex)  
- Sheriff: Sheriff of Hantonne
- Key sites: Hantonne Castle (Southampton), Harborkeep (Portchester), White City Abbey (Winchester) — Abbot is an Elector of Supreme Collegium, Magouns Castle (Arundel — Duke of Lindsey holds), Noviomagus/Chichester (Bishop is Roman, elector), Abbey of the Isle (Carisbrooke), Camulos' Town (Carisbrooke), Wetwood (Netley), Devil's Jumps
- Royal: Castle of the White City (Winchester)

**Silchester County** (modern Hampshire, Surrey, and Berkshire)  
- Duke: Ulfius (Duke of the Vale)
- Sheriff: Sheriff of Silchester
- Key sites: Silchester Civitas (Roman city), Riverford Abbey (Reading), Castle Astolat (Guildford), Stoneheight Castle, Wynchbank Castle (Windsor — royal), Thornbush Castle (Farnham), Castle of the Woodland, Oldtown Lake, Forest of the Badger (Bagshot), Sand Dunes Castle (Send), Riverford Castle (Abbot of Riverford), Bright Skull Castle, Levcomagus (Andover)
- Silchester is the Duke of Vale's administrative seat AND a Roman civitas

**Berroc County** (modern Middlesex, Surrey, and Kent)  
- Sheriff: shares with Thamesmouth
- Key sites: Stoneskeep (Duke of Vale holds), Roe Deer Gate Castle (Reigate — Count Salisbury holds 0 days), Twinford Castle

**Rydychan County** (modern Oxford, Buckshire, Wiltshire)  
- Sheriff: Sheriff of Rydychan
- Key sites: Rydychan Castle (Wallingford), Oxenford Castle (Oxford — exempt from standard obligations), Hill Lodge (Brill), Middlestone Castle, Deertown-on-Thames, Streamfield/Streamtown

**Gentian County** (modern Wiltshire and Berkshire)  
- Sheriff: shares with Salisbury
- Key sites: Sparrowhawk Castle (Marlborough — the Sparrowhawk Abbey), Bladud's Mound Abbey (Malmesbury), Gentian Mound (Marlborough), Gofannon's Smithy (Wayland's Smithy), Twisting River (Bedwyn), Rivertown (Eaton), Gloom Forests (Selwood/Pewsham/Melksham woods), Blackriver (Calne)

### Central Counties

**Wuerensis County** (modern Warwickshire, Northamptonshire, Leicestershire, Oxfordshire)  
- Sheriff: shares with Lambor
- Key sites: Warcastle (Warwick), The Red Tower (Kenilworth — royal), St Cofa's Tree (Coventry), Slayersfort (Banbury), Horsemane Fort (Mancetter), Lonely Path, Blockstown (Bloxham), **Castle Dykes** (Farthingstone — where the Savage Forest appears in 495)

**Tribruit County** (modern Northamptonshire, Buckinghamshire, and Berkshire)  
- Sheriff: Sheriff of Tribruit
- Key sites: Tribruit Castle (Northampton), King's Cliffe Lodge (hunting — royal), Dangerous Ford Castle (Bedford), Rooksroost Castle (Rockingham), Sentinel Ridge Castle, Stonewall (Stanwick), Goat Town (Geddington), Crookstream (Woburn), Stag's Meadow (Buckingham)

**Clarence County** (modern Gloucestershire and Warwickshire)  
- Sheriff: shared with Glevum
- Key sites: Corinium Civitas (Cirencester — Roman city), Birchwood Castle (Duke of Glevum holds 0 days), Bridge on the Cam Castle (Duke of Vale holds), Longridge Castle (Deputy Marshal Brychan), Longvale, Hidden Vale (Winchcomb), Stone quarry at Streamtown

**Glevum County** (modern Gloucestershire)  
- Duke: Eldol
- Key sites: Glevum Civitas (Gloucester — Roman city), Glevum Abbey, Goodrich Castle (Duke of Glevum 0 days), Wolfhill Castle (Duke of Vale 0 days), St Martin's Abbey, Mariner's Monastery (Lydney)
- Both Clarence and Glevum county castles are at Corinium; shared sheriff

**Lambor County** (modern Leicestershire, Nottinghamshire, and Rutland)  
- Sheriff: shares with Wuerensis
- Key sites: Lambor Castle (Lutterworth), Leir's Castle (Leicester — Duke of Marche holds), Leirstown Abbey (an Elector), Beautiful View Castle (Belvoir), Stonetown Castle, Bran's Town (Branston), Crowland Abbey (Thorney)

**Bedegraine County** (modern Nottinghamshire and Derbyshire)  
- Status: Conquered 481; royal territory
- Key sites: Bedegraine Castle (Nottingham), Clipstone Lodge (royal hunting), Field of Doves Castle, Oakmeet mines (Matlock — silver and lead), Sacred Grove of Spring (Nemetostatio), Deer (Derby)
- All mines are 100% royal revenue

### Northern Counties

**Linden County** (modern Lincolnshire)  
- Duke: Corneus of Lindsey (d. 494 when Saxons sack Linden Pool)
- Key sites: Linden Pool Castle (Lincoln), Linden Pool Abbey (Elector), King's House Castle (royal), New Works Castle (Newark), Flax Spring (Flaxwell), Humberside (Humberston), St Ninnian's, Woodgrove Castle (Barrow-on-Humber), Bards Abbey (Bardney), Llud's Stream (Louth)
- **Falls to Saxons 494; Corneus disappears; county occupied**

**Lonazep County** (modern Lincolnshire)  
- Key sites: Lonazep Castle (Folkingham), St Peter's Castle (Duke of Lindsey), Stoney Ford Castle (Sleaford), Slippery Ford Castle (Stamford), Castle Brown/Castle on the Water (Bourne — Sir Galehaut the Brown holds)

**Lonazep becomes increasingly vulnerable after 490**; Saxon raids escalate

**Huntland County** (modern Cambridgeshire and Huntingdonshire)  
- Key sites: Huntsman Castle (Huntingdon), Huntsman Abbey, Grantabridge Castle (Cambridge), Horns (Horningsea), Eels (Ely Isle), Huntsman Hill (Ramsey)

**Caercolun County** (modern Norfolk and Suffolk)  
- Status: Compromised from 485; most of it in Saxon hands by 490
- Key sites (pre-485): Camulodunum (Colchester — Roman city and Bishop, Elector), Castle of Vigor (Framlingham), Fort Brutus (Walton — Duke Saxon Shore), Castle of Buna's Island (Bungay — Duke Saxon Shore), Hawthorn Castle, Monument Hill Castle (Duke of Vale), Clearstream (Clare), Deepwater Harbor (Dunwich), Guinnon (Bury St Edmunds), Wonderful Isle (Eye), Highplace Harbor (Ipswich), Southtown (Sudbury)
- **Marked † in King's Progress — only available before 485**

**Caerwent County** (modern Norfolk)  
- Status: Mixed; some areas hold, eastern coast exposed
- Key sites: Fort Bran (Brancaster — Duke Saxon Shore), Broadstream Fort, Old Fort, Buck Castle (Duke of Vale holds), Seafort (Caister — Duke Saxon Shore, 0 days), Crossing Castle (Baroness of the Crossing), Venta Civitas (Norwich — Roman city), Greatford Castle (royal), Clareia (Tunstead)
- **Some locations marked †**

**Hartland County** (modern Hertfordshire, Essex, and Berkshire)  
- Key sites: St Albans (Hertfordshire), St Albans Abbey (Elector, 12 days owed — largest obligation in King's Progress), Kingstown Castle (Count Salisbury holds), Birch Hill Castle, Chattering Glade Lodge (royal hunting), Pleached Hedge Castle (Duke of Vale), Bishop's Hold, Saffron Castle, Ashtree Spring, Birchfarm

**Thamesmouth County** (modern Essex and Kent)  
- Sheriff: shares with Berroc
- Key sites: Londinium (London — Roman city and bishopric), White Tower (royal, 8+ days), Westminster Abbey (4 days), Llud's Castle (county castle — Thamesmouth and Berroc), Ford of the Roe Deer Castle (Count Salisbury), Castle Aneirin (Eynsford), Oatfield Palace (Havering — royal), Southwark, Bran's Abbey (Bermondsey), St Martin's Abbey Londinium

## Beyond Logres

### Cambria (Wales)
Key kingdoms: Powys (Shrewsbury), Gomeret (Sinadon), Galvoie (Worcester area), Amans, Cameliard (Carohaise — King Leodegrance), Estregales (King Canan, assassinated 494), Deesmouth (Chester)
Supreme Collegium electors: Archbishop of Carlion, Kings of Amans/Cameliard/Powys/Gomeret/Galvoie, several Abbot-bishops
Duke of the Marche guards Logres' border against Cambrian raids

### Cumbria
Key kingdoms: Roestoc (Doncaster — King included in Collegium), Malahaut (York/Eburacum — Centurion King, hostile from 489, Saxon ally by 493)
Also: Rheged, Rouse, Lestroite (mountain lords visited by Uther's Progress 490)

### Caledonia
Key kingdoms: Lothian (King Lot; marries Margawse 491-492), Garloth (King Nentres; marries Elaine 491-492), Gorre (King Uriens; betrothed to Morgan)
Supreme Collegium electors: Kings of Garloth and Lothian, Abbot-bishops at Fort of the Britons and Lookout Headland

### Saxon Territories (Hostile)
- Suth Seaxe (Sussex): King Ælle; most aggressive raider; sacks Anderida 491
- Kent: King Aesc; participant at Battle of Salisbury 480
- East Saxons: King Æscwine (from 487)
- Nohaut: King Octa (imprisoned 490, escaped 493)
- Deira: King Eosa (imprisoned 490, escaped 493)
- Perdue Forest (south of London): Saxon-held, has 22 of Britain's 33 iron mines

### Lyonesse
Southern kingdom; Sir Meliodas' home; future birthplace of Tristram. Peripheral to Uther Period.
