## Legal Mechanics (Appendix D)

### Court Hierarchy
1. Hundred Court — local; sheriff's tourn (twice yearly); frankpledge, common disputes
2. County Court — monthly; royal writs, felonies, amercements
3. Court of Common Pleas (Winchester/"the Bench") — subject vs. subject; no royal interest; Chief Justice = Justiciar
4. Court of the King's Bench (travels with king; coram rege) — crimes against king's law; treason; High Justice

### Legal Process (Time Scale)
Each step takes approximately 6 months:
1. Complaint filed with "twelve men of good standing"; fee paid; plaintiff must be present
2. If accused not present → writ of attendance (sheriff sends)
3. If ignored → writ of attachment (arrest order)
4. If still ignored → writ of distraint (livestock and chattel seized)
5. If still ignored → posse comitatus called; knights of county summoned to seize person
6. Once arrested: accused may replevin (pay bail) unless irreplevisable
7. Compurgators assembled for both sides; sheriff weighs their worth; verdict reached; punishment immediate

### Punishments

**Commoners:**
| Crime | Punishment |
|-------|-----------|
| Petty theft | Fine + return of property |
| Theft | Hand cut off |
| Poaching | Ears cut off |
| Murder | Strangled and burnt |
| Ignoring public duty | Stocks (public humiliation) |
| Heresy (Roman Church) | Burned alive |
| Repeat offenders | Non-lethal public torture |
| Breaking and entering | Hanging |
| Insult to a lord's power | Cage display |
| Treason | Hanged, drawn, and quartered; body parts displayed on city walls |

**Nobles (levels from least to most):**
1. Summary dismissal
2. Token amercement (fine)
3. Significant fine
4. Escheatment (crown takes land)
5. Loss of title
6. Debasement
7. Death

**Ordeal by Fire:** Red-hot iron held; hand examined after 1 week. Uninfected/unburned = innocent.  
**Ordeal by Water (Dunking):** Tied in sack, thrown in water. Drowning = guilty.

### Distraint Procedure
1. Sheriff seizes livestock (sparing plow teams if possible)
2. If livestock insufficient: chattel seized
3. If chattel insufficient: escheatment proceedings begun
4. Owner can pay fine to recover distrained animals (plus daily care fees)
5. All distrained goods go to king if party found guilty

## Church Structure

### British Church
- Pelagian theology (salvation through free will; not predestination)
- Abbey-based rather than diocese-based
- Two main orders: Russet Monks (uncolored habits; more austere) and Black Monks (black habits; more worldly)
- Led by Archbishop of Britain (Dubricus, based at Carlion-on-Usk)
- Fighting clergy: some abbots maintain military forces
- Also: Indigo Monks, Curtal Monks, regular canons, anchorites

### Roman Church
- Hierarchical; Pope-headed; Canon Law
- Diocese-based with bishops answering to Rome
- Bishops in Logres are Roman appointments, not Uther's
- They can and do defy the king; they answer to a foreign power
- Also: White Monks (Cistercians), Black Canons, Donatist Church (heretical by Roman standards), Arian Church (German faith, present in Saxon territories)

### Faerie/Pagan Elements
- Ladies of the Lake: faerie women connected to water; advisors; holders of magic items (including Excalibur's source)
- New Druids: Merlin leads this loosely defined group
- Sacred Groves: pre-Roman worship sites (Nemetostatio = Sacred Grove of Spring, Bedegraine)
- Pagan sacred sites are mapped; they are minorities in the kingdom but present

## New Skills (Appendix J)

### Clerk
**Starting Skill (Knights): 0**  
Covers writing, reading, arithmetic, filing records, filling out forms, proper forms of correspondence, judicial forms, and accounting. Required for court officers' administrative functions. Knights do not typically start with this skill but can develop it.

### Diplomacy
**Starting Skill (Knights): 0**  
Discourse between lords and kings (or their representatives). Requires knowledge of: customs of the other party, personalities involved, language (subtlety and bluntness as needed), current events and how they affect the discussion, appropriate gifts (timing, type, amount). Used in any formal negotiation with a peer or superior.

**Note:** Both skills are listed as generally held only by monks. Knights never start with these skills but NPCs like Sir Argan (Diplomacy 17) demonstrate knights can develop them.

## The King's Ire: Formal Stages

| Stage | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| 1 (Verbal rebuke) | Public humiliation; no mechanical effect but social cost |
| 2 (Token amercement) | Small fine; recorded |
| 3 (Significant fine) | Meaningful financial penalty |
| 4 (Disseizin) | Temporarily loses specific lands; crown collects their revenue |
| 5 (Escheatment) | Permanently loses lands; crown takes them |
| 6 (Loss of title) | Noble status revoked |
| 7 (Debasement) | Degraded to commoner status (culvertage) |
| 8 (Death) | Execution; requires king's formal approval (or infangentheof exception) |

**Reversing ire:** Requires the King's Mercy. Trigger: major service, powerful advocate (one of Uther's favorites), dramatic sacrifice, or Merlin's intervention.

**Destroying chattels:** The king can order property destroyed (not just seized) as punishment.  
**Licensed jacquerie:** The king can authorize peasants to revolt against a specific lord — a terrifying punishment reserved for the greatest enemies.  
**Seizing person:** The king can imprison anyone at any stage.

## Feudal Incidents

| Incident | Trigger | Effect |
|----------|---------|--------|
| Escheat | Any property holder dies | Crown seizes all land/goods; investigates for proper heir; inventories; calculates additional service owed. Even with living heirs, may take years. Crown collects all revenues in interim. |
| Primer Seizin | Inheritance by adult heir | Crown takes one full year's profits from the lands before transferring them to the heir. |
| Wardship | Heir is a minor | Crown manages (and profits from) the estate until heir reaches majority. Can be very profitable for the crown; devastating for the family. |

| King's Eyre | The itinerant royal court; "eyre" = "to journey" |
| Market Aids | Taxes levied on settlements and cities beyond geld |
| Outfangentheof | Right to pursue thief beyond one's borders |
| Pillory and Tumbrel | Right to punish minor offenses publicly |
| Plevin | Bail payment; "to replevin" = to pay bail |
| Plowland | Land one plow/team can work in a year + support land; ~120 acres average; "hide" for taxation |
| Posse Comitatus | Military array of the county; the levy |
| Primer Seizin | Inheritance tax; crown takes one year's profits when heir inherits |
| Purveyance | Royal right to take any goods at appraised value, leaving a receipt |
| Royal Forest | Land reserved by king for personal use; subject to Forest Law |
| Royal Ire | King's right to express anger and create consequences without justification |
| See | A cathedral city; seat of a bishop |
| Sheriff | County reeve; oversees all king's business in an area; administers county courts |
| Slight | To render a castle indefensible |
| Soldeier | Professional infantryman |
| Tourn | Moveable sheriff's court visiting each hundred twice annually |
| Value | Agreed-upon tax value of an area, expressed in libra (£) |
| Virgate | One-quarter of a plowland; supports one peasant family for a year |
| Warren | Falconry rights |
| Writ | Statement from the king constituting immediate, inviolable law |
