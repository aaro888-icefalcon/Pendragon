# Uther's Court — Structure, Officers, and Mechanics

Covers the structure of King Uther's royal court: permanent officers and their roles, the King's Progress (itinerant court), Easter/Christmas court events, court scenario mechanics, and court intrigue rules.

**Use when:** Players arrive at court, need to find an officer, want to pursue court intrigue, are seeking appointments, or the GM needs to run a court scene.

---

## Court Structure Overview

Uther's court is the Curia Regis — it travels with the king as the King's Eyre and settles at major locations. The permanent inner circle is called the Privy Council. All decisions of state ultimately rest with Uther, who is suspicious, lustful, and proud.

### Privy Council (Inner Circle)
- King Uther Pendragon
- Merlin (when present — increasingly absent after 491)
- Sir Ulfius (Marshal/Duke Vale)
- Sir Madoc ap Uther (Prince, until d. 491)
- Sir Bellias → Sir Brastias (Captain of the Guard)
- Abbot John (Chancellor)
- Sir Goreau → Sir Geraint (Seneschal)

## Office Descriptions

### Chancellor (Abbot John of Brightstream)
Controls all written correspondence, writs, and royal administration. Issues writs in the king's name. Corrupt — will forge documents for a price. Intrigue 17, Law 18. British Black Monk. One of Uther's favorites.
- **GM use:** All formal legal actions, writs of summons, royal grants, and pardons go through him. He can be bribed to delay, alter, or produce documents.

### Chamberlain (Brother Hugh, d. 494)
Controls access to the king's private chambers; manages the household staff and royal sleeping arrangements. Honest 18 but Lustful 18. Courtesy 18. British Black Monk. One of Uther's favorites. Dies 494.
- **GM use:** If you need to meet the king privately, you go through Hugh. He keeps court gossip.

### Marshal of Logres (Sir Ulfius, Duke of the Vale)
Commands Uther's armies in the field. Plans strategy and troop movements. Manages military logistics. Battle 19, Sword 20, Intrigue 18. One of Uther's favorites.
- **GM use:** Military assignments, battle plans, army composition, and complaints about soldiers all go through Ulfius.

### Seneschal of Logres
Sir Goreau (to 491, d. at Battle of Terrabil) → Sir Geraint (491-495, d. Infamous Feast). Manages the royal household estates, feasts, and daily logistics of the court. Stewardship 20/19.
- **GM use:** Court feasts, lodging at royal manors, provisioning, estate disputes.

### Dapifer and Herald (Sir Sadinal, d. 495 Infamous Feast)
Manages the royal table seating, introduces visitors, and oversees formal court ceremonies. APP 17, Heraldry 19, Courtesy 17, Stewardship 20.
- **GM use:** Formal introductions, seating rank disputes, ceremony protocol.

### Captain of the Guard
Sir Bellias (to 490, d. 490) → Sir Brastias (joins 491). Commands the King's Guard — Uther's personal bodyguard unit. Brastias is the more famous of the two; becomes Captain of Cornwall's bodyguard later.
- **GM use:** Security at court, investigations into threats against the king, guard duty assignments.

### Constable of Logres
Sir Eliezier the Strong (to 490) → Sir Argan ap Maelgad (490-493). Manages day-to-day law enforcement in Logres; oversees county constables. Note: Argan defeats Uther in trial by combat (493) and builds Uther's Shame castle; effectively ceases functioning as Constable afterward.
- **GM use:** Law enforcement, prisoner management, riot suppression.

### Forest Justiciar (Sir Do de Carduel)
Oversees the Royal Forests and enforcement of Forest Law. Cruel 17, Just 20. Reports forest violations, manages foresters.
- **GM use:** Poaching cases, forest access disputes, assart applications, outlaw hunts.

### Cofferer (Abner, d. 495 Infamous Feast)
Manages the King's Coffer (personal unaccountable funds). Energetic 18, Loyalty Uther 40, Clerk 17, Intrigue 16. One of the few who knows how much the king actually spends on his favorites and private matters.

### Treasurer (Brother Syrion)
Manages royal revenues from ferms, geld, and court fines. Honest 18, Hate Tax Cheats 16, Clerk 20. Based at Llud's Hall (Salisbury); the royal treasury is there.
- **GM use:** Sheriffs report to Syrion. Tax disputes, treasury access, crown debt.

## The King's Eyre (Itinerant Court)

The court travels constantly. Everywhere Uther stops, he holds court — receiving ambassadors, judging local cases, communicating with regional lords, collecting upkeep from his hosts.

### Who Owes Upkeep
| Type | Obligation |
|------|-----------|
| Baronial | Lords owe N days of feeding the king per Table G |
| County castle | Sheriff owes upkeep from county revenues |
| Hunting Lodge | Royal demesne — king pays himself |
| Bishopric/Elector | Bishops owe upkeep as part of Collegium duty |
| Civitas | Cities owe upkeep; Senate manages it |
| Abbey | Abbeys owe upkeep as landed barons |

Total days owed by all vassals: 394. King collects upkeep outside his own holdings for 252 days per year. The remaining ~113 days he feeds himself from royal demesne.

### Movement Rate
The king's baggage train moves ~10 miles per day. Gamemasters can chart his progress by picking any location within 10 miles of his current position.

### Locating the King (Random)
Roll 1d20 twice. First roll selects a county group (see Table G in `08-uther-reference.md`). Second roll selects the specific location, type, fief holder, and days owed.

## Court Calendar

### Easter Court
The major annual court. All Great Barons and important lords are expected to attend. Failure to attend without paying essoins (a fine/excuse payment) risks royal displeasure.
- Court location rotates (see 05-timeline for each year's location)
- Major announcements, knightings, marriages, and legal judgments are made here
- The most important court for player-knight advancement opportunities

### Christmas Court
Also major. Tends to be at a fixed royal residence (the White Tower in Londinium is common). Baronial obligations include attendance.
- Winter planning for spring campaigns
- Gift-giving season; the king's favor shown by gifts
- Key marriages and betrothals announced here

### Whitsun Court
Smaller court, less mandatory. Often held at a hunting lodge or baronial castle.

## Court Intrigue Mechanics

### The Court Scenario System
Tables A-J provide random generation for court events. The core framework:

**Table A: Who Has a Problem?** (Roll to determine which NPC or faction is at the center of a scenario)  
**Table B: What Kind of Problem?** (Legal dispute, military crisis, love affair, religious conflict, etc.)  
**Table C: Who Is Involved?** (Additional NPCs entangled in the problem)

### Key Mechanics
- **Intrigue skill** governs reading political situations, gathering gossip, and manipulating court relationships
- **Courtesy** is required for formal interactions; failure risks insult to the court
- **Orate** for formal speeches and public arguments
- **Recognize** to identify faces in a crowd (useful for identifying disguised NPCs or spies)
- **Heraldry** to identify coats of arms and determine rank/affiliation

### The Rumor Mill
- Once per court session, a player may roll Intrigue to learn one court rumor
- Critical success = GM reveals a significant secret (not necessarily accurate)
- Failure = player learns a false rumor
- Fumble = player is caught eavesdropping; Intrigue roll by target NPC to determine consequences

### Essoins (Absence Payments)
- Nobles who skip court without the king's leave owe essoins: a fee for the right not to attend
- Repeated absence = royal ire
- Gorlois' departure without leave (490) is the precipitating cause of his being declared a rebel (491)

### Royal Ire and Mercy
**The King's Ire** escalates through stages:
1. Verbal rebuke (public humiliation)
2. Token amercement (fine)
3. Significant fine
4. Disseizin (losing land temporarily)
5. Escheatment (losing land permanently)
6. Loss of title
7. Debasement
8. Death

**The King's Mercy** can reverse any stage of ire — but Uther is proud and suspicious; mercy requires either a great service, a powerful advocate, or Merlin's intervention.

### Seeking Royal Favor
- **Favor** = special privilege or right given by the king indicating approval
- Obtaining favor requires either direct service (battle, gift, information) or an advocate among Uther's favorites
- Uther's **favorites** (who have his ear): Abbot John, Brother Hugh, Sir Ulfius, Sir Madoc, Sir Bellias/Brastias, Sir Sadinal, Sir Argan (until 493), Sir Do de Carduel, Sir Yvo, Sir Gaudifier

### Random Court Scenarios (Abbreviated)

Roll 1d10 for scenario type at any court session:
1. Legal dispute between two lords — player-knights asked to investigate
2. Foreign embassy — player-knights assigned as escorts or interpreters
3. Assassination plot rumored — player-knights assigned to guard duty
4. Young knight seeking sponsorship — player-knights must decide whether to help
5. Romantic intrigue — Lady at court sends player-knight a token; complications follow
6. The king's ire falls on an NPC the player-knights know
7. Religious conflict between British and Roman Church factions
8. Tournament or martial display announced — glory opportunity
9. Saxon spy captured — interrogation opportunity
10. Count Roderick asks player-knights to represent Salisbury in a delicate negotiation

## Court Offices: Advancement Opportunities

Player-knights who distinguish themselves can petition for court appointments. Each appointment provides:
- Annual pay bonus (see Table 2.1)
- Automatic skill check in key skill each year
- Opportunity roll for special events each year (those marked "Yes")
- Access to the king's ear commensurate with rank

Appointments require recommendation from an existing officer (usually one of Uther's favorites), a demonstrated high relevant skill (typically 15+), and either Glory 2,000+ or a notable deed.
