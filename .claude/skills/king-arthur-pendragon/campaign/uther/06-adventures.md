# Adventures — Runnable Scenario Briefs

Compressed, runnable briefs for every named scenario in the Book of Uther. Each brief contains hook, scenes, key NPCs/stats, locations, and outcomes.

**Use when:** Running a session, looking for an adventure seed for a specific year, or filling time between major events. Cross-reference the timeline (05) for year placement.

---

## Format Note
Each adventure follows this structure:
- **Year:** When it occurs in the timeline
- **Hook:** How the player-knights get involved
- **Scenes:** Key encounter beats (not flavor text)
- **Key NPCs:** Stats or references
- **Location:** Where it happens
- **Outcomes:** What changes based on success/failure

## Battle of Menevia
**Year:** 480  
**Type:** Pitched battle (Book of Battle required)

**Hook:** Uther musters Logres' forces to respond to King Paschent's Irish invasion of Wales. Player-knights are called to the banner.

**Scenes:**
1. Muster at the border; Uther presents the plan
2. The battle itself (9 rounds, Large)
3. Pursuit of survivors; potential for individual glory (personal combat vs. Irish champions)

**Key NPCs/Stats:**
- Uther: Battle 20, commanding British
- Gillomanius (Irish King): Battle 15, commanding Irish
- Battle result: Decisive Victory for British

**Location:** Menevia (St David's area, Wales/Logres border)

**Outcomes:**
- Success: Paschent's invasion is broken; £2 plunder available; knights may earn glory for personal combat
- Failure: Not possible given historical result, but individual knights can die or be captured

## Battle of Salisbury
**Year:** 480  
**Type:** Pitched battle; Aurelius dies mid-battle; Gorlois must rally

**Hook:** The Saxons threaten Logres proper. Player-knights ride with Aurelius' army.

**Scenes:**
1. Pre-battle: Aurelius is pale and feverish (poisoned by the Saxon spy — player-knights may try to discover this)
2. Battle opens (Aurelius [Battle 16] vs. Aesc [Battle 18])
3. Round 6: Aurelius collapses and dies. Army risks routing.
4. Gorlois [Battle 21] takes command; rallies the knights; turns the battle
5. Aftermath: Saxon defeat; but Aurelius is dead; Uther is crowned

**Key NPCs/Stats:**
- Aurelius: Battle 16, d. round 6 (poison)
- Aesc of Kent: Battle 18
- Gorlois: Battle 21 (saves the day)
- 7 rounds total, Large battle

**Location:** Near Sarum (Salisbury), Salisbury County

**Outcomes:**
- Success (likely): Saxons defeated. Aurelius mourned. Uther assumes kingship.
- Drama opportunity: player-knights who notice Aurelius is ill before the battle can try to investigate the Saxon spy (Intrigue or Awareness roll)

## Battle of Bedegraine

**Type:** Conquest campaign; small battle

**Hook:** Uther declares Bedegraine must swear homage. Player-knights ride with the royal army.

**Scenes:**
1. March to Bedegraine; the King of Bedegraine refuses to submit
2. Battle (3 rounds, Small)
3. Uther personally slays the King of Bedegraine in single combat
4. Occupation and sheriff appointment

**Key NPCs/Stats:**

- King of Bedegraine: Battle 17

**Location:** Bedegraine County (Nottinghamshire/Derbyshire area)

**Outcomes:**
- Bedegraine absorbed into Logres

## Battle of Eburacum (Ambush)
**Year:** 484  
**Type:** Disaster battle — Saxon ambush; manage the rout

**Hook:** Uther marches north to relieve Malahaut from Saxon pressure. Player-knights join the royal army.

**Scenes:**
1. March through Linden and Malahaut territory
2. The trap: Saxons set an ambush (British -10 modifier round 1)
3. Battle (6 rounds, Large; Decisive Defeat for British)
4. Retreat: player-knights must fight rearguard to prevent complete annihilation
5. Night: Uther plans the Mt. Damen counterattack

**Key NPCs/Stats:**
- Uther: Battle 20
- Octa: Battle 19, HP 30, Axe 21 (magical +2 damage)
- Eosa: Battle 15, HP 36, Axe 20, SIZ 22

**Location:** Eburacum (York) and surrounding territory, Malahaut

**Outcomes:**
- Failure (historical): Army is badly damaged; many knights dead or captured; morale is shattered
- Player-knight survival is the primary goal
- Those who hold the rearguard earn significant glory

## Battle of Mt. Damen (Night Attack)
**Year:** 484 (same night as Eburacum aftermath)  
**Type:** Night raid; guerrilla battle

**Hook:** After the Eburacum disaster, Uther rallies survivors for a desperate night attack on the Saxon camp.

**Scenes:**
1. Uther's midnight plan: volunteers only; move silently
2. Night approach (Stealth rolls; sentries must be handled quietly)
3. The attack (3 rounds, Medium; surprise bonus for British)
4. Saxon camp in chaos; target Octa or Eosa for personal glory

**Key NPCs/Stats:**
- Uther: Battle 20, Sword 23 (personally in the attack)
- Saxon Veteran Axemen: Armor 8+shield, Axe 16, HP 32 (but sleeping/disorganized)

**Location:** Mt. Damen (northern Britain, exact location unspecified)

**Outcomes:**
- Success (historical): Decisive Victory. £10 plunder available. Army's morale restored.
- Personal glory: Capturing a Saxon chieftain, finding the Saxon battle standards, or reaching Octa/Eosa's tent all merit major glory awards

## Adventure of Water Leapers
**Year:** 488  
**Type:** Monster hunt

**Hook:** Reports come to court of a nest of "water leapers" attacking travelers and fishermen near a river or lake in Logres. Uther assigns knights to deal with it.

**Scenes:**
1. Investigation: locals report attacks; find the lair's location (Awareness, Hunting, Folklore rolls)
2. Approach: water leapers attack when crossing water or approaching the nest
3. The nest: multiple water leapers; combat in or near water

**Key NPCs/Stats:**
- Water Leaper: Flying/swimming creature; attacks from the water; GM creates stats (suggest: HP 12, SIZ 8, attacks with sharp beak/talons, Claw 14 for 2d6 damage; can grapple in water)
- 1d6+2 water leapers in the nest

**Location:** Any river or wetland in Logres (Lonazep's fens are thematic)

**Outcomes:**
- Success: Nest destroyed; local population grateful; glory for monster slaying
- Glory: Standard monster glory (typically 100-250 for a nest clearing)

## Lindsey Embassy
**Year:** 487  
**Type:** Diplomatic mission; courtly scenario

**Hook:** Uther announces he will visit Duke Corneus of Lindsey at Linden Pool Castle. Player-knights are assigned to the escort.

**Scenes:**
1. The Progress: travel north through multiple baronial lands; hospitality at each stop
2. Arrival at Linden Pool: Corneus' court; assess the Duke's mood (Intrigue roll)
3. The Revelation: Uther draws Excalibur in the great hall; Corneus is visibly shaken and renews his oath
4. Optional: Malahaut envoys arrive; player-knights overhear tense negotiations about the Centurion King's request for aid

**Key NPCs:**
- Duke Corneus: Uther is Suspicious (Corneus) 17; the Duke is loyal but wary
- Centurion King's envoys: seeking military commitment; Uther deflects

**Location:** Linden Pool Castle (Lincoln, Linden County)

**Outcomes:**
- Success: Mission accomplished; Corneus is loyal again; player-knights earn diplomatic glory
- Discovery: Player-knights who succeed at Intrigue may learn that Corneus is less loyal than he appears, or that the Centurion King is already considering alternatives

## The Prince's Passion
**Year:** 483 (or any mid-period year)  
**Type:** Court intrigue; romantic entanglement

**Hook:** Prince Madoc is involved in a love affair that threatens to cause a political incident. A lady at court is connected to one of Uther's baronial rivals. Madoc asks the player-knights to help him manage the situation discreetly.

**Scenes:**
1. Madoc approaches the player-knights privately; explains the situation (she is promised to another; her father is an influential baron)
2. Player-knights must either facilitate the affair, discourage it, or manage its discovery
3. The father discovers the liaison; confrontation at court
4. Resolution: legal, violent, or diplomatic

**Key NPCs:**
- Madoc: STR 18, CON 20, HP 35, Sword 15, Spear Exp 17; Proud 17, Valorous 18
- The Lady: APP high (suggest 16+), Intrigue moderate
- Her Father: A baronial figure; Battle/Sword stats appropriate to his position

**Location:** Court (wherever it is currently being held)

**Outcomes:**
- Success: Madoc's reputation protected; the baron's anger managed; player-knights earn a powerful patron
- Failure: Scandal at court; Madoc is embarrassed; the baron becomes an enemy; possible royal ire if Uther is displeased by the mess

## Excalibur's Peace (489 Political Crisis)
**Year:** 489  
**Type:** Political crisis; may include skirmishing

**Hook:** Uther calls his banners against Cornwall. Player-knights must ride — but everything is unclear. Is this war? Is the duke really a traitor? Why is the army so understrength?

**Scenes:**
1. Muster: notice how few barons send full levies (Intrigue to understand why)
2. Advance toward Cornwall; Gorlois sends envoys to negotiate
3. Merlin arrives and intervenes; the public display of Excalibur
4. The two armies camp together; player-knights interact with Cornish knights
5. United march north against Saxons; the political crisis is over — for now

**Key NPCs:**
- Merlin: The peacemaker; will not explain why he wants this resolved peacefully
- Gorlois: Battle 21, Passions: Love Ygraine 18, Homage Uther 9

**Location:** The road from Logres to Cornwall; then northward march

**Outcomes:**
- Success: Peace holds; combined army fights the Saxons; player-knights earn glory in the subsequent northern skirmishing
- Insight: Player-knights with high Intrigue may realize that Merlin is deliberately managing both Uther and Gorlois for an unknown purpose

## Battle of Linden Pool
**Year:** 490  
**Type:** Pitched battle — the biggest British victory

**Hook:** Octa and Eosa march south, bypassing Malahaut. Uther musters. Player-knights ride with the combined army.

**Scenes:**
1. Intelligence: scouts report two Saxon armies converging on Linden Pool area
2. Positioning: Uther and Gorlois discuss strategy (Gorlois' Battle 21 is crucial)
3. The battle (stats not fully detailed in text; treat as Large, 8-10 rounds)
4. Pursuit: Octa and Eosa are caught and taken prisoner; Saxon hostages collected
5. The Victory Feast at Linden Pool Castle — Ygraine is there; something in Uther changes

**Key NPCs:**
- Octa: Battle 19, HP 30, Axe 21 (magical)
- Eosa: Battle 15, HP 36, Axe 20, SIZ 22
- Gorlois: Battle 21 (essential to victory)
- Sir Bellias: Captain of Guard, killed this battle (Sword 21, Spear 22)

**Location:** Linden Pool area (Lincoln area, Linden County)

**Outcomes:**
- Decisive Victory: Octa and Eosa captured; Saxon threat to the north neutralized (temporarily)
- Complication: Uther's obsession with Ygraine is visible at the victory feast; player-knights with Intrigue may notice Gorlois noticing

## The Tintagel Campaign
**Year:** 491  
**Type:** Siege; moral complexity; Merlin's bargain

**Hook:** War against Cornwall is declared. Player-knights serve in Uther's army besieging Castle Terrabil.

**Scenes:**
1. The siege: Terrabil cannot be taken; the army ravages the countryside; frustration builds
2. Uther's desperation: he meets privately with Merlin; player-knights may be near enough to notice something strange
3. The withdrawal: Uther orders a false retreat; player-knights are confused — why retreat when we haven't won?
4. The sortie: Gorlois unexpectedly rides out from Terrabil with his men — Merlin's magic convinced him to do it; combat in the dark
5. Dawn: Gorlois is dead; Tintagel surrenders; Ygraine comes out with the news of her husband's death
6. Winter garrison: Player-knights winter at Terrabil, surrounded by hostile locals

**Key NPCs:**
- Gorlois: Battle 21, d. this battle (killed in the nighttime sortie)
- Prince Madoc: also d. this battle
- Sir Goreau (Seneschal): d. this battle

**Location:** Castle Terrabil (Bossiney, Cornwall), Tintagel Castle

**Outcomes:**
- Gorlois is dead regardless; player-knights who fought well earn glory
- Drama: player-knights who were near Uther during the night may have seen something wrong — a man who looked like Gorlois approaching Tintagel from the outside? This is a mystery that the GM can deploy or not.
- Moral weight: everyone in Cornwall blames the king and his army for what happened here

## Helping Merlin (Treason)
**Year:** 492  
**Type:** Chase/intrigue; moral trap

**Hook:** Merlin asks the player-knights for help. He has an urgent errand — he needs them to slow down or misdirect some men who are following him. He does not explain why.

**Scenes:**
1. Merlin's approach: he is friendly and urgent; he asks the player-knights to intercept a group of riders and delay them ("They are carrying a false summons — delay them; ask questions; let an hour pass")
2. The intercept: the riders turn out to be Uther's household knights, carrying the actual baby Arthur (who Merlin just took from the midwives)
3. The confrontation: Uther's men demand the player-knights stand aside; Merlin is already gone
4. The chase: Uther's men reach the point where Merlin was — nothing. The baby is gone.
5. The accusation: Uther's men report back; the player-knights are named as conspirators

**Key NPCs:**
- Merlin: fully in control; will not be caught; will never explain why he needed Arthur
- Uther's household knights: Sword 18-20, full armor; they are doing their job

**Location:** Somewhere near Tintagel or Londinium (where the birth occurred)

**Treason Trial follows:**
- King's Bench convenes
- Count Roderick of Salisbury defends them (risks his own status)
- Merlin is declared outlaw in absentia
- Player-knight outcome depends on GM choice: acquittal (due to Roderick's defense), amercement (fine), or conviction (requiring escape or pardon)

**Outcomes:**
- Acquitted: Player-knights are free; must walk carefully around Uther; owe Roderick significantly
- Convicted: Must flee; outlaws until pardoned; OR pardon comes eventually as Uther becomes distracted by his illness
- Insight: Player-knights now know Arthur exists, that Merlin has him, and that the succession is not as simple as Uther thinks

## Adventure of the Questing Beast
**Year:** 492  
**Type:** Monster encounter; introduction to Pellinore

**Hook:** Player-knights are traveling or hunting when they encounter a strange creature — it makes the noise of thirty baying hounds from inside its body, has a serpent's head, a leopard's body, and a lion's hindquarters. It is the Questing Beast (Glatisant). Behind it comes a tired but utterly determined king.

**Scenes:**
1. The creature: it crosses their path; it is clearly not threatening them; it is running from something
2. King Pellinore arrives: exhausted, excited, focused entirely on the beast. He barely acknowledges the knights.
3. Conversation: Pellinore explains he gave up his kingdom to hunt this beast; his son Lamorak is managing Norgales badly; he doesn't care
4. Optional: the Beast pauses and the players must decide — interfere with the hunt? Follow? Let it go?

**Key NPCs:**
- King Pellinore: not a threat; a harmless eccentric driven by divine compulsion; he has hunted this beast for years
- The Glatisant: it is not hostile to knights; it is supernaturally fast; catching it requires something beyond a simple chase

**Location:** Anywhere in rural Logres; Norgales border area works thematically

**Outcomes:**
- Encounter: Player-knights learn of Norgales' political chaos; may later investigate
- Future hook: Lamorak is at Uther's court (492) seeking news; player-knights can connect these events
- The beast itself cannot be caught this period; it reappears in the GPC

## The Argan Scandal (Court Adventure)
**Year:** 493  
**Type:** Court intrigue; legal drama

**Hook:** Sir Argan ap Maelgad, Constable of Logres, appears at Easter Court and publicly accuses King Uther of adultery with his wife Dyagenne. This is not a player-knight adventure directly — but their reactions and actions matter enormously.

**Scenes:**
1. Public accusation: Argan, half-mad with grief and rage, makes his accusation in open court; he has already killed Dyagenne
2. The challenge: Argan demands trial by combat; Uther accepts
3. The duel: Argan Sword 21, Energetic 16, Vengeful 18 vs. Uther Sword 23, Excalibur (+5); but God delivers justice
4. Uther loses: he is wounded, disarmed, helpless
5. The ransom: Argan demands a strong castle; Uther pays
6. Political fallout: player-knights must choose sides; the court is in shock

**Key NPCs:**
- Argan: Sword 21, HP — (not detailed; treat as ~22-24 given his STR/CON), Vengeful 18; he will not be talked down
- Uther: Sword 23 (+5 for Excalibur = 28 effective), but God's justice overrides mechanics
- Note: The GM may choose to handle this narratively (Argan wins because the story demands it) or mechanically (give Argan a lucky critical)

**Location:** Silchester Castle court (Easter Court 493)

**Outcomes:**
- Fixed: Argan wins; the castle is built; it is named Uther's Shame
- Player opportunities: mediate the aftermath? Escort Argan out safely? Track the castle's construction for intelligence value? Discover whose side the courtiers are on?

## Embassy to Malahaut (493)
**Year:** 493  
**Type:** Diplomatic mission in hostile territory

**Hook:** Count Roderick of Salisbury is tasked with delivering Uther's ultimatum to the Centurion King. He takes a bodyguard of Salisbury knights — the player-knights.

**Scenes:**
1. The journey north through Linden (Corneus is suspicious of strangers now)
2. The Centurion King's court at Eburacum: the king is cold, formal, and dismissive; the ultimatum is badly received
3. Discovery: player-knights with high Awareness or Intrigue notice that Saxon men-at-arms are visible in the city; the Centurion King is already accommodating the enemy
4. The departure: Roderick is unwelcome; they must leave before relations shatter completely
5. The ambush: Saxon warriors strike on the road home; Roderick survives but it is close

**Key NPCs:**
- Count Roderick: Battle 17; he manages this with dignity; he will not start a war here
- Centurion King: hostile; not going to submit; already committed to the Saxon alliance
- Saxon Ambushers: Veteran Axemen (Armor 8+shield, Axe 16, HP 32) in force

**Location:** Road from Salisbury through Linden to Eburacum (Malahaut) and back

**Outcomes:**
- Success (survival is success): Roderick survives; intelligence on the Saxon-Malahaut alliance is brought back; player-knights who fought well earn glory
- Critical information: If a player-knight succeeds at Awareness during the city visit, they see that the Centurion King's guard now includes Saxon veterans — this is the proof of the alliance

## Battle of St Albans
**Year:** 495  
**Type:** Pitched battle — Uther's last; first defeat then victory

**Hook:** Uther raises his army at Castle of the Rock. The player-knights march with Salisbury's remaining forces under Count Roderick. The king is visibly ill but personally present.

**Scenes:**
1. Muster: the army is understrength; many lordless knights have not rallied
2. The march: Uther's condition is visible; soldiers are worried but loyal
3. Arrival too late: the Saxons hold St Albans; initial assault fails
4. Night: Uther refuses to retreat; plans the morning battle
5. The field battle: Saxons gamble on Malahaut canceling the Logres knights; the gamble fails; player-knights crush Malahaut and turn the flank
6. Victory: Saxons routed; celebration

**Key NPCs:**
- Uther: Battle 20, ill; present but limited
- Ulfius: Battle 19, gravely wounded during fighting (absent from feast)
- Brastias: Sword 23, gravely wounded during fighting (absent from feast)
- Count Roderick: Battle 17; leads Salisbury contingent; d. at feast

**Location:** St Albans (Hartland County)

**Outcomes:**
- Victory (historical): Saxons defeated; great celebration begins
- The feast follows immediately: player-knights are at the victory dinner when the poisoning begins

## The Infamous Feast
**Year:** 495  
**Type:** Horror/survival scenario

**Hook:** The victory feast at St Albans (or wherever the celebration is held). The player-knights are attending. The wine is poured.

**Scenes:**
1. The feast: glory and celebration; Uther seems almost well for the first time in years
2. The first deaths: someone collapses; then another; the pattern becomes clear — the wine
3. Chaos: panic, accusations; the Saxon physician is seized and executed immediately
4. Triage: player-knights may try to save NPCs (Chirurgery and First Aid rolls); Count Roderick is dying; Uther is dying
5. The morning after: "There are no lords in Logres"

**Key NPCs/Saves:**
- Uther: d. (no save)
- Count Roderick: d. (no save unless player specifically intercepts the wine before he drinks — GM discretion)
- If player-knights themselves drank from the wine: Constitution roll or poison damage (suggest 4d6 poison damage, CON roll at -10 to halve)
- Sir Ulfius and Brastias: not present (wounded); they survive

**Location:** Victory feast location (probably near St Albans or at a nearby baronial hall)

**Outcomes:**
- Fixed: Uther and most nobility are dead; Logres enters the Anarchy
- Player choice: which surviving NPCs do they try to help? Who do they get to safety? Do they investigate the poisoning?
- The Sword in the Stone appears by Christmas: the mystery of Arthur begins

## The Savage Forest (495, Post-Feast)
**Year:** 495 onward  
**Type:** Faerie adventure; entry into the Anarchy era

**Hook:** The Savage Forest suddenly appears around Castle Dykes (Wuerensis) and spreads. One of ten possible reasons draws the player-knights in:

| Roll 1d10 | Reason to Enter |
|-----------|----------------|
| 1 | A faerie lord kidnapped a female relative of a player-knight or Count Salisbury |
| 2 | A faerie left a changeling of a player-knight's or Roderick's child |
| 3 | Prince Madoc's son (Eliwlod) is missing; a faerie did it |
| 4 | A faerie knows what castle a player-knight is fated to hold |
| 5 | A faerie killed the father of a player-knight or of Count Salisbury |
| 6 | A faerie stole the family heirloom of a player-knight or Count Salisbury |
| 7 | A faerie promised a healing potion but did not deliver |
| 8 | Archbishop Dubricus or a famous abbot said the king must be overcome and banished, or killed |
| 9 | Merlin said go there |
| 10 | Excalibur was supposed to be returned to the Faeries when Uther died. Instead it is in a stone. The faeries are very displeased. |

**Castle Sauvage appears because** the reason chosen has broken the magical barrier and forced it visible. The faerie wood appears because its lord wants to protect Castle Sauvage from outsiders.

**Key facts for running the Forest:**
- The Forest is not hostile by default; it is protective
- Faerie lords within it follow their own customs; players must use Courtesy and Faerie Lore
- The reason for entering determines what Castle Sauvage contains and who can resolve the situation
- The Forest continues to grow through the Anarchy period
