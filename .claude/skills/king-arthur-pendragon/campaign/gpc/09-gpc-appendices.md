### Beings and Beasts

#### Avanc (Afanc)
Dangerous water spirit; looks like giant black beaver or huge crocodile. Roars like a waterfall; splashes when walking on dry land (leaves wet trail). Extremely dangerous in water.
```
Glory Won 200
SIZ 20, DEX 10*, STR 40, CON 32
Move 5; Armor 5
Unconscious 13; Major Wound 32; Knockdown 20; Hit Points 52; Healing Rate 7; Damage 6d6
Combat Skills: Claws† 15
Significant Skills: Avoidance 5 (15 in water)
Prudent Modifier: +5; Valorous Modifier: –5
```
*Dodge: May attempt to Dodge one foe each round (using Avoidance or DEX, whichever better), whether or not it attacks.
†Multiple Attacks: Two claw attacks per round; may target separate opponents or both at same foe. Character attacked by both defends separately.
Whirlpool Attack: Each melee round, whirlpool strength increases by 5 (starting at 0). Applied as modifier to STR, Boating, Swimming. When crossing a ford: modified STR roll to stand; failure = swept in, then drowned. Victim in whirlpool: cannot be struck by melee weapons; avanc cannot use claws on that person.

Hatched from cock's egg by sitting toad. Looks like large snake with feathered cowl and crown; six white lengthwise stripes. Its gaze kills. Its body is acidic — weapons touching it are destroyed and the acid creeps up to the wielder. Can spit venom accurately to 25 yards.

#### Brownie / Boggart
Perhaps the most commonly encountered faerie; helpful rustic being at farms and country homes. Called bwca (Wales), bodach (Highlands), fenodoree (Man), grogan, grogach, gunna.
- Works at night to finish tasks servants left undone
- Exchange: leave out a bowl of cream or milk and a prized cake — as a GIFT to find, not a direct payment
- Direct payment offered = brownie leaves forever; never returns
- Well-treated: remains with family for generations; attaches to a particular family member
- Insulted: becomes a Boggart — causes mischief: destroys harvests, scares livestock, poltergeist, spoils milk, lames horses, leads travelers astray into bogs, uses elf-stones (penetrate armor without mark; fester; by mid-winter, victim loses 1 point off an attribute GM's choice)

## Appendix Two: The Enchantment Period

### Overview

The Enchantment of Britain is not a fixed Period — it slowly drifts into importance and ends with the Grail Quest's conclusion. It intersects the world of politics barely, but is a metaphysical time/place of High Adventure.

### Timeline of the Enchantment

| Years | Status |
|-------|--------|
| 495–528 | Forest Sauvage expands; strange agents appear; druids and pagans hear their gods more clearly |
| 529–539 | Bran's Head uncovered (529) — magical guardian of Britain lost; local faerie effects stronger. King Today visits (532) — Britain and Overthere are no longer separate kingdoms |
| 540–554 | Merlin's plans prepare the land for the Grail; monsters and strange beings common; Grail Quest preparations |
| 555+ | Enchantment ends when Galahad achieves the Holy Grail; faerie forests fade; new growth springs from dead trees; Britain re-enters natural cycles |

### The Enchanted Land

When a place becomes enchanted, it takes on vivid dreamlike quality:
- Things seem brighter and prettier
- Food tastes better
- Everything is easier for everyone
- Calm, regulated by pleasure, entirely satisfying

### Faerie vs. Wasteland Overlap

Where both phenomena occur:
- Areas move back and forth between the two states
- Near strong Faerie castles: bright faerie forests preside (unless the ruler is Unseelie — then even more gloomy)
- Transition can happen between visits by player knights

### Elves

**Names:** Tylwyth Teg (Fair Folk), Bendith Ymamau (Mother's Blessing in Wales), Good Folk, Gentry. They call themselves the Tuatha de Danaan (Children of Dana).

**The Court:**
- King: sometimes Finvarra, sometimes Oberon
- Queen: Oonagh, Nuala, Titania, or Mab (depending on source)
- Spriggan bodyguards; elf knights and courtiers
- Enchanting music is standard

**The Split:** Good elves = Seelie Court. Bad faeries = Unseelie Court. Seelie lords include Gleodalen (King of Dwarfs), King Sauvage of the Forest Sauvage.

**Origins (disputed):**
- Simply grew out of the earth
- Angels kicked from Heaven for neutrality in the war against Satan
- Descent from ancient gods and powers of old

**Elf Maids:** Like human women but better, more lavishly dressed, always with touch of magic. Most magical human women (Morgan le Fay, Nimue of the Lake) are virtually indistinguishable from elf women.

**Elf Knights:** Seem completely human; act in every way like humans. Not shy about iron (unlike common faeries). Their weapons and accouterments are of superior, foreign metal.

### Elfin Supernatural Powers

Sample powers from "Kilwch and Olwen" (Mabinogion) — use names of Welsh heroes as names for elves:

- **Henbedestyr:** No one could keep pace with him, on horseback or on foot
- **Sgilti Yscawndroed:** Never sought a path; went along the tops of trees; a blade of reed grass bent not under his feet
- **Drem son of Dremidyd:** Could see from Cornwall to North Britain
- **Gwadyn Odycith:** Soles of his feet emitted sparks of fire when they struck hard things (like heated iron from a forge)
- **Morvran son of Tegid:** No one struck him at Camlann for his ugliness; all thought he was an auxiliary devil
- **Sandde Bryd Angle:** No one touched him at Camlann for his beauty; all thought he was a ministering angel

### Elf Equipment

**Armor:** Basic elf armor is similar to humans of same period (or one period better). Often:
- +2–4 extra points of magical protection
- Or impervious to a specific creature (dragon, wolf, etc.) or weapon
- Distinctive in style: animal imagery (deer, boar), covered in ivy, strange faces; elegant, spiky, or flowing decoration
- Helmets: wings, horns, esoteric symbols as crests

**Weapons:** All knight weapons + superfluous decorative features. Many enchanted for a few extra damage points, often against a certain enemy type.

**Horses:** Tall, muscular, intelligent; sleek flanks; championship lines. Can be any color, including unnatural (green, blue, red to match rider's armor). Unusual mounts: striped black and white, or other combinations.

**Clothing:** Always beyond high fashion; worthy of admiration and envy; retinues dressed in uniform colors.

### Heraldry of Elves

- Elf lords have exclusive coats of arms
- Their knights often bear the same symbol (army affiliation)
- Heraldry success = identifies the knight as elf from such-and-such realm
- Heraldry critical = identifies the elf lord from his followers (quality of gear)

### Customs

- Elves follow human customs; enjoy Chivalry and Romance (idealistic, not materialistic)
- **Literal** in oaths and promises (the letter, not the spirit) — seems devious to humans
- Generally: no tournaments (as mortals know them)

### Faerie Castles

Layout and defensive value similar to mortal keeps. Often have:
- Lofty towers stretching over tall walls
- "Disneyland"-type structures
- Built from materials that seem impossible (silver stones, living wood)

### New Passion: Fey

When mortal knights become too exposed to Faerie, the mundane world pales for them. Knight is called "fey" — seeming more elf than man.

**Fey is generated when:** A traumatic experience involves Faerie — hideous monster encounter, serious dalliance with an elf-maid, or a journey to Faerie realms.

**Note:** Falling in love with a Faerie and acquiring Amor or Love (faerie lover) also generates a Fey passion equal to the Amor or Love value.

**Effects of Fey Passion:**
- All mortal women seem dull and coarse
- Mortal castles seem hovels by comparison with faerie courts
- This malaise affects every aspect of the knight's life
- May lead to premature death
- Accelerated Aging: Must make an additional aging roll every year, even if normally exempt

**Breaking Fey:**
1. **Falling in Love (Mortal):** Generate a Love or Amor passion. The new passion is immediately contested against Fey. If Love/Amor wins: Fey reduced to 0. If Fey wins: Fey remains. Can retry every time Love/Amor increases.

## Appendix Three: The Goblin Market

The Goblin Market is a faerie marketplace that appears and disappears, wandering through Britain's wild places. It has no fixed location.

### Finding the Market

**Roll to Encounter (once per year for wandering characters):**
- Hunting: Awareness vs. 15 in wild places
- OR: A faerie deliberately leads the knight to it (Glamour)

### What Is for Sale

The Market sells any item imaginable — but prices are never in money.

**Payment Methods:**
- A year of the buyer's life (ages 1 year immediately)
- A cherished memory (gone forever; that part of the character's history is blank)
- A skill or trait point (permanently reduced by 1d3)
- A promise of future service (binding; defaults lose Honor and passions)
- First-born child
- A piece of the buyer's shadow (mysterious long-term effect; GM's choice)

**Sample Wares:**

| Item | Price |
|------|-------|
| Faerie horse | 1 year of life |
| Healing potion (full restore) | A cherished memory |
| Enchanted weapon (+2d6 damage) | A promise of service |
| Map to any location in Britain | A skill point |
| A week of perfect weather | 2 years of life |
| Information about any NPC | A trait point |
| A faerie steed | First-born child |
| Any object the buyer has lost | A promise |

### Market Rules

1. No violence in the Market (enforced by goblins with immediately lethal consequences)
2. All trades are final — no returns, no exchanges
3. Buyers who renege on promises: cursed (GM determines effect; minimum = Cowardly check + 3 Honor lost)
4. The Market remembers every transaction; a buyer who returns is recognized

### Goblin Merchants

Each merchant is a goblin of unusual type. Roll 1d6 for the merchant's specialty:
1. Weapons and armor (enchanted only)
2. Potions and herbs (magical effects)
3. Horses and beasts (faerie stock)
4. Information and secrets
5. Services (hired faerie help)
6. Stolen goods from mortal lords (identifiable with Heraldry)

## Appendix Five: The Ladies of the Lake

The Ladies of the Lake are a sisterhood of powerful enchantresses who dwell in the lake kingdoms. They serve as counterparts to the male-dominated world of chivalry — as powerful in magic and politics as any king.

### The Principal Ladies

**Nimue (Chief Lady of the Lake)**
- The most powerful; Merlin's nemesis and successor as magical advisor to Arthur
- Imprisoned Merlin in the Forest of Brioceliande (532 area; confirmed absent ~533)
- Continues Merlin's role: advises Arthur, provides Excalibur (already done at start of campaign), appears at key moments
- Ultimately: one of the four ladies on the barge to Avalon at Camlann

**Morgan le Fay**
- Arthur's half-sister; greatest enchantress; student of Merlin before Nimue
- Generally antagonistic to Arthur and Guenever
- Specifically: tried to kill Arthur with the poisoned mantle (Year 546)
- Provided Morgan's Valley (a place of healing and magic)
- Also: one of the four ladies on the barge to Avalon
- Note: Despite all her plots against Arthur, at the end she is the one who takes him to be healed

**Morgause (Queen of Orkney)**
- Arthur's half-sister; mother of Gawaine, Agravaine, Gaheris, Gareth, and (by Arthur) Mordred
- Killed by her son Gaheris when he discovered her having an affair
- Her death sets off the Orkney clan's murderous cycle of revenge

**Elaine (Lady of Corbin / Lady of the Grail)**
- Daughter of King Pellam (the Fisher King's kin)
- Lancelot's true love (though she never wins him from Guenever)
- By Lancelot: mother of Galahad
- Raised Galahad in secret; prepared him for the Grail Quest

**The Lady of Avalon**
- The greatest of them all; she who receives Arthur at the end
- Sometimes identified with Nimue; sometimes Morgan; sometimes a third unnamed figure
- Rules the island of Avalon where Arthur is healed of his mortal wounds

### The Lake Kingdoms

The Ladies rule over a network of hidden kingdoms beneath or beside the great lakes and rivers of Britain:

| Lake/Location | Lady | Status |
|---------------|------|--------|
| Dozmary Pool (Cornwall) | Local lady | Active through campaign |
| Lake Vortigern (Wales) | Unnamed | Active through campaign |
| Loch of Lothian | Tied to Morgause | Active through 563 |
| The Enchanted Lake (France) | Viviane | Raised Lancelot there |
| Avalon | Lady of Avalon | Appears at Camlann |

### Player Interaction with the Ladies

**Asking for Help:** A lady can be petitioned by a knight with sufficient reputation (Glory 1,000+) or by presenting a specific magical problem.

**What They Can Do:**
- Heal magical wounds that cannot be healed by mortal means
- Provide magical items (at a price)
- Give information about the Enchantment and Wasteland
- Offer prophecy (always true; never clear)
- Transport knights to and from the Otherworld

**What They Want:**
- Service of worthy knights (for 1 year, minimum)
- Secrets of other courts
- Specific magical ingredients or objects
- Occasionally: they simply like the knight and help freely

### Morgan le Fay's Plot Timeline

| Year | Action |

| 530s | Provides the magical shield to trap knights |
| 541 | Castle of Annowre — enchants Arthur |

Portray her as complex: capable of great cruelty and great tenderness, always serving purposes only she fully understands.
