# GPC Chapter 10: Twilight Period (558–566)

**Situation:** The campaign enters its final phase. The Enchantments are gone, normal life resumes — but beneath the restored normalcy, the court's tensions are reaching a breaking point. The Lancelot-Guenever affair becomes a political crisis. Mordred's scheming intensifies. The tragedy unfolds across eight years, ending at the Battle of Camlann and the departure of Arthur to Avalon.

**Period Boundary:** 558–565 (Chapter 10 title says 558–565; Year 566 is the final reckoning).

## Year 558 — Return to Normal

**Situation:** After the Quest and the Germination, life returns to normal. Lancelot accepts every woman's petition for help; Guenever is jealous. Sir Alisander le Orphelin is made a knight.

**Court:** Camelot.

**Away From Camelot:** Mordred, Gaheris, Agravaine (gone north, Castle of Maidens in Lothian). Bors, Percivale, Galahad (Percivale and Galahad not returning — Galahad ascended, Percivale in Listeneisse as Grail King).

**Events:**

**Blood Tournaments Begin:** Mordred and followers firmly at Castle of Maidens, Lothian (the old Orkney kingdom). They use real weapons and mock the southerners who "drink French wine instead of mead."

**Challenge of Alisander:**
- Sir Alisander le Orphelin (son of Prince Boudwin, nephew to King Mark) is made a knight
- Given his father's bloody garments; tasked to avenge him
- Mark sends Morgan le Fay gifts to destroy him; she sends Sir Malgrin (wily, murderous old knight)
- Malgrin initially winning; boasts of unjust victories; Alisander inspired by Justice passion and slays him

**Brian Attacks Carduel:** Fleet lands at Solway Firth; British fleet waiting but pirates outnumber them; decisive pirate victory. Pillage of Carduel. Griflet arrives too late.

**Mordred in Eburacum:** Saying to the young prince: "If there was a war and you came to help me, there would be no submission. Malahaut would be free."

**Pax Arthur Ends (partially):** Because of fighting in Carduel: Land was pillaged (+2d6) Starting Bad Weather.

**Tournament Schedule (558):**

## Year 559 — Sir Urré's Healing / Brian Attacks

**Situation:** A Hungarian knight under a curse comes to Camelot. Every knight fails to heal him — until Lancelot. Sir Bors returns. Brian attacks Anglia.

**Court:** Carlisle.

**Away From Camelot:** Mordred, Gaheris, Agravaine (north). Lancelot (questing). Lavaine.

**Events:**

### Sir Urré's Healing

**Source:** Malory XIX, 10–12

**Background:** In Spain, Sir Urré slew the son of an enchantress. The mother cursed his wounds to never heal unless treated by "the best knight in the world." He has been traveling seven years. He arrives with his mother and sister.

**The Healing Sequence:**
- 110 knights present; King Arthur tries first — fails
- Each knight present attempts healing; all fail
- Lancelot arrives (just returned from quest)
- Lancelot refuses (Modest critical); Arthur orders him; Urré begs him
- Lancelot kneels humbly, prays, places hands on wounds
- Wounds stop bleeding, close, heal — as if 7 years old
- "I am ready to go jousting," says the healed Urré
- Lancelot weeps like a child

**Player Hook:** Player knights can attempt healing (automatic fail, but role-play the attempt). Witnessing this event: 50 Glory.

**Sir Bors Returns:** With tales of miracles and exotic lands. He has been on the road for three years.
- Relates: Percivale healed his grandfather (King Fisherman)
- The land bloomed as soon as healing was complete
- Percivale retired to rule Carbonek and Listeneisse
- Galahad and Bors sailed to Middle East; Galahad died ascending to heaven
- Bors returned alone

**Brian Attacks Anglia:** Fleet sweeps down to Yarmouth; local fleet destroyed; lands pillaged; Saxon locals join in; ducal army soundly defeated; Griflet arrives too late. Army suppresses Saxon rebellion afterward.

**Christmas Court 559:** Wedding of Sir Lavaine (Lancelot's companion) and Lady Felelolie (sister of Sir Urré). They settle in Britain permanently.

**Winter Phase (559):** Pax Arthur: –1d6 to Starting Bad Weather all Logres. Anglia: raided (+1d6).

**Tournament Schedule (559):**
- Pentecost at Camelot: artificial forest on tournament ground; knights and ladies dash about finding each other while squires in beast costumes hunt them; prize = large illuminated book + a varlet to read from it
- July: Neighborhood (3) (armor; no wager; armor+horse), Local, Regional (Dorchester, Dorset)
- August: Neighborhood (armor+horse), Local (2) (no wager; ransom), Regional (Gaiholm, Gorre, ransom)
- September: Neighborhood (2), Local (armor)
- October: Neighborhood, Local, Regional (Newcastle, Nohaut [armor]; Tadcaster, Malahaut [ransom])

**If the player does nothing:** Lancelot's miraculous healing of Sir Urré occurs regardless; Brian raids Anglia and Griflet arrives too late to stop the damage. Absent knights miss the 50-Glory witnessing of Urré's healing and gain no tournament Glory for the year.

### Poison and Treason

**Source:** Malory XVIII, 2–8

**The Dinner:** Guenever hosts 20 knights — all leading Orkneys and de Ganis (except Lancelot), plus others. She lavishes equal praise on all (Mordred says this is deliberate cover for her focus on Lancelot).

**The Death:** Sir Patrise (good Irish knight, cousin to Mador de la Port) takes an apple from the fruit dish. He eats it; seized with choking; bloats terribly; dies amid horrible expulsions. Round Table knights are horrified and shocked.

**The Accusation:** Sir Mador de la Port accuses Guenever of murder — a capital offense. King Arthur questions knights; all agree she must have poisoned him. No other explanation. Mador demands judgment: if no champion appears in 15 days, she is to be burned as a murderer.

**Lancelot is away.** Bors agrees publicly to defend her — states he thinks she is innocent.

**Trial Day:**
- Guenever placed in the Constable's box; iron post nearby, wood piled for burning
- Mador swears and pledges his body
- Bors swears the queen is in the right
- Last moment: a mystery knight appears, takes the quarrel
- Mystery knight and Mador fight; Mador strikes deep through the mystery knight's thigh
- In anger, the mystery knight wounds Mador nearly to death, dashes off his helmet
- Mador, defeated, surrenders; extracts oath that queen is innocent and matter is closed forever
- Mystery knight addresses the crowd: this was an insult to the entire Round Table; only one good knight defended the queen

**Unmaskment:** King begs the mystery knight to show himself — it is Lancelot.

**Lancelot:** "It is right that I always take your quarrel, or that of the queen, for I am your man."

**Note:** The actual poisoner was Sir Pinel le Savage, who intended to kill Gawaine. The apple was meant for him. The crime was never publicly pinned on Pinel (Guinevere was merely exonerated).

**Brian Attacks Estregales:** Mark attacks Estregales; then raids Leinster.

**Winter Phase (561):** Pax Arthur: –1d6 all Logres. Estregales, Leinster: raided (+1d6).

**Tournament Schedule (561):**
- Pentecost: 20 white mules pull wagon with fake island; knights imprisoned by men in beast costumes; ladies with flowers storm it; rescued knights recite poems; prize = gyrfalcon for "King of Poets"
- July: Neighborhood (2) (both armor), Local (ransom)

- September: Neighborhood (armor), Local (2) (no wager; ransom), Regal at Camelot on Lady's Day
- October: Neighborhood (2), Local (2) (Hallowmas at Hantonne, Hampshire), Regional (Carmarthen, Estregales [armor+horse])

**If the player does nothing:** Sir Patrise is poisoned and Guenever is accused of murder regardless; Lancelot returns in disguise at the last moment to serve as her champion and is unmasked. ## Year 563 — A Great Betrayal / The Burning / Britain at War

**Situation:** The crisis point of the entire campaign. Mordred and Agravaine expose the affair publicly. Lancelot kills Agravaine and most of the Orkney knights. Guenever is condemned to burn. Lancelot rescues her and flees to Joyous Garde. The siege begins.

**Court:** Carlisle.

**Away From Camelot:** No one significant. All Orkneys present at Arthur's court.

**Events:**

### A Great Betrayal

**Source:** Malory XX, 1–10

**Scene:** Late evening at court; drinks long, talk hasty. Agravaine and Mordred speak loudly enough to be overheard.

**"Someone must speak the truth,"** says Mordred. A lull falls. Even the king listens.

**"I will,"** says Agravaine. "Your wife, the queen, is having an affair with Sir Lancelot."

**Silence thick enough to hit with a hammer.** The king looks cold and strange. "You will say no more of this. Come into my chamber."

**Awareness:** Success = the Orkney clan are unusually sober tonight.

The moment the chamber door closes, the hall erupts. The king, two Orkney knights, and possibly player knights depart.

**Player Hook:**
- Allies of the Orkneys: recruited to help; must swear on Honor to stand and fight Lancelot or forfeit Honor
- Allies of de Ganis: join Sir Bors, who has taken charge; pairs sent to watch and report; larger group armed and waiting

### Gawaine vs. Lancelot — First Duel

**Setup:** Finally, one late summer day, Gawaine finds the right words — accuses Lancelot of treason and dishonor. Lancelot shouts from the walls, explaining to Arthur why he must come out.

**The Fight:**
- Joust: equal
- Foot duel: long and fierce; through the morning, equal
- Noon: Gawaine's unnatural strength (magical enhancement) begins to diminish
- Lancelot seizes initiative; gives Gawaine such a buffet it cuts through his helm; knocks him flat

**"Kill me,"** says Gawaine. "Finish this now, or I will get healed and come back and kill you!"

**"I will never strike a felled knight,"** says Lancelot. Re-enters the castle unmolested.

**Three weeks later:** Gawaine is back at the gate. "Where's that traitor?"

### Gawaine vs. Lancelot — Second Duel

- Same pattern; Gawaine's strength ebbs again at noon
- **Lancelot:** "You're a strong man and have done many great deeds, but now it's time for me to do mine." 
- Wham! On the helmet, right on the old wound. Gawaine goes down.
- Again Gawaine demands death; Lancelot spares him.
- Siege continues.

**Player Hook (if outside):** Pillaging to feed the army; periodic assaults on Lancelot's other castles.

**Intrigue in Britain:**
- Life under Mordred the Regent is seemingly normal
- He checks royal treasuries (cannot access them; notes the guardians)
- One day: messenger brings news that the king is dead — false, but Mordred does not investigate closely

**Mordred Seizes Power:**
- Mourning is great; Mordred summons a meeting of barons
- Parliament (loaded with his supporters): he is elected King of Logres
- Archbishop Dewi (Dubricius) crowns him at Carlion

**Mordred Summons Guenever:**
- She appears in mourning for her "dead" husband
- Mordred announces he will marry her so she suffers no diminishment
- **Guenever:** "I am the queen; I must have a wedding fit for a queen. I will go to London to purchase my wedding gown."
- Mordred hesitates, then agrees

**Queen's Rebellion:**
- Guenever goes to the White Tower
- Finds it kept by Castellan Sir Kay
- Tells Kay: "Eight knights loyal to Mordred guard me here. Please, order your guards to take them."
- Kay: "Yes, my lady, shortly." Twenty minutes later, 24 armed men confront Mordred's 8 knights
- Mordred's men invited to depart; they do

**Mordred's Siege of the White Tower:**
- Mordred summons forces; lays siege to the White Tower
- Queen trusts no words from Mordred; stays in the tower
- Troops loyal to Kay and the queen defend; cold winds blow from north

**Archbishop Dewi's Resistance:**
- Dewi visits Mordred; calls the proposed marriage sinful and illegal (marrying the wife of one's father)
- Mordred doesn't care; Dewi threatens excommunication; Mordred threatens assassins
- Dewi excommunicates Mordred; Mordred's men trail him to Somerset; cannot follow into the marshes where Dewi takes refuge in a hermitage

**Brian Raids Again:**
- Brian's army lands in Cheshire; brushes away Gomeret army
- Raids through Cheshire, Cameliard, and Lambor; returns to ships with great plunder

**Christmas Court (in France):**
- Arthur grieves in Berwick (he is in France throughout)
- Gawaine lies wounded, maybe dying
- In Benwick, Lancelot broods
- In London, Guenever checks guards and supplies daily; will never surrender to Mordred

**Winter Phase (564):** Cheshire, Cameliard, Lombor: pillaged (+2d6).

**If the player does nothing:** The siege of Benwick continues; Mordred seizes the throne; Guenever barricades herself in the White Tower and holds out regardless. Absent knights from Arthur's army gain no Glory from the duels before Benwick; absent knights in Britain gain no Glory from defending the White Tower and cannot aid Guenever in expelling Mordred's guard.

## Year 565 — Camlann

**Situation:** The end of everything. Arthur returns to Britain. Gawaine dies of his wounds at Dover. The battle of Barham Downs is inconclusive. Arthur returns to Camelot. The final meeting with Mordred ends with Camlann. Arthur goes to Avalon. The age of romance ends.

**Court:** Aquitaine (France), then Camelot, then the field.

**Events:**

**Brian Attacks Lindsey:** In early spring; significant damage to an Arthur ally; castles hold.

### Landing at Dover

**Fleet sails early:** northwest, then along Brittany coast, crossing to Dover.

**Opposition on the Beach:**
- Armed men gather as fleet approaches
- Ships in harbor and on beach; foot soldiers wade ashore
- This is a skirmish: 2 lightly armored footmen OR 1 average knight per player knight
- Whatever the result, the army comes ashore

### Death of Gawaine

**After the landing:** Among the dead and wounded, Gawaine is found — bleeding, unconscious. Old wounds from Lancelot reopened.

**He is dying and knows it.** He laments his foolishness: "If Lancelot were here, this would not have happened. This is my own fault for driving him away."

**Gawaine's Letter (dictated to a scribe):**
- Addressed to Sir Lancelot
- Full of dole and apologies
- Beseeches Lancelot to come quickly to help King Arthur
- Asks him to visit his grave when he returns to Britain

**Gawaine is buried at Dover Castle chapel.**

### Battle of Barham Downs

Mordred, learning Arthur has landed, marches with his vanguard from London.

```
Length: 2 rounds
Battle Size: Small
Commanders: King Arthur (Battle 26) vs. King Mordred (Battle 21)
Modifiers: None
Round 1: First Charge. Normal.
Round 2: Normal.
Victor: Indecisive.
Plunder: None.
Glory: 15 per round.
```

**Aftermath:** Mordred withdraws quickly to Canterbury. Arthur pursues; realizes Saxon peasantry is rising against his scouts. Marches to Rochester; south through Perdue Forest to Sussex; west to Camelot.

### Back to Camelot

**The Return:** The city appears dim and dark at first — shutters closed, empty streets. Then a child peeks out:

**"Mommy, it's the old king!"**

A woman appears and starts to slam the door — then sees:

**"It's King Arthur! It's the real king! It's the Pendragon!"**

Shutters open; people pour out with flowers, fruit, wine, bandages; cheering, holding babies up high to see.

**The Welcome:** The kitchen is stoked; servants squeal with delight; bards shake dust from instruments. Knights and lords from the outskirts ride in; pledging loyalty and fealty. Many dissatisfied with Mordred.

**Player Hook:** Arthur needs every knight. Get every living player character and family knight — bastard sons of knightly age, old grandfathers (tie them to the saddle).

**Note:** Knights with Lancelot are still in France. Knights with Mordred are with him at London.

**Mordred's Forces:**
- Northern and barbarian kings answering his call: Lothian, Norgales, Malahaut, Estregales
- In Camelot: Mordred warms himself in front of fire stoked by shattered Round Table pieces

### Battle of Camlann

**Source:** Malory XXI, 4

**Pre-battle:** Arthur dreams Sir Gawaine appears, surrounded by souls of those he aided alive. He tells Arthur to delay battle 30 days — Lancelot is coming and will ensure victory. Arthur sends Constantine, Bedivere, and Lucan to negotiate.

**The Peace:** Both parties agree to meet in the field with 14 witnesses each to ratify peace. Both commanders privately tell their men: "If you see a sword, attack!"

**The Serpent:** An adder strikes a good knight. Without thinking, he strikes back with his sword and slays it.

**Trumpets blast. "CHARGE!"**

```
Length: Special (as many rounds as needed; up to 12 per day until battle is done)
Battle Size: Huge
Commanders: King Arthur (Battle 26) vs. King Mordred (Battle 21)
Modifiers: Arthur is outnumbered: –5
Glory: 60 per round
```

### Camlann — The Final Rounds

**GM Direction:** The purpose of this battle is to end the campaign by killing all but one player character. "Let's see who collects the most Glory for this battle." (Remember: 1,000 Glory for dying a glorious death.)

**Start slow:** Earliest fights take longest; they thin out as the battle progresses.

**Passion rolls:** Remind all players to try passion rolls for inspiration. Shame to die without using every trick.

**Midpoint Relief — Sir Percivale:** When battle goes badly for Arthur, an army rushes in — led by a great knight in white. It is Sir Percivale with his Grail Knights! They clear the field around the king; the holy force heals the king and perhaps many others nearby.

**Later Relief — Sir Kay:** Another relief army appears to the rear: Sir Kay with the White Tower garrison and others. The brothers Kay and Arthur embrace briefly and push back into the fray.

**Even Later — Sir Ywaine:** From the wilds comes young Sir Ywaine with his great lion and his flock of raven warriors.

### The End

**When only one player character remains:** It is close to sunset.

The last knight of Britain sees King Arthur, wounded and out of breath, covered with enemies' gore. Mordred is visible across the field, leaning on a spear.

The two kings attack each other. Mordred impales Arthur through the chest. The king grimly pushes himself up the shaft and strikes Mordred, shattering his skull. Both fall.

**"Damn you, Mordred. It was an evil hour that I knighted you."**

**The king speaks to the last knight:**

**"This sword — take it and cast it into yonder pool, and then come back and tell me what you saw."**

The knight may or may not do this. If he has not thrown it and the king asks "What did you see?":
- **Truthful roll:** Success = he tells the truth (he saw nothing unusual)
- **"Throw it into the pool, man! I grow cold! They need this to open the way to Avalon!"**

**When the sword is thrown:** A hand in white samite reaches up from the water, catches it by the hilt, waves it three times in a circle, and disappears underwater.

**The Barge:** At the water's edge appears a black barge; four ladies in black. When they see the king they shriek and weep. The king commands: "Put me in the barge." The women crowd about him; one takes his head on her lap.

**"Ah, my brother, you have tarried."** She makes mystical gestures; the barge moves slowly off toward the setting sun.

**Canon last words of the last knight:**
"Ah, my Lord Arthur! What shall become of me now that you are gone and I am here, among my enemies?"

**Arthur:** "Comfort yourself, and do as well as you can. Put no trust in me, for there is no trust left in me to be trusted. I will be taken to Avalon to be healed, and if you hear no more of me, pray for my soul."

**If the player does nothing:** Camlann is fought and Arthur mortally wounds Mordred while being impaled regardless; Arthur is borne away to Avalon on the black barge. Absent knights gain no Glory from the battle rounds and cannot be the last knight to throw Excalibur into the pool — that role, and 50 Glory, falls to whoever Arthur finds at his side.

## Year 566 — The End

**Court:** Arthur's court is no more.

**Away From Camelot:** Virtually all are dead.

**Events:**

**Christmas is not celebrated.** The romance fades. History asserts itself.

**Lancelot Returns (too late):**
A messenger arrives in Benwick bearing Gawaine's letter. Lancelot weeps. He says: "I'll always be unhappy that I killed Gareth and Gaheris, and my good friend Sir Gawaine."

Bors: "Stop complaining. Take revenge. Visit Sir Gawaine's tomb, then avenge King Arthur and Queen Guenever."

Lancelot summons all ships in Ganis.

**Lancelot and Guenever (final parting):**
- Lancelot makes one last tragic visit to Guenever
- In despair, he retires to a hermitage at Glastonbury
- Guenever, in her abbey, dies years later
- Lancelot dies years later in his hermitage
- Both go directly to Heaven amidst the odor of great sanctity

**Wrapping Up:**
- Sons of Mordred have taken Hampshire and Salisbury
- Under Sir Bors, Lancelot's army destroys them
- A smaller force helps Breunor le Noir (La Cote Male Taile) avenge his family; they kill King Mark
- Sir Bors summons lords, clerics, and legalists to Camelot
- **Sir Constantine of Cornwall is named King of Britain** (by Bors's encouragement)

**Malory's Ending:** Bors and the last Round Table knights go to the Holy Land and die fighting Saracens.

**Alternative Ending:** They returned to Ganis and died as old men, surrounded by grandchildren.

**If the player does nothing:** Lancelot arrives too late; Mordred's sons are defeated by Bors; Constantine is named King of Britain regardless. Absent knights gain no Glory from avenging Arthur and cannot be among the last Round Table knights who sail to the Holy Land or retire to Ganis.
