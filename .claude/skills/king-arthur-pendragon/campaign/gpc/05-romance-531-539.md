# GPC Chapter 7: Romance Period (531–539)

**Situation:** Arthur's kingdom is at its apex of chivalry and romance. The Pax Arthur holds — Britain is unified, prosperous, and at peace. The Round Table is full. This period introduces tournaments, Courts of Love, and the mechanics of Romance as formalized institutions, along with the French campaigns and domestic intrigues.

**Key Mechanics Introduced This Period:**
- Romance/Amorous Adventures (Ten Steps to Love)
- Court of Love (glory awards by court size)
- Tournament types: Neighborhood / Local / Regional / Regal
- Wager types: for love / gear / gear+steed / ransom
- Market tables (Tables 7-1 through 7-7)
- Troop types (Romance Period version)

**Difficulty Levels:**

## Canon of Romance (31 Rules)

The Canon governs chivalric love affairs. Key rules for AI GM:
1. Marriage is no excuse for refusing to love.
2. He who is not jealous cannot love.
3. No one can be bound by a double love.
4. It is well known that love is always increasing or decreasing.
5. That which a lover takes against the will of his beloved has no relish.
6. Boys do not love until they arrive at the age of maturity.
7. When one lover dies, a widowhood of two years is required of the survivor.
8. No one should be deprived of love without the very best of reasons.
9. No one can love unless he is impelled by the persuasion of love.
10. Love is always a stranger in the home of avarice.
11. It is not proper to love any woman whom one would be ashamed to seek to marry.
12. A true lover does not desire to embrace in love anyone except his beloved.
13. When made public, love rarely endures.
14. The easy attainment of love makes it of little value; difficulty of attainment makes it prized.
15. Every lover regularly turns pale in the presence of his beloved.
16. When a lover suddenly catches sight of his beloved his heart palpitates.
17. A new love puts to flight an old one.
18. Good character alone makes any man worthy of love.
19. If love diminishes, it quickly fails and rarely revives.
20. A man in love is always apprehensive.
21. Real jealousy always increases the feeling of love.
22. Jealousy, and therefore love, are increased when one suspects his beloved.
23. He whom the thought of love vexes eats and sleeps very little.
24. Every act of a lover ends in the thought of his beloved.
25. A true lover considers nothing good except what he thinks will please his beloved.
26. Love can deny nothing to love.
27. A lover can never have enough of the solaces of his beloved.
28. A slight presumption causes a lover to suspect his beloved.
29. A man who is vexed by too much passion usually does not love.
30. A true lover is constantly and without intermission possessed by the thought of his beloved.
31. Nothing forbids one woman being loved by two men or one man by two women.

## Amorous Adventures: Ten Steps to Love

A structured system for romantic subplots. Each step is a scene requiring a skill roll.

| Step | Name | Key Roll | Success | Failure |
|------|------|----------|---------|---------|
| 1 | First Meeting | Awareness/Recognize | See her | Miss chance |
| 2 | Admiration | Compose/Sing | She notices | Embarrassment |
| 3 | Declaration | Romance or Courtesy | She listens | Rebuffed |
| 4 | Token Exchange | Heraldry/Intrigue | Token accepted | Refused |
| 5 | Assignment | Intrigue | Private meeting arranged | Chaperoned |
| 6 | Private Meeting | Romance | Conversation succeeds | Awkward silence |
| 7 | First Kiss | Romance vs. Chaste | Kiss | She pulls back |
| 8 | Secret Correspondence | Compose | Letters exchanged | Intercepted |
| 9 | Devotion Proven | Deed (quest/joust) | Passion increases | Nothing |
| 10 | Erotic Engagement | Romance vs. Chaste | Amor established | Friendship only |

**After Step 10:** Roll Amor passion (starting at 3d6). Each subsequent year, consult the passion rules (Pendragon core) for effects on skills and aging.

## Year 531 — Camille's Rebellion

**Situation:** The kingdom of Camille in the north has rebelled. A French noblewoman of Cymric territory holds her castle against Arthur's authority, aided by fairy warriors.

**Court:** Pentecost at Camelot. Arthur announces a campaign to settle Camille's rebellion and reinforce his authority in the north.

**Events:**
- Camille, daughter of a slain northern lord, refuses to pay fealty to Arthur
- She has allied with fairy/supernatural guardians (Flower Guardians) to defend her castle
- Knights of the Round Table are dispatched north
- Multiple castles in the region must be subdued

**Adventures:**

### CAMILLE'S REBELLION

**Source:** GPC original.
**Time:** 531
**Hook:** Player knights are ordered north to subdue Camille's rebellion. They must besiege her castle and deal with supernatural defenders.

**Scenes:**

3. **Flower Guardians** — Animate plant-creatures defend the walls; must be fought

5. **Storm or Await** — Players decide whether to storm or wait her out

## Year 532 — Logrin the Giant / Outlaws in Gwynned

**Situation:** A giant named Logrin haunts the Welsh Marches. Separately, a band of outlaws in Gwynned requires attention, and a young boy named Percivale is encountered for the first time.

**Court:** Pentecost at Camelot. Logrin's raids on the Marches are reported.

**Events:**
- Logrin the Giant raiding farms in the Welsh Marches
- Outlaws/bandits operating in Gwynned territory
- First appearance of young Percivale (wild youth, son of Pellinore)
- Merlin visits court briefly

**Adventures:**

### LOGRIN THE GIANT

**Source:** GPC original (recurring adventure 532, 533, 534)
**Hook:** Farmers and villages of the Marches report attacks by a single enormous giant. He is hungry and dimwitted but dangerous.

**Key NPC:**

#### Logrin the Giant
```
Glory Won 100 (first defeat) / 50 (subsequent) / 250 (if slain)
SIZ 28, DEX 10, STR 30, CON 20, APP 4
Move 6; Armor 8 (thick hide)
Unconscious 12; Major Wound 20; Knockdown 28; Hit Points 48; Healing Rate 5; Damage 10d6
Combat Skills: Club (tree trunk) 14, Throw Rock 12 (3d6 at range up to 50 yards)
Traits: Energetic 15, Lazy 12, Reckless 18
Note: Logrin is dimwitted (INT-equivalent 5). He can be tricked, bargained with (offer of food), or driven off. He is NOT inherently evil — just hungry. Killing him earns more Glory but wastes a potential ally.
```

**Scenes:**
1. **Find the Giant** — Track to his cave (Hunting/Tracking vs. 15); cave is in rocky hills
2. **Initial Encounter** — Logrin tries to grab and eat a horse first (horse Horsemanship check DV 15 or thrown)
3. **Combat or Parley** — If offered an ox or two sheep he will negotiate; if not, fight
4. **Recurring** — Logrin returns in 533 and 534 if driven off, not slain

**Outcomes:**
- Drive off: 100 Glory
- Bargain (offer food/tribute arrangement): 50 Glory + possible ally in future
- Slay: 250 Glory

### OUTLAWS IN GWYNNED / PERCIVALE (532–535)

**Source:** GPC original (extended arc)
**Time:** 532–535 (multi-year)
**Hook:** Player knights are tasked with clearing outlaws from the forests of Gwynned. Along the way they encounter a wild youth raised in isolation.

**Scenes (Year 532):**
1. **Into Gwynned** — Borderlands of Wales; forests are thick and poorly mapped
2. **First Outlaw Contact** — Ambush on road; outlaws are desperate not evil
3. **Young Percivale** — A wild boy in the forest, clearly noble-born but ignorant of chivalry
   - No armor, carries javelins, asks "what are those metal men?"
   - Knights must decide: take him to court? teach him? leave him?

**Percivale (Youth)**
- SIZ 10, DEX 18, STR 12, CON 15
- Javelin 17 (unskilled with any knightly weapon)
- Passions: Curiosity (knights) 20
- If taken to court: Lancelot is instructed to train him

**Scenes (Years 533–535):** Percivale grows; outlaws gradually suppressed. Final year: outlaw leader surrenders or is slain in single combat.

**Outcomes:**
- Clear outlaws: 50 Glory per year of effort
- Mentor Percivale: 25 Glory per year + future canonical ally
- Percivale joins Round Table (eventually): player knight who mentored him gains 100 bonus Glory

**If Players Do Nothing:** Outlaws continue; Percivale is found by Sir Gornemant and trained without player involvement.

## Year 536 — Battle of Paris

**Situation:** The climactic battle of the French campaign. Arthur defeats King Childebert of Paris in a decisive engagement.

**Court:** No Pentecost court — army is in France.

**Events:**
- Siege of Paris
- Decisive battle outside the city walls
- King Childebert surrenders
- Arthur accepts French fealty

### Battle of Paris

```
Length: 8 rounds
Battle Size: Large
Commanders: King Arthur (Battle 23) vs. King Childebert (Battle 10)
Victor: King Arthur, Decisive Victory
Plunder: 1 Andalusian charger, 1 charger, 3 rouncys, £7 in goods per knight
Glory: 45 per round ×2 for Decisive Victory = 90/round
```

**Special Events:**
- Round 4: Lancelot's charge breaks Childebert's center (famous deed; witness earns 25 Glory)

**Aftermath:** Arthur is declared Overlord of France. French tribute begins flowing to Camelot. Plunder distributed.

**If Players Do Nothing:** Battle still won; players simply miss Glory.

## Year 538 — Guenever's Kidnapping

**Situation:** Queen Guenever is kidnapped by a treacherous knight while on progress. Meleagant (also called Meliagrant or Melwas) takes her to his castle. This triggers the most important rescue adventure of the Romance Period.

**Court:** Pentecost at Camelot. The kidnapping occurs during the queen's summer progress.

**Events:**
- Queen Guenever departs Camelot for summer progress in Garloth
- Meleagant ambushes her escort; kills several knights
- She is taken to his castle (Badegamus / Gore region)
- Lancelot pursues (arriving at the Castle of Gore after great hardship)
- The infamous Cart Ride (Lancelot crosses in a cart — great shame/glory dichotomy)

**Adventures:**

### GUENEVER'S KIDNAPPING (ATTACK ON THE QUEEN)

**Hook:** Player knights may be part of the queen's escort and witness the ambush, or they are at Camelot when the survivors ride in with news.

**Scene 1: The Ambush**
- Meleagant and 20 knights attack
- Player knights may fight rearguard action; each round they hold delays the capture and earns Glory
- If they hold 3+ rounds: 25 Glory each

**Scene 2: SEARCHING**
- Race to find where Guenever was taken
- Awareness vs. 15: find tracks heading east toward the river
- Dwarf/cart encounters on the road (see canonical story)
- The Sword Bridge and the Water Bridge guard the castle

**Scene 3: LIBERATION**
- Meleagant's Castle (DV 20 concentric)
- Lancelot fights Meleagant and wins; the queen is freed
- Player knights may help defend during the fight or guard against escape

**Scene 4: LADIES' RESCUE**
- After the queen's rescue, a group of imprisoned ladies must also be freed from the castle dungeons
- 20 ladies from various kingdoms, some held for years
- Freeing all: 50 Glory; freeing some: 25 Glory

## Romance Period Appendix: The Wastelands

### What Is the Wasteland?
The Wasteland is a spreading magical blight caused by Sir Balin's Dolorous Stroke (514 AD). It began in Listeneisse (the Fisher King's realm) and slowly expands outward. By 540 it affects a substantial portion of Britain.

### Wasteland Spread Mechanics

Each year the Gamemaster rolls for Wasteland expansion using the **Starting Bad Weather** system. Counties in or near the Wasteland add modifiers:

| Proximity | Starting Bad Weather Modifier |
|-----------|------------------------------|
| Adjacent to Wasteland | +1d6 |
| In Wasteland (partly) | +2d6 |
| In Wasteland (fully) | +3d6 |

### Wasteland Events Table (d20)

Roll once per affected county per season:

| d20 | Event |
|-----|-------|
| 1–5 | No unusual event |
| 6–7 | Water sources dry up; livestock suffer |
| 8–9 | Crops fail; famine threatens |
| 10–11 | Strange beasts emerge from the dead forest |
| 12–13 | Peasants flee to neighboring county |
| 14–15 | Local lord's income halved this year |
| 16–17 | Road becomes impassable; travel takes twice as long |
| 18–19 | Desperate bandits attack holdings |
| 20 | The Fisher King's knights appear; ominous portent |

### Wasteland Encounters Table (2d20)

Roll on entering Wasteland territory:

| 2d20 | Encounter |
|------|-----------|
| 2–4 | Dying peasants on road; need healing (First Aid/Chirurgery) |
| 5–7 | Starving animals (wolves, wild pigs) attack |
| 8–10 | Ruined village; can forage 1d6 days' supplies |
| 11–13 | Knight errant, half-mad from the desolation |
| 14–16 | Grail Castle procession seen in distance (cannot be approached) |
| 17–19 | Enchanted knight guards a ford (fight or riddle) |
| 20–22 | Black-robed hermit with prophecy |
| 23–25 | Spring that gives visions of the Grail |
| 26–28 | Lamenting woman on white horse (leads to adventure) |
| 29–31 | Unseelie creatures: redcaps, goblins, or worse |
| 32–34 | The Fisher King's barge on a dry river |
| 35–37 | Dead forest suddenly in full blossom (lasts one day) |
| 38–40 | The Castle of the Grail (cannot enter; must find the right question) |

### Yellow Pestilence Rules

The Yellow Pestilence spreads during Wasteland years. Roll per holding:

| 1d6 | Effect | Starting Bad Weather Modifier |
|-----|--------|-------------------------------|
| 1–4 | No plague | +0 |
| 5 | Some sickness (–£1 income) | +5 |
| 6 | Pestilence (–£2d6 income; 1 in 6 serfs die) | +10 |

**Note:** The +5/+10 are added to the current year's Starting Bad Weather roll, making future Wasteland events more likely.
